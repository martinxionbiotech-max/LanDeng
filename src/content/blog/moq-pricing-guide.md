**Direct answer:** In business-to-business incense supply, there is no honest price list — pricing is **quotation-based**, prepared per inquiry, because unit cost is a function of several variables that change with every order: **product and material, quantity (MOQ), customization, packaging, and shipping terms**. The useful question is never "what is your price" but "what changes your price."

**Key facts:** This guide explains the quotation model, why MOQ exists, and the variables that move a quote up or down. It publishes no numbers — no MOQs, no prices, no margins — because those are, by definition, confirmed "on request" per inquiry. Fabricating them would mislead you; stating them honestly as "on request" is the point.

## Key Takeaways

- Incense B2B pricing is **quotation-based**, not catalog-based — a "price" without quantity and specification is a guess.
- **MOQ exists because setup cost is fixed** — the minimum order is where unit cost becomes workable, not an arbitrary hurdle.
- The variables that move a quote: **material, quantity, customization, packaging, shipping terms.**
- **"On request" is not evasion** — it is the honest answer when the number genuinely depends on your inputs.
- Ask for the **cost drivers**, not just the bottom line.

## Why There Is No Price List

A retail shop can print a price list because the product is fixed and the buyer takes one unit. B2B is the opposite: the product is often made *for* the order, and the order size varies by orders of magnitude. The same stick can cost very different amounts per unit at 5,000 versus 50,000 units, in a plain box versus custom retail packaging, shipped to one port versus another. A single printed price would be wrong for almost every real inquiry.

This is why every serious supplier — LanDeng included — responds to "how much?" with questions back: *what product, what quantity, what packaging, where to?* That is the quotation process working, not a stall.

---

## Why MOQ Exists

Minimum order quantity is not an arbitrary gate. It exists because **production setup has a fixed cost** — materials are bought, the line is configured, and packaging is prepared — regardless of whether you order a little or a lot. Below a certain quantity, that fixed cost cannot be spread enough to make the order viable.

What sets MOQ:

- **Product type** — an existing product has a lower barrier than a custom one.
- **Customization** — a custom formula or packaging raises the setup cost and therefore the MOQ.
- **Material** — a scarce botanical (real sandalwood, agarwood) may carry a higher practical minimum.

The exact number is always **per product and per project** — which is why it is stated on request, not published.

## The Variables That Move a Quote

| Variable | How it moves the quote |
|---|---|
| Material | Real wood/resin costs far more than fragrance oil; grade matters |
| Quantity | The largest driver — unit cost falls as fixed setup is spread |
| Customization | Custom formula, label, or box adds setup cost and lead time |
| Packaging | Plain bulk vs retail-ready packaging changes cost and time |
| Shipping terms | Incoterms shift who pays freight, insurance, and duties |

Ask your supplier to explain which of these dominates *your* quote. Understanding the cost drivers matters more than memorizing any number.

## How to Ask for a Quote That Actually Helps You

A vague inquiry gets a vague answer. A precise inquiry gets a real one. When you request a quote, provide:

1. **Product** — format, material, aroma profile, or a reference product.
2. **Quantity** — a target, even a range.
3. **Packaging** — plain bulk or retail-ready, and your label if any.
4. **Destination** — country and port, which drives shipping and duties.
5. **Any specification** — natural vs fragrance oil, burn time, certifications.

## FAQ

### Q: Why don't incense suppliers publish a price list?
Because B2B incense is usually made *for* the order, and the unit cost depends on variables that change every time — quantity, material, customization, packaging, and shipping terms. A printed price would be wrong for nearly every real inquiry. The quotation model is the honest response: the supplier asks for your product, quantity, packaging, and destination, then prepares a number that reflects those inputs. A supplier who quotes a price without asking any of those questions is guessing.

### Q: What is a typical MOQ for incense?
There is no single typical MOQ, because the minimum depends on the product, whether it is custom, the material, and the packaging. An existing product in plain bulk has a lower practical minimum than a custom formula in retail packaging, and a scarce botanical may carry a higher one. This is why MOQ is stated per product and per project, on request, rather than published. The useful question is "what is the MOQ for this specific product at this quantity," not "what is your MOQ."

### Q: What factors affect incense wholesale pricing?
Five factors dominate. Material — real wood and resin cost far more than fragrance oil, and grade matters. Quantity — the largest single driver, since larger orders spread fixed setup cost further. Customization — a custom formula, label, or box adds setup cost and lead time. Packaging — retail-ready packaging costs more and takes longer than plain bulk. Shipping terms — Incoterms determine who pays freight, insurance, and duties. Understanding which of these dominates your quote is more useful than any memorized figure.

### Q: How do I get an accurate incense quote?
Give the supplier a complete brief: the product (format, material, aroma, or a reference product), your target quantity or a range, the packaging (plain bulk or retail-ready, and your label if any), the destination country and port, and any specification such as natural versus fragrance oil or required certifications. A precise brief produces a precise quote; a vague one produces a guess. The [quote request](/request-a-quote/) page lists the full set of fields.

### Q: Is "price on request" a red flag?
No — in B2B incense it is the norm, not an evasion. Because the cost genuinely depends on your inputs, a supplier cannot honestly name a price without them, and a supplier who does is likely quoting a placeholder. What would be a red flag is a supplier who refuses to explain *which* variables drive the cost after you have given a real brief. Honesty here looks like "on request," followed by a real quote once you provide the specification.
