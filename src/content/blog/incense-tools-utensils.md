# 香具 (The Incense Tool Set): Chopsticks, Spoons, and the 炉瓶三事

**Direct answer:** 香具 are the **small tools** of the incense practice — chopsticks, spoons, spatulas, and brushes — used to handle charcoal, ash, and material in the 隔火焚香 method. The classic arrangement is the **炉瓶三事** ("three things at the burner"): the **censer (香炉)**, the **incense box (香盒)**, and the **tool holder (香瓶)**.

**Key facts:** You do not need this tool set to burn a stick or cone — it belongs to the resin/powder heating tradition. It is the "kitchen" of incense: functional, and in the best examples, beautiful.

---

## The Tool Set

| Tool | Chinese | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Chopsticks | 香箸 | Pick up and place charcoal or incense chips |
| Spoon | 香匙 | Transfer powder or resin |
| Spatula | 香铲 | Flatten and shape the ash bed |
| Tongs | 香夹 | Grip hot charcoal |
| Feather brush | 羽扫 | Sweep stray ash off the rim |
| Ash tamper | 灰押 / 香押 | Pack and level the ash |

These are used together in the [隔火焚香 setup](/blog/gehuo-fenxiang-setup/) — chopsticks for the coal, spatula and tamper for the ash, spoon for the material.

---

## The 炉瓶三事

The "three things at the burner" is the canonical grouping:

| Item | Chinese | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Censer | 香炉 | The burner itself |
| Incense box | 香盒 | Stores the incense material |
| Tool holder | 香瓶 | Holds the chopsticks, spoon, and spatula (often a small vase) |

The 炉瓶三事 is a **composition** — the three objects together on a stand form a small still life, which is itself part of the aesthetic. The tools are not hidden away; they are displayed.

---

## What You Actually Need

- **Sticks, cones, coils** → a holder and an ash catcher. No tools required.
- **Resin/powder (隔火焚香)** → the full set above, or a modern electric heater that replaces most of it.

Do not buy a full 香具 set as a beginner with sticks. Buy it when (and if) you move to resin and powder heating — see [incense powder & resin](/blog/incense-powder-resin/).

## The Author's Take

**Position:** The 炉瓶三事 tells you more about the tradition than any single tool — because it shows that incense was a *composed* practice, with its tools treated as objects worth displaying, not just using.

**Reasoning:**
- Grouping censer, box, and tool holder together is an aesthetic choice, not a functional requirement.
- Beginners should skip the full set; the tools only earn their place once you practice 隔火焚香.

*This is the author's editorial view — not a verified fact.*

## FAQ

### Q: What tools do I need for incense?
For sticks and cones, just a holder. For resin/powder (隔火焚香), a set of chopsticks, spoon, spatula, and ash tools.

### Q: What is the 炉瓶三事?
The "three things at the burner" — the censer (香炉), incense box (香盒), and tool holder (香瓶).

### Q: What is a 香箸?
Incense chopsticks, used to handle charcoal and chips in the 隔火焚香 method.

### Q: Do I need all these tools as a beginner?
No — start with sticks and a simple holder. The full set is for resin/powder heating.
