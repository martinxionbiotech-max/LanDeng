**Direct answer:** Buying Chinese incense for business means choosing one of three paths — **wholesale** (buying existing products in bulk), **OEM** (a factory makes product to your specification under your brand), or **private label** (a factory's existing product re-branded as yours). Each path has different minimum orders, lead times, and levels of control, and none of them is served by a published price list — every serious quotation is prepared per inquiry.

## Key Takeaways

- The three buying paths are **wholesale**, **OEM**, and **private label** — they differ in control, order size, and lead time, not just price.
- **No honest supplier publishes a fixed price list.** Real quotations are prepared per product, quantity, and destination.
- Your first job as a buyer is **vetting the supplier**, not negotiating price.
- **Samples verify a product, not a relationship** — they answer "what does it smell like," not "will they deliver on time."

## The Three Buying Paths

Every B2B incense purchase fits one of three routes. Choosing wrong is the most expensive early mistake a buyer can make.

| Path | What you get | Control | Typical order size | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wholesale | Existing products, in bulk | Low — you pick from a catalog | Larger, per-inquiry | Retailers, resellers, buyers who want proven product fast |
| OEM | Product made to your formula/spec, under your brand | High — you direct the recipe and packaging | Varies by project | Brands with a specific scent or format in mind |
| Private label | A factory's existing product, re-branded | Medium — you control the label, not the formula | Often lower than OEM | Buyers who want a brand without product development |

See [the wholesale guide](/blog/wholesale-guide/) and [the OEM & private-label guide](/blog/oem-private-label-guide/) for the full treatment of each.

## Which Path Fits Which Buyer

The decision is less about "which is cheapest" and more about **what you already have**.

- **You have a store and want proven product now** → wholesale. You accept someone else's product and sell it under your shop's name.
- **You have a specific scent, format, or recipe in mind** → OEM. You invest time in development and own the formula.
- **You have a brand but no product team** → private label. You take an existing formula and put your name on it.

The common thread: **all three require an inquiry**, because order size, destination, and specification change the price and lead time every time.

## FAQ

### Q: What is the difference between OEM and private label?
OEM means the factory manufactures a product to your specification — your formula, format, and packaging — and it carries your brand. Private label means you take a factory's *existing* product and put your brand on it, without developing a new formula. The practical difference is control and lead time: OEM gives you ownership of the recipe but takes longer and usually costs more to develop; private label is faster and cheaper to launch because the product already exists. Which one fits depends on whether you have a specific scent or format in mind, or just a brand and a market. See [the OEM & private-label guide](/blog/oem-private-label-guide/).

### Q: How do I buy incense wholesale from China?
Start by defining your product (format, ingredients, aroma profile), your target quantity, and your destination, because these three variables drive every quotation. Then vet the supplier — legal identity, production capability, and references — before you ask for a sample. Finally, confirm MOQ, lead time, packaging, and shipping terms per inquiry rather than expecting a published price list. The [wholesale guide](/blog/wholesale-guide/) and [factory vetting guide](/blog/factory-vetting-guide/) walk through each step.

### Q: What is a typical minimum order quantity for incense?
There is no single typical MOQ, because it depends on the product format, whether it is an existing or custom product, and the factory's production setup. This is why LanDeng — like any honest supplier — confirms MOQ per inquiry rather than publishing a fixed number. The useful question is not "what is the MOQ" in the abstract, but "what is the MOQ *for this specific product and this quantity*." See [the MOQ & pricing guide](/blog/moq-pricing-guide/).

### Q: Can I get samples before placing a large order?
Yes — sampling is a standard step, and it is exactly what the sample is for: to verify the aroma, format, and burn before you commit to a bulk order. But be clear-eyed about what a sample proves. It verifies the *product* (how it smells and burns), not the *supplier's* ability to deliver the full order on time and to spec. Treat sampling as one checkpoint in a longer vetting process. See [the samples program](/blog/samples-program/).
