**Direct answer:** Agarwood and sandalwood are the two reference woods of Chinese incense, but they are **different materials formed in opposite ways**. Agarwood (沉香) is resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* heartwood that becomes fragrant only after injury; sandalwood (檀香) is the fragrant heartwood of *Santalum* trees, scented in the living core with no wounding required. Agarwood is deep, resinous, sweet, and evolving; sandalwood is warm, creamy, sweet, and soft-woody. Both carry serious substitution risk — but for different reasons.

**Key facts:** Agarwood is a *condition* of *Aquilaria* wood (Thymelaeaceae); sandalwood is *Santalum* heartwood (Santalaceae). Both are woods, not resins, and both are typically warmed rather than flamed — agarwood to protect its staged aroma, sandalwood to release its slow "cooked" wood note. Neither is "better"; each holds a different role in a blend.

## Key Takeaways

- Agarwood is **resin-saturated heartwood formed by injury**; sandalwood is **fragrant heartwood present in the living tree** — opposite formation stories.
- Agarwood's top grade is **奇楠 (qínán / "kyara")**; sandalwood's reference is **老山檀 (old-mountain Indian *Santalum album*)** — two different "top of category" ideas.
- Agarwood is graded by **resin density (the sinking test)**; sandalwood is graded by **species, heartwood content, and oil content**.
- Both are **high substitution risk**, but agarwood is faked *as wood*, while sandalwood is faked *as species or oil*.
- Both reward **gentle heat** over open flame.
- This page makes **no health or medical claims** for either material.

## What Each Wood Actually Is

Both are called "woods," but they are not the same category:

- **Agarwood (沉香)** is a *condition* of wood. Healthy *Aquilaria* heartwood is pale, light, and nearly scentless; only after the tree is wounded — by insects, weather, or deliberate inoculation — does it saturate the damaged heartwood with resin over years or decades. "Agarwood" names the resin-transformed result, not a species. See the [agarwood entity page](/ingredients/agarwood/).
- **Sandalwood (檀香)** is fragrant *heartwood*. The scent lives in the mature core of *Santalum* trees and is present in the living tree, with no injury required. The tree is **hemiparasitic** — it taps host-plant roots — which is part of why it is slow and costly to establish. See the [sandalwood entity page](/ingredients/sandalwood/).

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood is resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* heartwood — a condition produced by wounding — while sandalwood is the fragrant heartwood of *Santalum* trees, scented in the living core. One is made fragrant by injury; the other is fragrant by nature.

## Botanical Source and Naming

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood comes from *Aquilaria* species (Thymelaeaceae), chiefly *A. sinensis* in southern China and *A. malaccensis* in Southeast Asia. Sandalwood comes from *Santalum* species (Santalaceae), with *S. album* (Indian/white sandalwood) as the reference.

- **Agarwood** — genus *Aquilaria*, family Thymelaeaceae; roughly 15–20 tree species across South and Southeast Asia, chiefly *Aquilaria sinensis* (白木香) and *Aquilaria malaccensis* (the main Southeast Asian commercial species, CITES Appendix II).
- **Sandalwood** — genus *Santalum*, family Santalaceae; hemiparasitic trees. The reference species is *Santalum album*; the main legal commercial substitute is Australian *S. spicatum*.

In Chinese, the names record their nature: 沉香 (chénxiāng) means "sinking fragrance" (the density test), while 檀香 (tánxiāng) means "sandalwood fragrance." One naming trap: 檀香 is easily confused with 紫檀 ("red sandalwood"), a different plant — *Pterocarpus santalinus*, a crimson dye wood. See [red sandalwood](/ingredients/red-sandalwood/).

## Aroma: Deep Resin vs Creamy Wood

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood is woody, resinous, sweet, and complex, and it develops in stages as it warms — top note, body, dry-down. Sandalwood is warm, creamy, sweet, and soft-woody, with a "buttery" smoothness in good material. Agarwood is deep and evolving; sandalwood is gentle and long-lasting.

| | Agarwood (沉香) | Sandalwood (檀香) |
|---|---|---|
| Character | Deep, layered, evolving | Warm, smooth, grounding |
| Notes | Woody, resinous, sweet, complex | Creamy, sweet, soft-woody ("buttery") |
| Behavior on heat | Develops in stages (头香 / 本香 / 尾香) | Slow, deep, long-lasting release |
| Role in a blend | Base note — anchors and deepens | Base note — rounds and grounds |

Because both are base notes, they are **complements rather than substitutes**: sandalwood's smoothness lets brighter notes sit on top, while agarwood adds the deeper, rarer dimension. They frequently sit together as the two wood anchors of a single 合香 formula.

## Heating: Why Both Are Warmed, Not Flamed

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood is warmed over indirect heat (隔火焚香) so its staged aroma unfolds without a charred note; sandalwood is warmed or burned slowly to release its long, deep "cooked" wood aroma.

- **Agarwood** — the refined method rests chips above charcoal buried in ash, releasing scent without combusting; a direct flame burns off the delicate top notes.
- **Sandalwood** — the wood smolders steadily and slowly; its gentle, long-lasting aroma anchors a blend. It *can* be burned as a wood, unlike a resin, but rewards slow heat over a harsh flame.

## Grades: qinan (奇楠) vs old-mountain sandalwood (老山檀)

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood's top grade is 奇楠 (qínán), "oil-wraps-wood" material so resin-saturated it is soft and fragrant at room temperature. Sandalwood's reference is old-mountain sandalwood (老山檀) — aged, oil-rich Indian *S. album* heartwood. Different grading systems, different materials.

The two "top grades" are not parallel:

- **奇楠 (qínán / "kyara")** — the highest agarwood grade, structurally different from ordinary agarwood: "oil wraps wood" (油包木) rather than "wood wraps oil" (木包油). It shaves into curls and kneads into pellets, and is fragrant unheated. See [qínán / kyara](/blog/qinan-kyara/).
- **老山檀 (lǎoshān tán)** — "old-mountain sandalwood," the traditional reference for aged, oil-rich Indian *Santalum album* heartwood. It is a *quality tier within one species*, not a separate material state like 奇楠.

Agarwood is graded primarily by **resin density** (the sinking test); sandalwood by **species, heartwood content, oil content, and age**. See the [agarwood grading guide](/blog/agarwood-grading-guide/) and the [sandalwood entity page](/ingredients/sandalwood/).

## Price Drivers: Why the Gap Is So Wide

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood's price is driven by resin density, the 奇楠 distinction, and scarcity — with 奇楠 at the very top of the market. Sandalwood's price is driven by species (*S. album* vs substitutes), heartwood and oil content, and CITES pressure on wild stock. Directional signals, not a price list.

The two woods occupy different price bands:

- **Agarwood** — ordinary medicinal-grade chips are inexpensive; burnable/gradeable material scales steeply with resin density; 奇楠 reaches the highest prices in the market.
- **Sandalwood** — genuine *S. album* powder is costly, and 老山檀 commands a premium; plantation and Australian *S. spicatum* material are far cheaper, and very cheap "sandalwood" is almost always synthetic fragrance oil.

These are directional market signals, not a price list. The [material comparison matrix](/blog/incense-material-comparison-matrix/) places both against the other classical materials.

## Substitution Risk: Two Different Kinds of Fake

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood is faked *as wood* — dyed white wood, unrelated woods, and oil-injected material. Sandalwood is faked *as species or oil* — other *Santalum* species, and synthetic fragrance oil sold as wood. Both are high-risk, but the deception works differently.

- **Agarwood adulteration** — dyed or oil-soaked white wood, foreign woods dressed up to look resinous, and oil-injected low-grade material engineered to sink in the density test. Screening methods are in the [authentication database](/blog/incense-authentication-database/).
- **Sandalwood substitution** — species substitution (Australian or other *Santalum* for *S. album*), oil-for-wood substitution, and synthetic "sandalwood" fragrance. See the [substitution cross-reference](/blog/incense-substitution-cross-reference/).

## Traditional Use and Context

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood and sandalwood are the two reference woods of Chinese incense — sandalwood the creamy staple, agarwood the deeper, rarer premium wood. Both appear in classical materia medica, but that language is traditional, not clinical.

- **Agarwood** — the premium anchor in 合香 and the material of refined appreciation; recorded in classical materia medica for "moving qi" (traditional language, not clinical evidence).
- **Sandalwood** — a staple base wood used in sticks, blended incense, and temple ritual; recorded in classical materia medica as a standard aromatic wood.

## What Buyers Should Look For

> **Direct answer:** For agarwood, pin down species, origin, density, and authenticity before price. For sandalwood, pin down which *Santalum* species, whether the label means wood or oil, and the origin and harvest basis. Both reward a label that names the species.

- **Agarwood** — which *Aquilaria* species and origin; wild vs cultivated (a legal and price question); aroma on gentle heat (the decisive test); particle size and moisture for powder. See the [agarwood buying checklist](/blog/how-to-buy-agarwood-for-incense-manufacturing/).
- **Sandalwood** — which *Santalum* species; wood versus oil (they smell different); origin and harvest basis; the "buttery" note as the authenticity marker. See the [bulk ingredient sourcing guide](/blog/bulk-incense-ingredient-sourcing/) for the framework.

## The Standard Comparison

| Criterion | Agarwood (沉香) | Sandalwood (檀香) |
|---|---|---|
| Material origin | Resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* heartwood (a condition, forms after injury) | Fragrant *Santalum* heartwood (scent present in the living tree) |
| Botanical source | *Aquilaria* spp., Thymelaeaceae | *Santalum* spp., Santalaceae (hemiparasitic) |
| Aroma | Woody, resinous, sweet, complex; develops in stages | Creamy, sweet, soft-woody, "buttery" |
| Top grade / reference | 奇楠 (qínán) — "oil wraps wood" | 老山檀 — aged Indian *S. album* |
| Grading system | Resin density (sinking test 沉水) + 奇楠 distinction | Species, heartwood content, oil content, age |
| Heating method | Warmed over indirect heat (隔火焚香) | Warmed or burned slowly |
| Typical forms | Chips, powder, whole pieces, oud oil | Chips, powder, oil, whole wood, sticks |
| Substitution risk | **Very high** — dyed wood, foreign woods, oil-injection | **High** — species substitution, oil-for-wood, synthetic fragrance |
| Sourcing considerations | CITES-listed; wild depleted, mostly cultivated plantation supply | Wild *S. album* CITES-listed; plantation *S. album* + Australian *S. spicatum* dominate |

Neither column is "better." The table describes what each wood is and how it behaves — agarwood the deep, scarce, heavily-faked resin wood; sandalwood the creamy, slow, frequently-substituted heartwood.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Assuming the two woods are interchangeable.** They are base notes that coexist in a blend, not substitutes for each other.
2. **Flaming agarwood.** That destroys the staged aroma the material is prized for; it should be warmed.
3. **Conflating 奇楠 with 老山檀.** One is agarwood's top *material state*; the other is a *quality tier* of sandalwood.
4. **Skipping the species check on both.** Agarwood you must verify as wood; sandalwood you must verify as species and as wood-vs-oil.
5. **Conflating sandalwood (檀香) with red sandalwood (紫檀).** Different plants; the shared 檀 character causes persistent confusion.

## FAQ

### Q: Which is more expensive, agarwood or sandalwood?
Agarwood, by a wide margin at the top end. Its top grade 奇楠 (qínán) sits at the very top of the incense market, while ordinary medicinal-grade agarwood is inexpensive — so the spread within "agarwood" is enormous. Genuine *Santalum album* is also costly, but sandalwood's ceiling sits well below 奇楠's, because it has legal plantation and Australian substitutes that cap its price.

### Q: Do agarwood and sandalwood smell the same?
No. Agarwood is deep, woody, resinous, and sweet, and it develops in stages as it warms. Sandalwood is warm, creamy, sweet, and soft-woody, with a "buttery" smoothness in good material. Both are base notes, but agarwood is darker and more complex, while sandalwood is smoother and more soothing.

### Q: What is the difference between qinan (奇楠) and old-mountain sandalwood (老山檀)?
They are different things entirely. 奇楠 (qínán, "kyara") is the top *grade* of agarwood — "oil-wraps-wood" material so resin-saturated it is soft, pliable, and aromatic at room temperature. 老山檀 (lǎoshān tán, "old-mountain sandalwood") is the traditional reference for aged, oil-rich Indian *Santalum album* heartwood. One is a material state within agarwood grading; the other is a quality tier within sandalwood. See [qínán / kyara](/blog/qinan-kyara/) and the [agarwood grading guide](/blog/agarwood-grading-guide/).

### Q: How do you use agarwood vs sandalwood in incense?
Both are warmed rather than flamed. Agarwood is warmed over indirect heat (隔火焚香) — chips above charcoal buried in ash — so its staged aroma unfolds without a charred note. Sandalwood is warmed or burned slowly, releasing a long, deep "cooked" wood aroma; it anchors a blend as a base note.

### Q: Is agarwood or sandalwood more likely to be faked?
Both are high-risk, but in different ways. Agarwood is the most heavily faked aromatic in the category — dyed white wood, unrelated woods, and oil-injected material engineered to sink. Sandalwood is faked as *species* (other *Santalum* for *S. album*), as *oil* (synthetic fragrance sold as wood), and cheap "sandalwood" sticks are almost always fragrance oil. See the [authentication database](/blog/incense-authentication-database/) and the [substitution cross-reference](/blog/incense-substitution-cross-reference/).

### Q: Is sandalwood the same as red sandalwood?
No. Sandalwood (檀香, *Santalum album*) is a creamy, sweet-scented incense wood. Red sandalwood (紫檀, *Pterocarpus santalinus*) is a crimson dye wood valued for color, not aroma. The shared 檀 character causes persistent confusion. See [red sandalwood](/ingredients/red-sandalwood/).
