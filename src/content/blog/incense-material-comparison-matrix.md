# Incense Material Comparison Matrix: 7 Classical Materials at a Glance

**Direct answer:** The seven classical incense materials — **agarwood, sandalwood, frankincense, borneol, benzoin, myrrh, and clove** — differ along five axes: **aroma, botanical source, relative price, use, and substitution risk.** This matrix puts them side by side, because most English content compares only agarwood and sandalwood.

**Key facts:** "Relative price" is a directional signal, not a price list. The most important column for a buyer is **substitution risk** — the chance that the label name hides a different material.

## Reading the Three "Resin" Columns

Agarwood, frankincense, and myrrh are all *resins* (or resin-bearing materials), but they sit at very different points:

- **Agarwood** is resin-saturated *heartwood* — a slow, scarce, expensive process.
- **Frankincense** and **myrrh** are tapped *gum resins* — more abundant and far cheaper.

This is why "resin" is not a price signal by itself: the *which* resin matters enormously.

---

## The Two Substitution Hotspots

1. **Agarwood** — oil-injection fakes and unrelated woods (see the [authentication database](/blog/incense-authentication-database/)).
2. **Sandalwood** — other *Santalum* species and synthetic fragrance sold under the name (see the [sandalwood guide](/blog/sandalwood-incense/) and [substitution cross-reference](/blog/incense-substitution-cross-reference/)).

Borneol deserves a note too: natural borneol (from *Dryobalanops*) and synthetic borneol (from turpentine/camphor) are chemically the *same molecule*, so the "natural" claim is a sourcing and price question, not an aroma one.

## FAQ

### Q: What is the most expensive incense material?
Agarwood — and its top grade 奇楠 (kyara) is the most expensive material in the market, orders of magnitude above the rest.

### Q: What is the difference between frankincense and myrrh?
Both are tapped resins, but frankincense is fresh and citrus-woody while myrrh is bitter and earthy. Both are far cheaper than agarwood.

### Q: Is borneol the same as camphor?
No — they are different compounds, but both have a cool, sharp character. Borneol is the traditional "snow" note in blends like 雪中春信.

### Q: Which material is most likely to be faked?
Agarwood (oil-injection and substituted woods), followed by sandalwood (substitute species and fragrance oil).

### Q: What is the difference between Siam and Sumatra benzoin?
Siam benzoin (from *Styrax tonkinensis*) is considered finer and sweeter; Sumatra benzoin (*S. benzoin*) is more common. Both are real benzoin — the grades differ.
