# Buying 奇楠 (Qinan): What to Check Before You Pay

**Direct answer:** The single most important 奇楠 (qinan) buying rule is **price honesty** — 奇楠 is orders of magnitude more expensive than ordinary agarwood, so anything sold as 奇楠 at a bargain is almost certainly not 奇楠. The supporting checks are the **seller's ability to name species and grade**, and the **aroma on gentle heat**.

**Key facts:** 奇楠 is the most-faked material in the incense world, precisely because its price is so high. This is a buyer-intelligence guide, not a laboratory method.

---

## The Price Reality (Directional)

| Material / grade | Rough price signal (RMB, market-dependent) |
|---|---|
| Ordinary agarwood (medicinal chips) | ~0.4–1.4 RMB/g — a cheap herb |
| Burnable agarwood | tens to thousands of RMB/g |
| 奇楠 (qinan / kyara) | ~3,000–8,000 RMB/g wholesale; top white 奇楠 can exceed 16,000 RMB/g |

The gap between ordinary agarwood and 奇楠 is **not a few percent — it is orders of magnitude**. A "奇楠" bead at a price that matches ordinary agarwood is a contradiction in terms.

---

## The Three Checks, in Order

| Check | What to ask | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Seller names species & grade | "Which *Aquilaria* species? Which grade — 沉水 or 奇楠? Which origin?" | A vendor who cannot answer is already telling you everything |
| 2. Aroma on gentle heat | "What does it smell like warmed, not burned?" | 奇楠 is complex, sweet, layered — even unheated; a flat "perfume" or "burnt wood" smell is a red flag |
| 3. Price vs. grade | "Does the price match the claimed grade?" | Too-cheap-for-奇楠 is the strongest single signal |

---

## Why the Common Tests Fail

- **The sink test (沉水)** — oil-injection fakes are *engineered to pass* it. Density alone proves nothing.
- **Color** — dyed "white wood" can imitate any color grade; the five-color hierarchy is also disputed as distinct grades.
- **"Room-temperature aroma"** — a real 奇楠 signal, but synthetic fragrance can fake a one-note version.

The reliable route is not a single trick — it is a **seller you can hold accountable** and, where the value justifies it, **laboratory testing**.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** My rule is simple and slightly cynical: if you cannot afford real 奇楠, do not buy "affordable 奇楠" — buy good ordinary agarwood and be done with it.

**Reasoning:**
- The price gap is so large that "affordable 奇楠" is a near-contradiction; it is where the fakes concentrate.
- A good 沉水-grade ordinary agarwood gives 90% of the experience at a fraction of the risk.

*This is the author's editorial view and experience — not a verified fact.*

## FAQ

### Q: How do I know if 奇楠 is real?
Ask the seller to name the species, grade, and origin; check the gentle-heat aroma (complex and sweet); and treat too-cheap-for-奇楠 as a red flag.

### Q: Is the sink test reliable for 奇楠?
No — oil-injection fakes are designed to pass it. Combine any density test with aroma and seller checks.

### Q: What should I buy if I can't afford real 奇楠?
Good 沉水-grade ordinary agarwood — most of the experience at a fraction of the risk.

### Q: Why is 奇楠 so expensive?
It is the top resin-saturated grade of agarwood, and genuine material is scarce — the price gap to ordinary agarwood is orders of magnitude.
