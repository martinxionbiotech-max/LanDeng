**Direct answer:** Incense formats differ mainly by **burn time** and **smoke level**: a stick runs roughly 20–60 minutes, a cone 15–30, a coil 1.5–4 hours, and resin/powder is session-based (minimal smoke on an electric heater, more on charcoal). Choose the format to match the session length and how much smoke you want.

**Key facts:** These times are **approximate** — they vary by thickness, material, and maker. The matrix is a planning aid, not a spec sheet.

## The Matrix

| Format | Typical burn time | Smoke level | Best for | Holder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thin / coreless stick | ~20–40 min | Low–moderate | Short sessions, small rooms | Stick holder / tray |
| Standard stick | ~30–60 min | Moderate | Everyday sessions | Stick holder + ash catcher |
| Cone | ~15–30 min | Moderate | Quick sessions, backflow effects | Flat heatproof dish |
| Coil (盘香) | ~1.5–4 hours | Moderate | Long, continuous scent | Coil stand / plate |
| Resin — electric heater | Session-based (hours) | Minimal | Low-smoke, controlled aroma | Electric heater + dish |
| Resin — charcoal | ~15–30 min per charcoal | Higher | Traditional, strong release | Charcoal in sand/ash |
| Powder (香粉) | Depends on method | Varies | Seal patterns (篆香), custom blends | Charcoal or pressed |

## The Author's Take

**Position:** Match the burn to the session first, then choose the scent — the format is the plan, the scent is the flavor.

**Reasoning:**
- People agonize over scent and ignore burn time, then wonder why a 25-minute stick dies halfway through a 45-minute sit.
- The best incense is the one whose burn ends when your session ends — a stick that outlives your meditation by twenty minutes is a mismatch.

*This is the author's editorial view and experience — not a verified fact.*
---

## How to Read the Matrix

1. **Session length first.** A short sit needs a stick or cone; hours of scent needs a coil; controlled, low-smoke appreciation needs resin on an electric heater.
2. **Smoke second.** If smoke matters (small room, sensitivity), choose electric-heated resin or a thin stick. See [ventilation & indoor air](/blog/incense-ventilation-indoor-air/).
3. **Format ≠ scent.** The same sandalwood can be a stick, coil, or resin — the format changes the burn, not the aroma.

---

## The Two Variables That Matter

- **Burn time** — how long the scent lasts in one session.
- **Smoke output** — how much particulate you are adding to the room.

Everything else (holder, cleanup, convenience) follows from these two. Most "which format" confusion is really "which burn time and smoke level do I want."

## FAQ

### Q: How long does an incense stick burn?
A: Roughly 20 to 60 minutes, depending on thickness, material, and how densely the paste is packed. Thin, coreless sticks sit at the short end — around 20 to 40 minutes — while standard and thicker sticks run 30 to 60 minutes. Treat these as planning ranges, not exact specs, because two sticks with the same label can burn at different rates. Match the burn to your session: a 20-minute stick for a short sit, a longer one for a fuller practice, and check the maker's stated time when precision matters.

### Q: What burns the longest?
A: A coil (盘香) burns the longest of the common self-contained formats — often 1.5 to 4 hours, and large coils can run even longer. Heated resin on an electric heater is a different case: it is session-based and can keep releasing aroma for hours with minimal smoke, because you are warming material rather than burning it. Between a single stick (20–60 minutes) and a cone (15–30 minutes), the coil is the clear long-burn choice for continuous scent without relighting.

### Q: Which format produces the least smoke?
A: Resin on an electric heater produces the least smoke, because the heater warms the material below combustion temperature — aroma is released as vapor rather than smoke, with no flame. By contrast, charcoal-heated resin gives off more smoke, and sticks, cones, and coils all burn and produce visible smoke. If you want the cleanest, most controlled option, an electric heater with loose resin or wood chips is the format to choose. A thin, coreless stick is a lighter-smoke alternative if you prefer the convenience of a stick.

### Q: Is a cone or a stick better?
A: It depends on how long you want the burn. A cone lasts roughly 15 to 30 minutes, which suits a quick, self-contained session or a short visual accent. A stick burns 20 to 60 minutes, making it the better everyday default for a longer sit. Neither is inherently better — they simply map to different session lengths. If you want a brief burst, reach for a cone; if you want a half-hour or more of steady scent, a stick is the more natural match.

## More Questions Answered

### Q: Do thicker sticks burn longer?
Usually yes. A thicker stick carries more material, which tends to burn more slowly, so a thicker stick often lasts longer than a thin one. But thickness is not the whole story: the binder ratio and how densely the paste is packed also change the burn rate, and a dense, tightly packed thin stick can outlast a loosely packed thick one. Humidity in storage matters too. That is why every burn time here is stated as a range rather than a spec — treat the number on the label as a guide.

### Q: Can I shorten a burn?
You can snuff a stick or cone out early and relight the remaining portion later, though the relit end may not burn as evenly. For a coil, you can cut or break off a shorter segment of the spiral, which is the most "cuttable" format because the unburned portion stays intact. Another option is simply to choose a shorter format to begin with — a cone instead of a stick, or a small coil instead of a large one — so the burn ends when your session does.

### Q: What is the best format for a beginner?
A standard stick is the easiest entry point. It is self-contained, has a predictable 20-to-60-minute burn, and needs only a simple holder with an ash tray — no special setup or technique. From there you can explore the other formats as your needs emerge: a cone for shorter sessions, a backflow cone for the visual effect, a coil for hours of continuous scent, or loose resin on an electric heater for a low-smoke, pure-material experience. Start simple, then expand once you know what session lengths you actually want.

### Q: Why do burn times vary so much between brands?
Several factors change the burn rate. Thickness matters — a thicker stick carries more material — but so does material density, the ratio of binder to fragrance, how tightly the paste is packed, and even the humidity the incense absorbed during storage. A damp stick burns slower and less cleanly, while a dry, dense one burns longer and more evenly. Because no two makers use exactly the same recipe or conditions, the same "size" of stick can burn noticeably differently. That is why every time here is a range.

### Q: Is there a format that runs all day?
A large coil can run for hours, but no incense should ever burn unattended — even a long coil needs a safe, heatproof setup and periodic checks. For true all-day scent, the realistic choices are a coil placed in a safe spot where you can check it regularly, or an electric incense heater, which warms resin or wood without an open flame and is easier to run safely for extended periods. Whichever you choose, keep it away from drafts, flammables, children, and pets, and ventilate the room.
