**Direct answer:** The woody aroma family is the **dry, warm, clean scent of aromatic wood** — and it is the backbone of classical Chinese incense. Its nine canonical members range from the creamy "buttery" smoothness of sandalwood (檀香) and the deep, layered sweetness of agarwood (沉香) to the clean pencil-box character of cedar (雪松). Woody is a scent character, not a botanical class: it includes a grass root (vetiver) and a leafy herb (patchouli) alongside true woods.

**Key facts:** The woody family has **nine canonical members** in `aroma.json`: agarwood, benzoin, cedar, orange-peel, patchouli, sandalwood, vetiver, borneol, and pine-resin. Most also belong to other families — agarwood is also resinous and sweet, patchouli also earthy and balsamic. The family is defined by how a material *smells and behaves in a blend*, not by whether it is literally wood.

## What "Woody" Means

> **Direct answer:** Woody is the dry, warm, clean scent of aromatic wood — distinct from the sticky depth of resin and the sweet lift of florals. Within the family there is a spectrum: creamy and soft at one end (sandalwood), deep and layered in the middle (agarwood), clean and sharp at the other (cedar).

The woody character sits on a spectrum of three registers:

- **Creamy-soft** — sandalwood's warm, sweet, "buttery" smoothness, the gentle, long-lasting base.
- **Deep-layered** — agarwood's woody, resinous, sweet complexity, which develops in stages as it warms.
- **Clean-sharp** — cedar's dry, slightly sweet-and-resinous note, brighter and more "pencil-box" in the *Juniperus* forms.

Woody is the *foundation* family because its materials are long-lasting bases that let brighter notes — florals, citrus, spices — sit on top without clashing.

## Characteristic Chemistry (as published)

> **Direct answer:** The woody family does not share one molecule. What is published in the entity pages is fragmentary: agarwood's resin chemistry is described as **sesquiterpenes and related compounds**, and borneol (a woody-canonical member) is a **bicyclic monoterpene**. Sandalwood and cedar are described only as having well-characterized essential-oil chemistry, with no named molecule.

The honest picture is that woody is a *sensory* grouping, not a single chemical class:

- **Agarwood** — the entity page notes its resin chemistry is "sesquiterpenes and related compounds," studied but not presented as clinical evidence.
- **Borneol** — a bicyclic monoterpene; a woody-canonical member whose cooling character comes from the TRPM8 cold receptor.
- **Sandalwood, cedar, vetiver, patchouli** — the entity pages describe their essential-oil chemistry as "well characterized" or "studied," without naming a single defining molecule.

Where a specific molecule is not named in the corpus, this page does not invent one.

## Representative Entities

### Agarwood (沉香) — the deep, layered premium wood

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood is resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* heartwood — wood that turns fragrant only after injury. It is woody, resinous, and sweet, develops in stages as it warms, and is the most prized and most heavily faked incense material.

Agarwood is the family's peak: a *condition* of wood, not a species, formed when *Aquilaria* trees are wounded and saturate their heartwood with resin over years. Its woody character is inseparable from its resinous and sweet layers. See the [agarwood entity page](/ingredients/agarwood/).

### Sandalwood (檀香) — the creamy, gentle base

> **Direct answer:** Sandalwood is the fragrant heartwood of *Santalum* trees, warm, creamy, sweet, and soft-woody, with a "buttery" smoothness in good material. It is the family's gentle, long-lasting base note.

Sandalwood's scent is present in the living heartwood — no injury required — which distinguishes it from agarwood. Its smoothness is why it anchors a blend and lets brighter notes sit on top. See the [sandalwood entity page](/ingredients/sandalwood/).

### Cedar (雪松) — the clean, sharp wood

> **Direct answer:** Cedar is a dry, clean wood note — slightly sweet and resinous. True *Cedrus* reads warm and balsamic; the "cedarwood" of trade is often *Juniperus virginiana*, brighter and more pencil-like.

Cedar is the family's clean register, and its main trap is naming: "cedar" covers at least two unrelated families (Pinaceae and Cupressaceae). See the [cedar entity page](/ingredients/cedar/).

### The wider woody set

The remaining canonical members round out the family:

- **Vetiver (岩兰草)** — a grass root that reads woody, earthy, and smoky; a base note. See [vetiver](/ingredients/vetiver/).
- **Patchouli (广藿香)** — a leafy herb that reads woody, earthy, and balsamic. See [patchouli](/ingredients/patchouli/).
- **Borneol (龙脑)** — a cooling, minty-woody crystal, not a wood. See [borneol](/ingredients/borneol/).
- **Pine resin (松香)** — piney, woody, balsamic resin. See [pine resin](/ingredients/pine-resin/).
- **Benzoin (安息香)** — sweet, vanilla, balsamic, woody resin. See [benzoin](/ingredients/benzoin/).
- **Orange peel (陈皮)** — citrus, bittersweet, with a woody aged note. See [orange peel](/ingredients/orange-peel/).

## Why Woody Anchors a Blend

> **Direct answer:** Woody materials are bases — long-lasting, gentle, and grounding — so they anchor a blend and let brighter top notes (florals, citrus, spices) sit on top without clashing. This is why woods are the foundation of classical Chinese incense.

The blend logic follows from the aroma behavior:

- A **sandalwood** or **cedar** base gives a smooth, long-lasting floor.
- A **floral** or **citrus** top note (see the [floral family](/blog/floral-aroma-family/)) lifts that floor.
- A **resinous** note (see the [resinous family](/blog/resinous-aroma-family/)) adds depth in the middle.

The classical pairing — agarwood and sandalwood as the "two reference woods" of Chinese incense — is exactly this: two woody bases with different registers, one deep and layered, one creamy and gentle. See the [material comparison matrix](/blog/incense-material-comparison-matrix/).

## FAQ

### Q: What is the woody aroma family?
The dry, warm, clean scent of aromatic wood — the foundation family of classical Chinese incense. Its nine canonical members are agarwood, benzoin, cedar, orange-peel, patchouli, sandalwood, vetiver, borneol, and pine-resin, listed in [aroma.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/aroma.json).

### Q: Is everything in the woody family actually wood?
No. Woody is a scent character, not a botanical class. Vetiver is a grass root, patchouli a leafy herb, borneol a crystalline monoterpene, and benzoin and pine resin are resins — all read as "woody" but are not wood. The family describes how a material smells, not what plant part it is.

### Q: What are the representative woody materials in Chinese incense?
Agarwood (沉香), sandalwood (檀香), and cedar (雪松). Agarwood is the deep, layered premium wood; sandalwood the creamy, gentle base; cedar the clean, sharp wood. The three are the family's flagship registers.

### Q: How does woody differ from earthy?
Woody is the dry, warm, clean wood register; earthy is the heavier, darker root-and-soil register. They overlap — vetiver reads as both woody and earthy — but woody leans clean and warm, earthy leans rooted and grounded. See the [aroma families hub](/blog/incense-aroma-families/).

### Q: What molecule defines the woody family?
There is no single defining molecule. The entity pages describe agarwood's resin as sesquiterpenes and related compounds, and borneol as a bicyclic monoterpene, but sandalwood, cedar, vetiver, and patchouli are described only as having well-characterized essential-oil chemistry. Woody is a sensory grouping, not a single chemical class.

### Q: Why are woods used as bases in incense?
Because they are long-lasting and gentle. A woody base anchors a blend and lets brighter top notes — florals, citrus, spices — sit on top without clashing. This is why agarwood and sandalwood are the two reference woods of Chinese incense.
