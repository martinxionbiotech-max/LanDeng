**Direct answer:** Agarwood powder and agarwood chips are the same resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* wood in two physical forms. **Chips** are cut pieces warmed whole — the classic form for 品香 (appreciation); **powder** is ground resin wood blended into sticks and cones — the form for 制香 (incense making). The form follows the use, and both carry the same authenticity risk: agarwood is one of the most heavily faked aromatics.

**Key facts:** Because genuine agarwood is expensive, both forms are targets for adulteration. Chips can be inspected by eye (resin veins, density, a burn test), while powder hides fillers behind a uniform grind — so the buying checks differ by form. No single "best" grind or chip size exists; the right target depends on the material and the formula.

## Key Takeaways

- **Chips = appreciation** (品香); **powder = making** (制香) — the same wood, two jobs.
- Chips can be **inspected by eye**; powder requires **particle-size and moisture specification**.
- Both forms are heavily faked — see [agarwood](/ingredients/agarwood/) for the adulteration map.
- The top grade, [奇楠 (qinan)](/blog/qinan-kyara/), is almost always kept whole — grinding it away is usually a sign of fraud or low material.
- Authenticity checks (burn test, gentle heat, water test) differ in what they reveal by form.

| Aroma release | Slow, layered, develops in stages | Fast, blends into a formula |

## The Same Wood, Two Forms

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood chips and powder are not different materials — they are the same resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* heartwood in two physical forms. Chips are the wood cut into pieces; powder is the wood ground to a fine, blendable particle. The split happens at processing, not in the tree.

Agarwood — *chenxiang* (沉香) in Chinese — is the resin-saturated heartwood of *Aquilaria* trees, formed when the tree responds to wounding by depositing resin. It is not a species but a condition of the wood; the fragrant part is the resin-bearing heartwood, separated from pale, scentless sapwood. See [agarwood](/ingredients/agarwood/) for the full identity and [Aquilaria sinensis on eFloras](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200014506) for the botanical record.

From that one material, processing branches by end use:

- **Chips and slivers** — cut pieces for warming on charcoal or an electric heater; small, even pieces heat most uniformly.
- **Powder** — ground resin wood for blending into sticks and cones; particle size affects packing, burn, and aroma release.

The form is a processing decision, not a quality statement — although, as the next sections explain, the form changes what you can verify and what is worth verifying.

## Appreciation (品香): Why Chips Win

> **Direct answer:** For appreciating agarwood, chips are the classic form because they keep the resin intact and release the aroma in stages — top note, body, dry-down — as heat penetrates. This staged unfolding is the whole point of 品香, and a powder cannot reproduce it the same way.

Agarwood's defining trait is that it **develops in stages** as it warms: a top note, a body, and a dry-down, rather than a single flat scent. A chip is the form that reveals this, because only its outer surface warms at first, and the resin inside is drawn out gradually as heat penetrates. The refined method is 隔火焚香 — resting the chip above charcoal buried in ash, or on an electric heater, so it releases aroma below combustion. See [indirect-fire incense](/blog/gehuo-fenxiang-setup/).

A powder, by contrast, exposes most of its surface at once, so its aroma is released quickly and uniformly — useful for blending, but it does not unfold the way a whole chip does. For someone tasting a single material, chips are the working form.

---

## Incense Making (制香): Why Powder Wins

> **Direct answer:** For making incense, agarwood is used as powder — a small proportion blended into a stick or cone formula, where it lifts and deepens the whole. A powder distributes evenly through the paste, which a chip cannot; this is why powder is the form of 制香.

In commercial and craft incense making, agarwood rarely stands alone. It is a premium anchor: a small amount of agarwood powder blended into a formula lifts and deepens the entire stick. To do that, it must be distributed uniformly through the paste with the other ingredients and a binder — and only a fine powder can do that. A chip cannot be spread evenly through a formed product.

The practical variable for a maker is **particle size and moisture**: they change how the powder packs, draws air, and burns, and how the finished stick dries and stores. The correct target depends on the binder, the other ingredients, and the production line — so no single grind transfers across formulas. See [how incense is made](/blog/how-incense-is-made/) and the [makko binder](/blog/makko-natural-binders/) for the mechanics.

## Authenticity: The Risk Is in Both Forms

> **Direct answer:** Agarwood is one of the most heavily faked aromatics, and both forms are targets — but the risk looks different. Chips can be checked by eye and by burn; powder hides fillers behind a uniform grind and needs a particle-size, moisture, and purity specification instead. The form changes which checks work.

Because genuine agarwood is expensive, it is heavily adulterated. Common fakes include dyed or oil-soaked white wood, unrelated woods dressed up with fragrance, and oil-injected low-grade material. See [agarwood](/ingredients/agarwood/) and the [authentication database](/blog/incense-authentication-database/) for the full map.

The form shifts what you can verify:

- **Chips** — resin veins, density (the 沉水 water test), and the burn test are visible or practical. Genuine agarwood gives white smoke with visible oil seepage and a clean sweet aroma; a fake gives black smoke and a burnt-wood or perfume smell. These are screening checks, not laboratory proofs.
- **Powder** — the uniform grind hides fillers and contaminants, so the checks are different: request a sample, confirm particle size and moisture, and burn a small amount against a known reference. A cheap "agarwood powder" is the easiest thing in this trade to cut with cheaper wood.

Neither form is automatically safe. A sophisticated fake exists in either — but a visible, resin-veined chip is harder to disguise than a bag of uniform brown powder.

## The 奇楠 (Qinan) Question

> **Direct answer:** 奇楠 (qinan / kyara), the top agarwood grade, is almost always sold and used as whole chips or pieces — not powder. Genuine 奇楠 is so resin-saturated it is soft and kneadable, and grinding it into an anonymous powder would destroy the very features that identify it, which is why "奇楠 powder" is a strong fraud signal.

奇楠 (qínán) is the top grade of agarwood — structurally different from ordinary agarwood: "oil wraps wood" (油包木) rather than "wood wraps oil" (木包油), so the fiber is so resin-saturated it shaves into curls and kneads into pellets, and is fragrant even unheated. See [奇楠 (qinan / kyara)](/blog/qinan-kyara/).

This grade belongs in chip or whole-piece form, and for a practical reason: the features that identify 奇楠 — softness, curl-shaving, room-temperature fragrance — are only visible or testable in a whole piece. Ground to powder, those features vanish, which is why "奇楠 powder" is widely treated as a red flag in the trade. If a seller offers "qinan powder," the burden is on them to prove the grade, since the powder form strips away the checks. See [how to buy qinan without being misled](/blog/qinan-buying-authentication/).

## Quality and Sourcing by Form

> **Direct answer:** For chips, verify resin content, density, and aroma on gentle heat; for powder, verify particle size, moisture, and purity against your own formula. The sourcing question is the same for both — species, origin, and wild-vs-cultivated — but the inspection method changes.

The buying checks split by form, while the sourcing questions stay constant:

- **Species and origin** — which *Aquilaria* species, and which region; a real seller can answer. See [agarwood](/ingredients/agarwood/).
- **Wild vs cultivated** — a documentation question with legal and price implications, since *Aquilaria* is CITES-listed and wild stands are depleted.
- **Form-specific checks** — chips: resin veins, density (沉水), aroma on gentle heat; powder: particle size, moisture, and a sample burn against your formula.

For a maker, consistency is the overriding concern: a steady, well-documented powder with known particle size beats an irreproducible top grade. See the [wholesale guide](/blog/wholesale-guide/) for the sourcing framework.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Assuming chips are "higher quality" than powder.** They are the same wood in two forms; powder is simply processed for blending.
2. **Trusting "qinan powder."** Genuine 奇楠 is kept whole; ground 奇楠 loses the features that identify it.
3. **Burning chips in an open flame.** Chips are warmed on charcoal or an electric heater, not lit like a stick.
4. **Accepting a nominal powder grade without sampling.** Particle size and moisture — not the label — decide how a powder behaves in your formula.

## FAQ

### Q: Are agarwood chips and powder the same material?
Yes — the same resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* heartwood in two physical forms. Chips are the wood cut into pieces; powder is the wood ground to a fine, blendable particle. The split happens at processing: chips for warming whole, powder for blending into sticks and cones. Neither form is inherently higher quality — the form follows the intended use.

### Q: Which is better for appreciating agarwood, chips or powder?
Chips. Agarwood's defining trait is that it develops in stages — top note, body, dry-down — as it warms, and a chip reveals this because only its outer surface warms at first, releasing the resin gradually. Powder exposes most of its surface at once, so it releases quickly and uniformly, which is useful for blending but does not unfold the way a whole chip does. For tasting a single material, warm a chip on charcoal buried in ash or on an electric heater.

### Q: Is agarwood powder usually fake?
Not automatically, but powder is the easier form to adulterate. Because the uniform grind hides fillers and foreign wood, a cheap "agarwood powder" can be cut with cheaper material without being obvious to the eye. The buying check is different from chips: request a sample, confirm particle size and moisture, and burn a small amount against a known reference. Chips are harder to fake convincingly because the resin veins are visible. See [agarwood](/ingredients/agarwood/) for the full adulteration map.

### Q: Should I buy qinan (奇楠) powder?
Be very cautious. Genuine 奇楠 — the top agarwood grade — is identified by features that only survive whole: it is soft, shaves into curls, kneads into pellets, and is fragrant at room temperature. Ground to powder, those features vanish, so "奇楠 powder" is widely treated as a fraud signal. If a seller offers it, the burden is on them to prove the grade, which is difficult once the identifying features are gone. See [how to buy qinan](/blog/qinan-buying-authentication/) before purchasing.

### Q: How do I use agarwood chips?
Warm them whole, not in an open flame. The refined method is 隔火焚香: rest a chip above charcoal buried in ash, or on an electric heater, so it releases aroma below combustion. A little material goes a long way — a single chip or a few slivers, warmed gently, is enough. Avoid burning the chip directly, which chars the resin and loses the layered aroma. See [indirect-fire incense](/blog/gehuo-fenxiang-setup/) for the setup.
