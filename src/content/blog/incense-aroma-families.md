**Direct answer:** LanDeng classifies its 150 botanical incense ingredients into **ten aroma families** — woody, resinous, sweet, herbal, spicy, floral, citrus, earthy, smoky, and balsamic. Each family groups materials that share a characteristic scent character, and most ingredients belong to more than one family (agarwood, for example, is woody *and* resinous *and* sweet). This page is the hub that maps all ten, backed by the machine-readable `aroma.json` dataset.

**Key facts:** The ten families come from `aroma.json`, a Schema.org `Dataset` served from `data.incenseherbs.com`. Each family is a `DefinedTerm` with its member ingredients listed by slug. A family is a **scent character**, not a botanical class — the same plant part can carry several characters, and one character can span woods, resins, flowers, and roots.

## Key Takeaways

- The ten families are **woody, resinous, sweet, herbal, spicy, floral, citrus, earthy, smoky, balsamic**.
- A family is a **scent character, not a taxonomy** — most of the 150 ingredients carry more than one.

- This page makes **no health or medical claims** — aroma character is descriptive, not therapeutic.

## What an Aroma Family Is (and Is Not)

> **Direct answer:** An aroma family is a shared scent character — woody, floral, spicy, and so on — not a botanical category. One ingredient can belong to several families, and one family can contain woods, resins, flowers, and roots that happen to smell alike.

The family system is a *sensory* grouping, which is why it does not map cleanly onto botany:

- **Agarwood** is woody, resinous, and sweet at once — its character is layered, so it sits in three families.
- **Vetiver** is earthy, woody, and smoky — a grass root, not a wood, yet it reads as woody.
- **Frankincense** is resinous, citrus, and earthy — a resin that also carries a bright citrus edge.

This is a feature, not a bug: the families describe how a material *smells and behaves in a blend*, which is what a blender or a buyer actually works with. The canonical membership is in [aroma.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/aroma.json).

## The Ten Families, Defined

### Woody (木香) — the backbone of Chinese incense

> **Direct answer:** Woody is the dry, warm, clean scent of aromatic wood — from the creamy "buttery" smoothness of sandalwood to the deep, layered sweetness of agarwood and the clean pencil-box character of cedar. It is the foundation family of classical Chinese incense.

Canonical members: agarwood, benzoin, cedar, orange-peel, patchouli, sandalwood, vetiver, borneol, pine-resin. Full treatment in the [woody family page](/blog/woody-aroma-family/).

### Resinous (树脂香) — deep, sticky, aromatic

> **Direct answer:** Resinous is the scent of gum-resin — frankincense's clean, bright resin, pine resin's conifer sharpness, sweetgum's sweet balsam. These are materials that melt under heat rather than burn like wood.

Canonical members: agarwood, frankincense, pine-resin, sweetgum. Full treatment in the [resinous family page](/blog/resinous-aroma-family/).

### Sweet (甜香) — sugar, honey, amber

> **Direct answer:** Sweet is the sugared, honeyed character that rounds a blend — benzoin's vanilla, osmanthus's apricot-and-honey, rose's romantic sweetness. It is the widest family, spanning resins, flowers, and woods.

Canonical members: agarwood, benzoin, cedar, chrysanthemum, cinnamon, jasmine, orange-peel, osmanthus, rose, sandalwood, angelica, calamus, sweetgum, ambergris.

### Herbal (草本香) — green, grassy, bitter-green

> **Direct answer:** Herbal is the green, grassy, sometimes bitter character of leafy and rooty herbs — mugwort's camphoraceous green, chrysanthemum's bittersweet floral, angelica's aromatic root. It bridges kitchen-garden and temple.

Canonical members: chrysanthemum, mugwort, angelica, calamus.

### Spicy (辛香) — warm, clove-cinnamon heat

> **Direct answer:** Spicy is the warm, penetrating heat of cinnamon, clove, and patchouli's spicy edge. These are the "warming" notes that give a blend its bite and its lift.

Canonical members: cinnamon, clove, patchouli, angelica, calamus.

### Floral (花香) — blossom, honeyed, heady

> **Direct answer:** Floral is the blossom scent of rose, jasmine, and osmanthus — sweet, honeyed, and in jasmine's case heady. It is a largely modern-or-symbolic addition to a canon built on woods and resins.

Canonical members: chrysanthemum, jasmine, osmanthus, rose. Full treatment in the [floral family page](/blog/floral-aroma-family/).

### Citrus (柑橘香) — bright, zesty, lemony

> **Direct answer:** Citrus is the bright, zesty, lemony character carried by orange peel and, in part, by frankincense's fresh top. It is the smallest family alongside smoky, and reads as a lifting top note.

Canonical members: frankincense, orange-peel.

### Earthy (土香) — rooty, soil-like, grounded

> **Direct answer:** Earthy is the rooty, soil-like, grounding character of vetiver, patchouli, and myrrh — heavy, long-lasting base notes that anchor lighter materials.

Canonical members: frankincense, myrrh, patchouli, vetiver, calamus, ambergris.

### Smoky (烟香) — charred, burnt-wood

> **Direct answer:** Smoky is the charred, burnt-wood character, canonically carried by vetiver alone. It is the narrowest family, defined by a single representative material.

Canonical member: vetiver.

### Balsamic (香脂香) — sweet-resinous, vanilla-amber

> **Direct answer:** Balsamic is the sweet-resinous, vanilla-amber character of benzoin, myrrh, and pine resin — materials that are simultaneously sweet and resinous, and that act as fixatives.

Canonical members: benzoin, myrrh, patchouli, pine-resin, sweetgum.

## How the Families Overlap

> **Direct answer:** The families are not a partition — most ingredients belong to several. A single material can be woody and resinous (agarwood), floral and sweet (rose), or earthy and smoky (vetiver). The overlap is what makes blending an art rather than a checklist.

The overlap is visible in the data:

- **Agarwood** appears in woody, resinous, and sweet — the deepest, most layered material in the canon.
- **Patchouli** appears in woody, spicy, earthy, and balsamic — a leafy herb that reads as rooted wood.
- **Vetiver** appears in woody, earthy, and smoky — the one material that spans the ground and the smoke.

This is why a blend is composed on character, not on a single family tag. The full relationship graph is in [relationships.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/relationships.json).

## The Data Layer

> **Direct answer:** The ten families are published as a machine-readable `Dataset` — [aroma.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/aroma.json) — with each family a `DefinedTerm` listing its member ingredients by slug. The same family tags also appear in each ingredient page's `aroma` frontmatter field.

- **Canonical source:** [aroma.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/aroma.json) — ten `DefinedTerm` records, each with a `memberIngredients` list.
- **On each entity page:** the `aroma` frontmatter field carries that material's family tags, feeding the page's meta description and `DefinedTerm`.
- **Companion datasets:** [ingredients.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/ingredients.json) (150 entities) and [relationships.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/relationships.json) (450 typed edges, including aroma links).

The full dataset inventory is on the [knowledge map](/blog/landeng-knowledge-map/).

## FAQ

### Q: How many aroma families does LanDeng use?
Ten: woody, resinous, sweet, herbal, spicy, floral, citrus, earthy, smoky, and balsamic. They are defined in [aroma.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/aroma.json), each as a `DefinedTerm` with its member ingredients listed.

### Q: Can one ingredient belong to more than one aroma family?
Yes — and most do. Agarwood is woody, resinous, and sweet; patchouli is woody, spicy, earthy, and balsamic; vetiver is woody, earthy, and smoky. The families describe scent character, not a single botanical class, so a layered material carries several tags.

### Q: What is the difference between "resinous" and "balsamic"?
They are related but distinct. Resinous is the deep, sticky gum-resin character (frankincense, pine resin); balsamic is the sweet-resinous, vanilla-amber character (benzoin, myrrh). Many balsamic materials are also resinous or sweet — benzoin, for example, sits in woody, sweet, and balsamic — but the two families emphasize different aspects of the same physical material.

### Q: What is the difference between "woody" and "earthy"?
Woody is the dry, warm scent of aromatic wood (sandalwood, cedar, agarwood); earthy is the rooty, soil-like, grounded character of roots and resins (vetiver, patchouli, myrrh). They overlap — vetiver reads as both woody and earthy — but woody is the cleaner wood register, earthy the heavier, darker ground register.

### Q: Which aroma families are the smallest?
Smoky (a single canonical member, vetiver) and citrus (two — frankincense and orange-peel). They are deliberately narrow: smoky is a distinctive charred character carried by one material, and citrus is a bright top note that appears in only two canon members.

### Q: Where can I see the full membership list?
The machine-readable list is in [aroma.json](https://data.incenseherbs.com/datasets/aroma.json). The human-readable hub pages for the three flagship families are [woody](/blog/woody-aroma-family/), [resinous](/blog/resinous-aroma-family/), and [floral](/blog/floral-aroma-family/), and every ingredient's `aroma` field lists its own family tags.
