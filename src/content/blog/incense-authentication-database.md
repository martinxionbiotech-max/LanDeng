# Incense Authentication: How to Spot Fake Agarwood, Sandalwood, and Resin

**Direct answer:** The most common incense fakes are **oil-boiled, oil-injected, and chemically-soaked** wood passed off as agarwood or sandalwood. The practical detection signals are burn behavior (white smoke and oil seepage vs black smoke), aroma on gentle heat (complex and sweet vs "perfume" or "burnt wood"), and the **sink test** for agarwood density.

**Key facts:** This is a buyer-intelligence reference, not a laboratory method. For definitive authentication, use a reputable seller and, where it matters, laboratory testing.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The three common fakes: **煮油 (oil-boiling), 高压灌油 (oil-injection), 泡药水 (chemical/dye soaking).**
- Signals: **white smoke + oil seepage** (real agarwood) vs **black smoke** (many fakes).
- A **complex, sweet aroma on gentle heat** is the hallmark; "perfume" or "burnt wood" is a red flag.
- The **sink test** works for agarwood density (沉香 = "sinking fragrance").
- These are traditional heuristics — not laboratory proof.

---

## The Common Fakes

| Method | Chinese | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Oil-boiling | 煮油 | Boils ordinary wood in oil to add weight and scent |
| High-pressure oil injection | 高压灌油 | Forces oil into non-resinous wood |
| Chemical/dye soaking | 泡药水 | Dyes and scents non-resinous "white wood" |
| Substitution | — | Unrelated woods (Lauraceae, Burseraceae) with synthetic fragrance |

---

## Detection Signals

| Signal | Real (tends to) | Fake (tends to) |
|---|---|---|
| Burn smoke | White smoke with oil seepage | Black smoke |
| Gentle-heat aroma | Complex, sweet, layered | "Perfume" or "burnt wood" |
| Room-temperature aroma | 奇楠 is aromatic even unheated | Synthetic has a one-note smell |
| Density | Resin-dense agarwood sinks (沉水) | Oiled wood may sink but smells wrong |

---

## The Sink Test (Agarwood)

Because 沉香 means "sinking fragrance," the classic density test is literal: does the piece sink in water? Resin-dense agarwood sinks; lower-grade wood floats. (See the [grading guide](/blog/agarwood-grading-guide/) for the full 沉水 system.)

**Caveat:** oil-injection fakes are designed to *pass* weight/density checks, so the sink test alone is not proof — combine it with the burn and aroma signals.

---

## The Honest Limits

These are **traditional buyer heuristics**, accumulated in the Chinese agarwood market — not laboratory methods. A skilled counterfeit can defeat any single signal. The reliable route is:

1. Buy from a **reputable seller** who names the species and origin.
2. Use **laboratory testing** where value justifies it.
3. Treat "too cheap for the claimed grade" as the strongest single signal.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** My contrarian experience: the strongest authenticity signal is not a burn test — it is the **seller**.

**Reasoning:**
- A vendor who cannot name the species, the origin, and the grade is already giving you all the information you need.
- The burn and aroma signals are a useful *backup* — but “what exactly is this, and where is it from?” is the honest shortcut.

## FAQ

### Q: How can I tell if agarwood is real?
A: Check the burn (white smoke + oil seepage vs black smoke), the gentle-heat aroma (complex and sweet vs "perfume"), and the sink test — and prefer a reputable seller. See the [grading guide](/blog/agarwood-grading-guide/).

### Q: What is "oil-injected" agarwood?
A: Non-resinous wood forced with oil under high pressure to add weight and scent — a common fake. See the [grading guide](/blog/agarwood-grading-guide/).

### Q: Is the sink test reliable?
A: It is a useful density signal, but oil-injection fakes are designed to pass weight checks, so combine it with burn and aroma signals.

### Q: Why is fake sandalwood common?
A: True *Santalum album* is scarce and expensive, so substitutes (other species or synthetic fragrance) are common. See the [sandalwood guide](/blog/sandalwood-incense/).
