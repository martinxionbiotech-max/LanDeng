# 江南李主帐中香 (The Lord of Jiangnan's Bedchamber Incense): Recipe

**Direct answer:** 江南李主帐中香 is a classical 合香 attributed to **李煜 (Li Yu)**, the last ruler of the Southern Tang — hence "江南李主" ("Lord of Jiangnan"). Its most famous version steams **agarwood and a trace of sandalwood inside hollowed goose pears**, infusing the wood with a clean, sweet fruit note.

**Key facts:** This is one of the best-documented classical recipes, with several versions in the 香乘. Its "assistant" is a *fruit*, and its "guide" is a *process* (steaming and aging) rather than an ingredient.

---

## The Recipe (鹅梨 / goose-pear version)

> **沉香末一两，檀香末一钱，鹅梨十枚** — one *liǎng* agarwood powder, one *qián* sandalwood powder, ten goose pears. Hollow each pear like a jar, fill with the powders, seal with the pear's top, steam three times (蒸三溜), strip the peel, grind and blend, then age (久窨) before burning.

| Role | Ingredient | Job in the blend |
|---|---|---|
| 君 (chief) | 沉香 agarwood | The deep, resinous backbone — the formula exists to carry it |
| 臣 (minister) | 檀香 sandalwood | A trace (1/10 by weight) that warms and rounds the agarwood |
| 佐 (assistant) | 鹅梨 goose pear | The fruit's juice lends a clean, sweet top note |
| 使 (guide) | steaming + aging | The process that fuses the fruit into the wood |

---

## Other Recorded Versions

The 香乘 records several 帐中香 recipes, including one that **soaks agarwood in storax oil (苏合油) for a hundred days** and finishes it with rose water (蔷薇水). The goose-pear version is simply the most famous — and the most illustrative of how a fruit can be an "ingredient" in the classical sense.

---

## Why the Ratio Matters

Note the economy: **ten pears and a full 两 of agarwood, against a single 钱 of sandalwood.** The chief dominates; the minister is a trace. This is the disciplined logic of 合香 — a little support, not a chorus.

---

## The Honest Caveats

- The 李煜 attribution is traditional, widely repeated, but not a signed historical document.
- The historical 两/钱 weights varied by dynasty — read the ratio, not the grams.
- 鹅梨 refers to an old pear variety (likely a type of 鸭梨); the exact cultivar is not fixed today.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** This recipe is the single best teaching example of 合香 — because the "assistant" is a pear and the "guide" is a steaming step, it makes the role system impossible to romanticize away.

**Reasoning:**
- When the signature note is a fruit, the formula's *structure* becomes visible instead of hidden behind "precious woods."
- The 10:1:1 pear-to-wood ratio is a concrete, memorable illustration of the chief's dominance.

## FAQ

### Q: What is 江南李主帐中香?
A bedchamber incense attributed to Li Yu, the last ruler of the Southern Tang — most famously made by steaming agarwood inside hollowed goose pears.

### Q: Why use a pear?
The pear's juice infuses the agarwood with a clean, sweet fruit note — an early example of a fruit as an "ingredient" in incense.

### Q: What are the other versions?
One soaks agarwood in storax oil (苏合油) for a hundred days and finishes with rose water (蔷薇水).

### Q: Is the Li Yu attribution certain?
It is traditional and widely repeated, but not a signed, verifiable historical document.
