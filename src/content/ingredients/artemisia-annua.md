> **Direct answer:** Sweet wormwood (青蒿, qīnghāo) is the herb *Artemisia annua*, an annual member of the *Artemisia* (mugwort) genus, family Asteraceae. It has a fresh, herbal, slightly camphoraceous, faintly sweet scent, and is a classical Chinese aromatic and materia-medica herb. It is a different species from, and must be distinguished from, mugwort (艾草, *Artemisia argyi*).

## Aroma profile

青蒿 has a fresh, green, herbaceous scent with a camphoraceous, slightly medicinal edge and a faint sweetness — the clean, bitter-green aroma of the *Artemisia* herbs. It is lighter and less musky-bitter than mugwort (艾草), and it reads as a crisp aromatic herb rather than a heavy, smoky one.

## The 青蒿 / 艾草 distinction (read this)

青蒿 and 艾草 are **closely related but distinct species in the same genus** (*Artemisia*):

- **青蒿 (sweet wormwood)** — *Artemisia annua*, an annual herb with a fresh, camphoraceous scent. This entry.
- **艾草 (mugwort)** — *Artemisia argyi* (and *A. vulgaris*), the perennial mugwort of moxibustion and 艾 incense. Its own entry.

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之四, records 青蒿 as its own entry ("草蒿…北人呼為青蒿"), distinct from 艾 (the mugwort entry in the same section). **This page describes 青蒿 = *Artemisia annua*, not 艾草.**

## Botanical source and origin

*Artemisia annua* is an annual herb in the Asteraceae (daisy) family, native to Asia and widespread across China and Eurasia. Flora of China records the species; it is one of many Chinese *Artemisia* herbs, including mugwort (*A. argyi*), and the two are distinct species despite sharing the genus and a general herbal character.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 青蒿 is a classical Chinese materia-medica herb, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目). This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Aromatic herb:** its fresh, camphoraceous scent places it among the aromatic herbs of the 香囊 (sachet) tradition.
- **Naming:** the *Bencao Gangmu* records the aliases 草蒿 and 香蒿, and explains "北人呼為青蒿" — the northerners call it 青蒿.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica and aromatic records are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Asteraceae placement, and the distinction from 艾草.

## FAQ

**What is 青蒿 (sweet wormwood)?** The annual herb *Artemisia annua* (Asteraceae), a classical Chinese aromatic and materia-medica herb with a fresh, herbal, camphoraceous, faintly sweet scent. It is one of many Chinese *Artemisia* herbs, distinct from mugwort.

**Is 青蒿 the same as 艾草 (mugwort)?** No — closely related but distinct species in the same genus (*Artemisia*). 青蒿 is *Artemisia annua*, an annual with a fresh, camphoraceous scent; 艾草 is *Artemisia argyi* (and *A. vulgaris*), the perennial mugwort of moxibustion. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records them as separate entries.

**What does 青蒿 smell like?** Fresh, green, and herbaceous, with a camphoraceous, slightly medicinal edge and a faint sweetness — the clean, bitter-green aroma of the *Artemisia* herbs. It is lighter and less musky-bitter than mugwort (艾草).

**Is sweet wormwood an incense ingredient?** It is a classical aromatic and materia-medica herb, and its fresh camphoraceous scent places it among the 香囊 (sachet) herbs, but it is not a lead 香材. It reads as a crisp aromatic herb rather than a resin or wood.

**Why is it called 青蒿?** The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records the aliases 草蒿 and 香蒿 and explains that "北人呼為青蒿" — the northerners call it 青蒿. The name marks it among the *Artemisia* herbs, distinguished from mugwort (艾).
