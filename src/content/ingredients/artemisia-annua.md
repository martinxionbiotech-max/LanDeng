> **Direct answer:** Sweet wormwood (青蒿, qīnghāo) is the herb *Artemisia annua*, an annual member of the *Artemisia* (mugwort) genus, family Asteraceae. It has a fresh, herbal, slightly camphoraceous, faintly sweet scent, and is a classical Chinese aromatic and materia-medica herb. It is a different species from, and must be distinguished from, mugwort (艾草, *Artemisia argyi*).

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** The name 青蒿 means "green wormwood" — 青 (green) + 蒿 (the wormwood/artemisia class). The *Bencao Gangmu* records the aliases 草蒿 ("grass wormwood") and 香蒿 ("fragrant wormwood") and explains that northerners call it 青蒿 (北人呼為青蒿). The 蒿 character names a broad class of *Artemisia* herbs.

The name 青蒿 (qīnghāo) is a color-and-class name: 青 "green" + 蒿, the character for the wormwood/artemisia class. The lifecycle (annual versus perennial) is itself a useful clue to their roles.

青蒿 carries a modern association that demands a careful boundary. *Artemisia annua* is the source of artemisinin, a compound central to modern antimalarial therapy — a discovery so significant it was recognized with a Nobel Prize. - **Incense use is aromatic, not medicinal.** This page describes 青蒿's fresh, camphoraceous aroma and its classical aromatic/sachet role. It makes no claim that burning or carrying the herb does anything medical.
- **Fame is not fragrance.** The artemisinin story is about chemistry and medicine, not scent. A buyer should not pay an incense premium because the plant produced a famous drug.

This is the honest framing: record the modern fame, and keep it out of the aromatic claim.
