**Technical answer:** 山鸡椒 — *shānjījiāo* in Chinese — is the small, aromatic fruit of *Litsea cubeba*, a laurel-family (Lauraceae) tree native to southern China and Southeast Asia. It is the source of litsea cubeba (may chang) essential oil, with a bright, lemony-citrus, slightly spicy aroma. It is a modern crossover in fragrance rather than a classical incense material — and, critically, it shares a Chinese materia-medica name with cubeb (荜澄茄), which is a different plant, so this page disambiguates the two explicitly.

## Aroma profile

山鸡椒 is bright and citrusy — lemony, clean, and slightly spicy, with a faint floral lift. It is often compared to lemongrass or lemon verbena, and its essential oil is a staple of modern perfumery and aromatherapy as a fresh, lemony top note. In incense it would read as a *bright citrus accent*, a modern addition rather than a classical material.

## The 荜澄茄 / 山鸡椒 confusion (read this)

山鸡椒 (*Litsea cubeba*) and 荜澄茄 (cubeb, *Piper cubeba*) are different plants, but Chinese materia medica has applied the name 荜澄茄 to both, which is a standing source of confusion:

- **荜澄茄 (cubeb)** — *Piper cubeba*, a pepper-family (Piperaceae) spice vine native to Indonesia, with a sharp, peppery, camphoraceous bite. Its own entry.
- **山鸡椒 (this page)** — *Litsea cubeba*, a laurel-family (Lauraceae) tree native to southern China, with a lemony-citrus aroma.

**This page describes 山鸡椒 = *Litsea cubeba* = mountain pepper.** When a classical source says 荜澄茄, it may mean either plant — confirm which is meant. The two are different species in different families.

## Botanical source and origin

*Litsea cubeba* is a small to medium evergreen tree of the laurel family (Lauraceae), native to southern China, Southeast Asia, and parts of the eastern Himalaya. The part used is the small, black, pepper-like fruit, which is steam-distilled to yield litsea cubeba essential oil (also called may chang oil), valued for its lemony-citrus character. The laurel family also includes cinnamon (*Cinnamomum*) and bay laurel (*Laurus nobilis*).

## In Chinese tradition

- **Modern fragrance:** 山鸡椒 is best known today as the source of litsea cubeba (may chang) essential oil, a lemony-citrus top note in modern perfumery and aromatherapy.
- **Name overlap with cubeb:** in Chinese materia medica the name 荜澄茄 has been applied to both cubeb (*Piper cubeba*) and 山鸡椒 (*Litsea cubeba*), a documented naming confusion.
- **Not a classical incense material:** 山鸡椒 does not appear as a defining material in the classical incense compendium 香乘; its fragrance use is a modern crossover.
