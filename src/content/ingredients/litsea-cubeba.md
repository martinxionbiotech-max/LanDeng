> **Direct answer:** Litsea cubeba (山鸡椒, shānjījiāo) is the small, aromatic fruit of *Litsea cubeba*, a laurel-family (Lauraceae) tree native to southern China and Southeast Asia. It is the source of litsea cubeba (may chang) essential oil, with a bright, lemony-citrus, slightly spicy aroma. It is a modern crossover in fragrance rather than a classical incense material, and it shares a Chinese name with cubeb (荜澄茄), a different plant.

## What determines quality?

> **Direct answer:** Litsea cubeba quality turns on the essential oil — its brightness, lemony-citrus clarity, and the dominant citral content that gives the lemon character. Because the product is an oil, the quality checks are on the oil, not on a dried fruit.

The quality points center on the oil, since that is the commercial product:

1. **Oil brightness** — a clean, bright, lemony-citrus odor; a dull or off scent signals poor distillation or age.
2. **Citral content** — the lemon character comes largely from citral (a mix of the isomers geranial and neral); a good oil is citral-forward.
3. **Clarity and freshness** — clear, fresh oil, properly stored.
4. **Identity** — confirm it is *Litsea cubeba* oil, not confused with lemongrass or another citrus-lemony oil.

These are oil-quality checks, consistent with a material whose value is a distilled essential oil rather than a dried plant part.
