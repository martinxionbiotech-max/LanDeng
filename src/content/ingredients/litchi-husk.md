**Technical answer:** Litchi husk — *lìzhī ké* (荔枝壳) in Chinese — is the dried, leathery red husk (pericarp) of the litchi fruit, *Litchi chinensis*, a tropical evergreen tree of the soapberry family (Sapindaceae) native to southern China. It is a distinctly Chinese incense ingredient: the 香乘 (Xiang Cheng) records a "荔枝香" (litchi incense) made from the husk, praised as the freshest and most delicate of fruit-peel incenses. The husk is a *fruit peel* material, used for its sweet, fruity, faintly floral scent.

## Aroma profile

Litchi husk, when dried and burned, gives a sweet, fruity, faintly floral scent that is notably fresh and delicate — lighter than citrus peel and less sharp than spice. It is a *fruit-peel* note, in the same family as tangerine peel (陈皮), but sweeter and more floral. The 香乘 singles it out: of the fruit peels, litchi husk makes the freshest, most refined incense (取其壳合香最清馥).

## Botanical source and origin

*Litchi chinensis* is a tropical evergreen tree of the soapberry family (Sapindaceae), native to the lowlands of southern China — Guangdong, Fujian, and nearby regions — and cultivated across warm regions worldwide. The fruit is the familiar litchi, and its rough red husk (pericarp) is the part used in incense. The husk is dried and blended, and the litchi has been cultivated in China for well over a millennium.

> **A naming caution:** litchi husk (荔枝壳) is a fruit *peel* material, distinct from the litchi fruit itself and from other fruit peels such as tangerine peel (陈皮). It is valued for its sweetness and delicacy, not for the taste of the fruit.

## In Chinese tradition

- **荔枝香 (litchi incense):** the 香乘 (Xiang Cheng) records 荔枝香 — an incense made from the litchi husk — quoting the 香谱: "取其壳合香最清馥" ("taking its husk to blend incense, it is the freshest and most refined"). It is a classical fruit-peel specialty.
- **A distinctly Chinese material:** litchi husk is a rare example of a fruit peel that became a named incense material in its own right, tied to the litchi's southern-Chinese origin.
- **Materia medica:** 荔枝 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 果部) with various described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The incense use is documented in the classical recipe record. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity and the husk's role as a classical fruit-peel incense material.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Litchi husk should be clean, dry, and intact, with a faintly sweet, fruity scent when warmed.
- It is a fruit *peel* material — lighter and more delicate than woods and resins, and usually blended, not burned alone.
- Distinguish litchi husk (荔枝壳) from the litchi fruit and from other fruit peels such as tangerine peel (陈皮).

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
