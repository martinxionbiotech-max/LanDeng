> **Direct answer:** Yulan magnolia (玉兰, yùlán) is the flower of *Magnolia denudata*, a deciduous tree of the magnolia family (Magnoliaceae) native to central and eastern China. Its large, white, fragrant spring flowers are a celebrated symbol of early spring, and the flower is used in Chinese incense and fragrance as a fresh white-floral note. It is closely related to, and easily confused with, 辛夷 (the dried magnolia flower bud, *Magnolia biondii*).

## Aroma profile

> **Direct answer:** 玉兰 is a fresh, clear white floral with a green, slightly lemony brightness and none of the heavy indole of jasmine or the spicy camphor of its bud counterpart 辛夷. It is lighter and more "open" than a bud — a clean spring-flower character that lifts rather than deepens. In a blend it works as a fresh floral top note.

## Botanical identity

> **Direct answer:** *Magnolia denudata* is a deciduous tree of the magnolia family (Magnoliaceae). Flora of China describes a tree to 25 m tall whose large flowers — 10–16 cm across, with nine white tepals usually pinkish at the base — open on bare branches in late winter and early spring, before the leaves. It is grown as an ornamental and for timber and is widely cultivated in temperate parts of the world.

Flora of China describes *Magnolia denudata* precisely: **trees to 25 m tall, to 1 m in trunk diameter**, with bark that is deep gray and coarsely fissured; leaf blades obovate to broadly obovate, 10–15(−18) cm; **flowers appearing before the leaves**, 10–16 cm across, erect and fragrant, with **nine white tepals**, oblong-obovate, **usually pinkish at the base**; flowering **February–March**, fruiting August–September. It grows in forests at 500–1,000 m in Anhui, Chongqing, northern Guangdong, Guizhou, Hubei, Hunan, Jiangxi, Shaanxi, Yunnan, and Zhejiang, and is "grown as an ornamental and for timber," "widely cultivated in temperate parts of the world." Flora of China places it in the segregate genus *Yulania* as *Yulania denudata*; GBIF accepts *Magnolia denudata*. See [Yulania denudata in Flora of China](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=250090269) and [Magnolia denudata on GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/species/3153582).

## The 玉兰 / 辛夷 distinction (read this)

> **Direct answer:** Two magnolia-family materials share the "magnolia" name but differ in species and, crucially, plant part: 玉兰 is the open white flower of *Magnolia denudata*, while 辛夷 is the dried, unopened bud of *Magnolia biondii* and relatives. Flower vs bud is the key distinction — an open flower is fresh and green-lemony, while a closed bud is spicy and camphoraceous.

The distinction is real and worth pinning down:

- **玉兰** (*Magnolia denudata*) — the open white flower; fresh, green, slightly lemony. This entry.
- **辛夷** (*Magnolia biondii*, Flora of China *Yulania biondii*) — the dried, unopened bud; spicy and camphoraceous. Its own entry.

The practical point is that **flower and bud are different materials** from different (though related) species, with different aromas: an open flower reads fresh and green-lemony, while a closed bud reads spicy and camphoraceous. A buyer should not treat "magnolia" as one material; the plant part — flower vs bud — is the specification.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** 玉兰 is a flower, handled by careful drying to preserve its fresh, green-lemony white-floral note. The flower is delicate and the note is volatile, so gentle, low-temperature drying is the practical concern; the open flower is the material, distinguishing it from the closed bud (辛夷) that is dried unopened.

As a flower, 玉兰 is handled by drying — carefully, because the fresh white-floral note is delicate and volatile. Gentle, low-temperature drying preserves the green-lemony character; harsh or prolonged heat flattens it. The open flower is the material of use, which is exactly the distinction from 辛夷: the bud is dried *unopened* (which concentrates its spicy camphor), while 玉兰 is the *open* flower (fresh and green-lemony). Plant part and drying are therefore the two processing-relevant variables.

## What determines yulan magnolia quality?

> **Direct answer:** Quality turns on freshness and the preservation of the fresh, green-lemony white-floral note — a clean, gently dried open flower beats a faded, musty, or heat-damaged one. Plant-part clarity (open flower, not bud) is the identity check, since 玉兰 and 辛夷 are different materials.

The quality factors, in rough order:

1. **Aroma** — fresh, clear white floral with a green, slightly lemony brightness; a clean, springy note beats a faded or musty one.
2. **Condition** — clean, well-dried open flower, free of mold and damage.
3. **Plant part** — the open flower (玉兰), not the closed bud (辛夷).
4. **Freshness** — the delicate floral note fades with age and poor storage.

There is no standardized commercial grading ladder for 玉兰; aroma and plant-part clarity are the practical signals.
