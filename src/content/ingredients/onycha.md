> **Direct answer:** Onycha (甲香, jiǎxiāng) is the operculum, the hard, shell-like disc that seals the opening of certain marine gastropods (sea snails). In classical Chinese incense it was used as a fixative that binds and stabilizes a blend's scent, contributing a faint marine, animalic undertone. It is animal-derived, not botanical — one of two animal materials in this encyclopedia alongside musk.

## Botanical (and zoological) identity

> **Direct answer:** Onycha is not botanical at all — it is the operculum, the hard, horn-like disc on the foot of certain sea snails (marine gastropods, class Gastropoda, phylum Mollusca) that seals the shell opening when the animal withdraws. It is a mollusc structure, distinct from the shell itself.

Onycha is not a plant — it is the operculum, the hard, horn-like disc on the foot of certain sea snails (marine gastropods) that seals the shell opening when the animal withdraws. It is a mollusc structure, distinct from the shell itself, and it is animal-derived through and through. The class Gastropoda (the snails and slugs) is the zoological context, and the operculum is a common feature of many marine gastropods — a protective "door" that closes when the animal retracts. This zoological identity, not any botanical one, is the source of the material, which is why onycha is placed here as the encyclopedia's second animal material alongside musk.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** 甲香 (jiǎxiāng) means "shell fragrance" — 甲 for the shell or the hard covering, 香 for "fragrance." The name records the material's origin in the snail's hard operculum and its use as a fragrance fixative, not as a scent in its own right.

The name 甲香 is transparent: 甲 (shell/hard covering) + 香 (fragrance), naming the snail's hard operculum as a "fragrance" material. The name is a fixative-name, not a scent-name — it records the material's use in 合香 (blended incense) as a functional binder and stabilizer, not as an aromatic showcase. This is consistent with the classical treatment: onycha appears in formulas dosed as a fixative, specified to be 法制 (treated) before use, and its name reflects its role rather than any scent it carries.

## Why is it used in incense?

> **Direct answer:** Onycha was used in classical 合香 as a fixative — to bind and stabilize the volatile aromatics of a blend, so that the scent holds and does not dissipate. It was processed (法制) before use and dosed as a functional ingredient, not as a lead fragrance. It is now a historical ingredient.

Onycha's role in classical incense was the **fixative's role**: it was added to blends to bind and stabilize the volatile aromatics of the other ingredients, so that the finished incense held its scent. It was specified to be 法制 (treated) before use, and it was dosed as a *functional* ingredient — present for what it does to the blend, not for a scent it contributes. This is why onycha appears in court and temple blends as a stabilizing agent: the material's job was to hold the frankincense, borneol, sandalwood, and musk together, not to add its own note. It is now a historical ingredient, substituted or omitted in modern reproductions.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Classical 合香 (blended incense):** 甲香 appears in the 香乘 (Xiang Cheng) and 香譜 (Xiangpu) as a fixative in court and temple blends, most famously in the 花蕊夫人衙香 (Huarui Furen's court incense), where it is dosed at 一两 (one liǎng) alongside frankincense and borneol, and is specified to be 法制 (treated) before use.
- **Materia medica:** 甲香 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 介部) with various described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Onycha vs the animal fixatives: musk and ambergris

> **Direct answer:** Onycha, musk, and ambergris are the animal-derived materials of the classical canon, but with different roles and status. Onycha is the shellfish fixative, now obsolete; musk is the deer-gland animalic fixative; ambergris is the whale-derived fixative. Onycha is the least fragrant of the three — a functional fixative, not a scent.

| | Onycha (甲香) | Musk (麝香) | Ambergris (龙涎香) |

| Fragrance | Faint, marine, animalic | Strongly animalic | Marine, sweet, animalic |
| Modern status | Largely obsolete | Regulated/restricted | Rare, protected |

The three are grouped as animal fixatives, but onycha is the functional one — least fragrant, most purely a fixative, and now the least used.
