**Direct answer:** Fenugreek — *húlúbā* (葫芦巴) in Chinese — is the small, hard seed of *Trigonella foenum-graecum*, a legume (Fabaceae) native to the Mediterranean and western Asia. Its roasted seed has a warm, sweet, maple-like, faintly nutty-bitter aroma, and it entered Chinese materia medica as a late, "recent" aromatic rather than a classical 香材 — the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records it as a relatively new arrival.

## Aroma profile

葫芦巴's raw seed is mildly bitter, but roasting brings out a warm, sweet, maple-syrup note with a nutty, faintly celery-like edge — the compound sotolone is responsible for that characteristic maple character. It is a food-spice profile first; as a fragrant material it reads warm and gourmand rather than woodsy or resinous.

## A provenance note (read this)

葫芦巴 is a **later addition** to the Chinese record, not a classical 香材:

- The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之四, records 葫芦巴 as a relatively recent materia-medica item: "今醫家治元臟虛冷為要藥，而唐已前方不見用，本草不著，蓋是近出" — it became an important medicine in later times, but does not appear in pre-Tang formulas, and is not in the older bencao, being a recent arrival.
- Its name points to a foreign origin: the text notes "種出海南諸番" — the seed came from overseas ("barbarian" regions).

This page records it honestly as a later aromatic and food-spice crossover — **no classical 香材 origin is claimed.**

## Botanical source and origin

*Trigonella foenum-graecum* is an annual legume native to the Mediterranean and western Asia, now cultivated widely across North Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, and China. The seed is used as a spice and flavoring; Flora of China records the species as an introduced/cultivated plant.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 葫芦巴 appears in traditional Chinese materia medica. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Aromatic/food use:** the roasted seed's sweet, maple-like scent brings a gourmand warmth, and it appears in spice and flavoring contexts.
- **Not a classical 香材:** it is absent from the classical incense canon and is treated here as a later crossover.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Fabaceae placement, and its late arrival in the Chinese record.

## FAQ

**What is 葫芦巴 (fenugreek)?** The small, hard seed of *Trigonella foenum-graecum*, a legume (Fabaceae) native to the Mediterranean and western Asia. Roasted, it has a warm, sweet, maple-like, faintly nutty-bitter aroma, and it entered Chinese materia medica as a late, "recent" arrival rather than a classical 香材.

**Is fenugreek a classical Chinese incense ingredient?** No. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records 葫芦巴 as a relatively recent addition — it appears in later materia medica but not in pre-Tang formulas, and its name points to a foreign ("番") origin. It is a later aromatic and food-spice crossover, not part of the classical incense canon.

**Why does fenugreek smell like maple?** The characteristic sweet, maple-like note comes from sotolone, an aroma compound in the roasted seed. The raw seed is more bitter; roasting develops the warm, sweet, maple-syrup character with a nutty, faintly celery-like edge.

**What does 葫芦巴 smell like?** Warm, sweet, and maple-like, with a nutty, faintly bitter edge — a gourmand, food-spice profile rather than a woodsy or resinous one. It reads sweet and comforting, closer to a flavoring than to a classical incense resin.

**How is fenugreek used in fragrance?** As a warm, sweet, gourmand accent. It is a food spice first, and in fragrance it lends a maple-like warmth rather than a resinous or floral note. It is a later crossover into the Chinese record, not a lead 香材.
