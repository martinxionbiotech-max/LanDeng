**Technical answer:** 蜀椒 — *shǔjiāo* in Chinese — is the dried fruit husk of *Zanthoxylum bungeanum*, a prickly-ash tree of the rue family (Rutaceae) native to central and western China. It is the source of the Sichuan pepper (花椒) of cooking, with a citrusy, woody aroma and the distinctive numbing sensation of hydroxy-α-sanshool. In classical Chinese incense it is the "椒" (pepper/pepper-tree) that the *Xiangcheng* (香乘) counts among the earliest aromatics of Chinese antiquity — 兰蕙椒桂 (orchid, melilot, pepper, cinnamon).

## Aroma profile

蜀椒 is spicy and citrusy with a woody undertone and a distinctive numbing, tingling quality — the trademark of the *Zanthoxylum* genus, which sets it apart from true pepper. The aroma is bright and warm rather than hot, and the numbing sensation is a mouth-feel, not a scent; in incense the husk contributes a warm, citrus-spicy lift. It is one of the oldest aromatics recorded in Chinese culture.

## The 椒 of antiquity (read this)

The single character 椒 (pepper/pepper-tree) appears at the very start of the Chinese aromatic record. The *Xiangcheng* (香乘), opening its discussion of 香品 (aromatic materials), notes that "before the Qin and Han, only 兰蕙椒桂 were spoken of" (秦漢已前未聞惟稱蘭蕙椒桂而已) — orchid, melilot, pepper, and cinnamon as the four earliest named aromatics. The 椒 in this classical formula is the pepper-tree (*Zanthoxylum*), the ancestor of today's Sichuan pepper, not the black pepper (*Piper nigrum*) that later entered China from abroad.

## Botanical source and origin

*Zanthoxylum bungeanum* is a prickly-ash tree or shrub of the rue family (Rutaceae), native to central and western China and long cultivated there. The part used is the dried fruit husk, which carries the aromatic oil and the numbing hydroxy-α-sanshool. It is the classical 蜀椒, and the broader 花椒 (Sichuan pepper) of cooking is largely this species. Note the distinction from true pepper (*Piper nigrum*), which belongs to a different family (Piperaceae).

## In Chinese tradition

- **Earliest aromatic:** the *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records that "before the Qin and Han, only 兰蕙椒桂 were spoken of" (秦漢已前未聞惟稱蘭蕙椒桂而已) — 椒 among the four earliest named aromatics of Chinese antiquity.
- **Culinary spice:** 蜀椒/花椒 is the defining spice of Sichuan cooking, prized for its citrus aroma and numbing quality.
- **Warm spice in blends:** 蜀椒's warm, citrus-spicy character lends itself to spice blends and, in classical practice, to aromatic uses alongside cinnamon and the other early aromatics.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 蜀椒 is the prickly ash (*Zanthoxylum bungeanum*), the Sichuan pepper — distinguish it from true pepper (*Piper nigrum*), a different family.
- Expect a citrusy, woody, spicy aroma with a numbing, tingling quality from hydroxy-α-sanshool.
- The husk is the aromatic part; the seeds are usually sifted out in culinary use.
