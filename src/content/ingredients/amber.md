## Aroma profile

Amber is resinous and warm, sweet and balsamic, with a smooth, rounded character. As a fossil resin it does not carry the fresh, piney note of living resin — instead it has a dry, warm sweetness that emerges when gently heated. Note the distinction between true amber (fossil resin) and "amber" as a perfumery accord: the latter is a warm, sweet, resinous *accord* built from labdanum, benzoin, and vanilla, not the fossil material. Classical Chinese 琥珀 is the fossil resin.

## A fossil resin, not a botanical (read this)

琥珀 must be understood for what it is: **fossil resin, not a botanical material.** It does not belong to the living plant kingdom in the way that frankincense or pine resin do. The classical sources understood its origin as a transformation:

- The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records that "pine resin, given a thousand years, transforms into amber" (千年松脂化為琥珀), and that the resin of the sweetgum "enters the earth and in a thousand years becomes amber" — a folk explanation of fossilization.
- The *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records amber among aromatics and ornaments — its static-electric attraction ("amber attracts mustard seeds," 琥珀拾芥) is noted as a classical observation of its property.

**This page describes 琥珀 = amber = fossil resin.** It is not a botanical, and we state that plainly rather than filing it under a living plant.

## Source and origin

Amber is fossilized tree resin, formed over millions of years from the resin of ancient trees — often conifers or, in some deposits, leguminous trees — which hardened and polymerized underground. Chinese 琥珀 was obtained from deposits and trade, and was valued both as an aromatic material and as a gemstone. Because it is a fossil product, its botanical identity is not a single living species, which is why this entry classifies it as "fossil resin, not botanical."

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 琥珀 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Classical aromatic:** the *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records amber among aromatics and ornaments, and notes its static-electric property (琥珀拾芥).
- **Folk origin:** the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records the belief that pine resin, over a thousand years, transforms into amber (千年松脂化為琥珀).

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The classical aromatic and ornament use is documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are the fossil-resin identity, the classical records, and the warm resinous character.
