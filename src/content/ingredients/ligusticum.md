**Technical answer:** Ligusticum — *gǎoběn* (藁本) in Chinese — is the dried root and rhizome of *Ligusticum sinense*, an aromatic herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) native to China. It is a classical Chinese incense ingredient with a herbal, slightly spicy, aromatic root character, best known in the incense record as one of the named ingredients of the 寿阳公主梅花香 (Shouyang princess's plum incense) formula, where it supplies a herbal middle tone alongside spikenard and angelica.

## Aroma profile

Ligusticum root is herbal and aromatic, with a slightly spicy, warming, faintly woody character typical of the carrot-family aromatic roots. It is a *middle* note — it sits in the herbal register of a blend, supporting and grounding brighter or sweeter ingredients. In classical formulas it is roasted or dried and ground, and it contributes body rather than a leading scent.

## Botanical source and origin

*Ligusticum sinense* is an herbaceous plant of the Apiaceae family, native to China. The part used is the root and rhizome, harvested, cleaned, and dried. The genus *Ligusticum* groups aromatic "lovage-like" herbs of the carrot family, several of which are used as spices and incense materials.

> **A naming caution:** The English word "lovage" is sometimes applied to 藁本, but true lovage is *Levisticum officinale*, a different plant. 藁本 is *Ligusticum sinense* — a related but distinct aromatic root. Labels using "lovage" for a Chinese incense ingredient may conflate the two.

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 藁本 is a named ingredient in classical 合香 formulas, most famously the 寿阳公主梅花香 (Shouyang princess's plum incense), recorded in the Ming-dynasty *Xiang Cheng* (香乘) with 藁本 at 半两. It supplies a herbal middle tone in the blend.
- **Herbal middle note:** in the plum-incense accord, 藁本 sits with spikenard and angelica as the herbal, slightly medicinal middle register beneath the plum and wood notes.
- **Materia medica:** 藁本 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The incense use is documented in the classical recipe record. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity and 藁本's role as a herbal aromatic in classical blended incense.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Ligusticum root should be clean and dry, with a herbal, aromatic, slightly spicy scent when warmed.
- Note that "lovage" on an English label may mean either true lovage (*Levisticum officinale*) or 藁本 (*Ligusticum sinense*) — confirm the species for incense use.
- 藁本 is a supporting herbal note, usually blended, not burned alone.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
