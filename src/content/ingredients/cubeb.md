**Technical answer:** Cubeb — *bìchéngqié* (荜澄茄) in Chinese — is the dried, unripe fruit of *Piper cubeba*, a climbing vine of the pepper family (Piperaceae) native to Java and Sumatra in Indonesia. It is a peppery spice with a sharp, slightly woody, faintly camphoraceous bite, used in Chinese incense as a warm spice accent in some 合香 (blended incense) contexts. It belongs to the same genus as black pepper and long pepper, but is a distinct spice.

## What is cubeb?

> **Direct answer:** Cubeb (荜澄茄, *bìchéngqié*) is the dried, unripe fruit of *Piper cubeba*, a climbing vine of the pepper family (Piperaceae) native to Java and Sumatra. It is a peppery spice with a faintly camphoraceous bite, dried whole with its stalk attached — the "tailed pepper."

Cubeb is a **pepper-family spice**, a distinct species from black pepper (*Piper nigrum*) and long pepper (*Piper longum*). It is gathered unripe and dried whole, with the small stalk ("tail") usually still attached — the source of the name "tailed pepper." Its defining commercial fact is a naming hazard: the Chinese materia-medica name 荜澄茄 has also been applied to *Litsea cubeba* (山鸡椒, "mountain pepper"), a laurel-family plant, so the name alone does not identify the material.

The naming task is a disambiguation:

## Aroma profile

> **Direct answer:** Cubeb is sharp and peppery, with a woody, slightly resinous, faintly camphoraceous edge — often described as a cross between black pepper and allspice, with a cooling, pine-like finish. It is a spice accent, not a base note.

Cubeb is sharp and peppery, with a woody, slightly resinous, faintly camphoraceous edge — it is often described as a cross between black pepper and allspice, with a cooling, almost pine-like finish. In the pepper family it is closely related to long pepper (*Piper longum*) and black pepper (*Piper nigrum*).
