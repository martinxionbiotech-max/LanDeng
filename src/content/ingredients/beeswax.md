> **Direct answer:** Beeswax (蜂蜡, fēnglà) is the wax secreted by honey bees (*Apis cerana* and *Apis mellifera*) to build their honeycomb. It is a bee product, not a botanical; in classical Chinese incense it is a wax — a molding, coating, and sealing material distinct from honey (蜂蜜), which serves as a binder. The *Bencao Gangmu* records both side by side.

The refinement distinction — white vs yellow — is the practical quality signal.

## Why is beeswax used in incense craft?

> **Direct answer:** Beeswax is used as a molding, coating, and sealing wax — it melts to form, hardens to hold, and coats to protect, without contributing a strong scent of its own. It is a craft material for the physical structure of incense bodies, distinct from honey's binding role.

Beeswax's role in the incense craft is physical and protective. Where honey binds a loose powder into a dough, beeswax works on the *formed* object: it can be melted and used to coat a surface (sealing a finished body against moisture and air), to mold a shape, or to fix components together. Its value is in the transition between liquid and solid — beeswax flows when warm and hardens when cool — which makes it a natural molding and sealing medium. It is the "hardware" of incense craft, complementing honey's "binder" role: honey holds a powder together, beeswax holds a form's shape and surface.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Beeswax is processed by rendering the comb and refining the wax — melting it and separating the pure wax from residue — to produce 黄蜡 (yellow wax) and, with further purification, 白蜡 (white wax). The white is the more refined, and the refinement is the quality signal.

The processing is **rendering and refining**:

1. **Rendering** — melt the honeycomb and separate the wax from the honey and the comb residue.
2. **Refining** — filter and re-melt the wax to remove impurities.
3. **Whitening / purification** — further processing yields 白蜡 (white wax), the more purified grade, from 黄蜡 (yellow wax).

There is no distillation or extraction step; beeswax *is* the wax, and the craft question is how thoroughly it has been rendered and purified — yellow is the less refined, white the more refined.

## What determines quality?

> **Direct answer:** Beeswax quality is a matter of purity and refinement: 白蜡 (white wax) is more purified than 黄蜡 (yellow wax), and a clean, well-rendered wax is preferred for molding and coating. The color is the practical quality signal.

The quality factors:

1. **Purity / refinement** — the decisive factor; 白蜡 (white) over 黄蜡 (yellow).
2. **Cleanliness** — freedom from comb residue, honey, and foreign matter.
3. **Consistency** — an even, workable melt for molding and coating.

There is no standardized grading ladder; the practical signal is color and cleanliness — the whiter and cleaner, the more refined.

## Common forms

> **Direct answer:** Beeswax is distinguished by refinement into 黄蜡 (yellow wax) and 白蜡 (white wax), the white being the purer. It is used as a solid wax that is melted for molding and coating.

## What buyers should look for

> **Direct answer:** Buyers should treat beeswax as a craft wax, not a fragrance, and specify refinement — 白蜡 (white, purified) over 黄蜡 (yellow). The practical checks are purity, cleanliness, and melt consistency.

For a buyer or maker:

- **Role** — confirm you are buying beeswax as a *wax* (molding, coating, sealing), not a fragrance and not a binder.
- **Refinement** — specify 白蜡 (white wax, purified) over 黄蜡 (yellow wax).
- **Cleanliness** — freedom from comb residue and foreign matter.
- **Melt consistency** — an even, workable melt for the intended molding or coating.

Because beeswax is a structural craft material, the buyer is specifying a *working property* (melt, mold, coat, seal), and refinement is the decisive quality variable.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

From a manufacturing perspective, beeswax is the complementary structural material to honey, and it illustrates a subtler point: **the classical incense craft had a full materials vocabulary for the physical object, not just for the scent.** Three points follow:

- **The wax is the hardware of the craft.** Honey binds the powder into a dough; beeswax molds, coats, and seals the formed object. A complete incense craft needs both — the binder for the interior, the wax for the form and surface. This is a two-material structural system, and treating beeswax as "just another ingredient" misses its specific physical role.

The buyer who judges it by scent is misreading the material; the buyer who judges it by purity and melt is reading it correctly.

In our view, the professional framing for beeswax is a **pure, well-rendered, workable wax** bought for its melt-and-mold behavior — the "hardware" of incense craft, and a reminder that the physical object and the scent are two different things, each with its own materials.
