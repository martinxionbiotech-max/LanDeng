> **Direct answer:** Paicao (排草, páicǎo) is the dried whole herb of *Lysimachia capillipes*, a small perennial of the primrose family (Primulaceae) native to southern China. It is an aromatic herb with a sweet, hay-like, faintly woody scent, used in Chinese incense as a supporting herbal note in 合香 (blended incense). It belongs to the same genus as linglingxiang (零陵香), *Lysimachia foenum-graecum*.

## Aroma profile

Paicao is herbal and sweet, with a hay-like, faintly woody character typical of the *Lysimachia* aromatic herbs — a fresh, green-sweet scent that recalls coumarin-tonka notes without being heavy. It is a *supporting* herb: it fills the herbal register of a blend, supporting and softening brighter or spicier ingredients, rather than leading with its own strong note.

## Botanical source and origin

*Lysimachia capillipes* is a small perennial herb of the primrose family (Primulaceae), native to southern China. The part used is the dried whole herb, gathered and dried for its aromatic character. The genus *Lysimachia* groups several aromatic herbs used in Chinese incense and materia medica — most famously 零陵香 (*Lysimachia foenum-graecum*), a closely related species with a similar hay-sweet profile.

> **A naming caution:** 排草 (*Lysimachia capillipes*) and 零陵香 (*Lysimachia foenum-graecum*) are closely related species of the same genus, both aromatic *Lysimachia* herbs — but they are distinct materials, and 零陵香 has its own entry. The shared sweet, hay-like character reflects their common genus, not the same plant.

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 排草 is a supporting aromatic herb in classical 合香 formulas, contributing a sweet, hay-like body beneath woods and resins.
- **Scenting herb:** the whole herb is used as a scenting material for garments and spaces, in the same tradition as other aromatic herbs.
- **Materia medica:** 排草 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 草部). This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic use is documented in classical records. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the *Lysimachia* genus placement, and the sweet, hay-like aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Paicao should be clean, dry, and fragrant with a sweet, hay-like scent when gently warmed.
- Distinguish 排草 (*Lysimachia capillipes*) from 零陵香 (*Lysimachia foenum-graecum*) — closely related but distinct herbs.
- In incense, paicao is a supporting herb, usually blended, not burned alone.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
