> **Direct answer:** Dragon's blood (血竭, xuèjié) is a bright red resin obtained from the fruit of *Daemonorops draco* (now often placed in *Calamus*), a climbing rattan palm of the Arecaceae family native to Southeast Asia. It is a resinous incense and pigment material with a sweet, woody, balsamic scent and a deep red color, used in Chinese incense, lacquer, and materia medica. The name covers several unrelated red resins.

## Aroma profile

Dragon's blood is resinous and sweet, with a woody, balsamic warmth and a faintly smoky edge when burned. It is a *resin* note in the family of frankincense and myrrh, but with a distinctive red color that has made it valuable as a pigment and a dye as much as a fragrance. It adds a warm, balsamic depth to blends.

## Botanical source and origin

*Daemonorops draco* is a climbing rattan palm of the Arecaceae family, native to Sumatra, Borneo, and the Malay Peninsula; the resin forms as a red exudate on the surface of the fruit. Botanically it has been moved to the genus *Calamus* (as *Calamus draco*), so the two names refer to the same material. The resin is scraped, gathered, and dried into red lumps or powder.

> **A naming caution:** "dragon's blood" is a shared name for several unrelated red resins — the rattan-palm resin (*Daemonorops draco*), the dragon-tree resin (*Dracaena* species), and others. In the classical incense, pigment, and Chinese materia-medica trade, 血竭 is the rattan-palm resin *Daemonorops draco*. Confirm the source species when the name appears.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Incense and pigment:** 血竭 is used as a red resin in incense and as a traditional red pigment and dye in lacquer and crafts.
- **Materia medica:** 血竭 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 木部) with various described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Incense context:** dragon's blood appears as a warm, balsamic resin note in some blends, alongside frankincense and myrrh.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The incense and pigment uses are well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the shared-name disambiguation, and the resinous, pigment use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Dragon's blood resin should be deep red, brittle, and fragrant with a sweet, balsamic scent; powder should be vivid red, not brown.
- Confirm the source species — 血竭 is the rattan-palm resin (*Daemonorops draco*), not the dragon-tree (*Dracaena*) resin that shares the "dragon's blood" name.
- In incense, dragon's blood is a warm, balsamic resin note, usually blended, not burned alone.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
