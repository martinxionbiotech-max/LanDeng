**Technical answer:** Cyperus — *xiāngfùzǐ* (香附子, "fragrant *fuzi* tuber") in Chinese — is the aromatic tuber/rhizome of *Cyperus rotundus*, the purple nutsedge, one of the world's most widespread weeds. It is a classical Chinese incense *blending* ingredient, and it serves as a traditional substitute where the rarer spikenard (甘松) is unavailable.

## Aroma profile

香附子 has a woody, spicy, earthy aroma with a slightly smoky, rooty undertone — a grounded, dry note rather than a sweet or floral one. It is a *supporting* incense material, contributing earthy body and warmth to a blend. Its character is close enough to spikenard's earthy-sweet root note that it has served as a traditional stand-in.

## A humble plant with a big role

*Cyperus rotundus* (purple nutsedge) is an ordinary-looking sedge — and one of the most widespread weeds on earth — but its small, aromatic tubers made it a standard incense and materia-medica item. The name 香附子 ("fragrant *fuzi* tuber") reflects both its scent and the tuber's resemblance to the *fuzi* (aconite) root. Classical sources record several names for it, including 莎草根 (sedge root) and 雀头香 ("sparrow-head incense," from the tuber's shape).

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 香附子 is a classical blending ingredient. It appears in standard formulas — the *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records it in the Han-dynasty 建宁宫中香 palace-incense formula alongside 藿香, 零陵香, 檀香, 白芷, 甘松, and others.
- **A traditional substitute for spikenard:** 香附子 has been used where 甘松 (spikenard) is scarce or costly — a substitution recorded in classical and trade practice.
- **Materia medica:** 香附 is a major traditional-medicine herb, classically described for "regulating qi" (理气) and related uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic role — a blending tuber and a spikenard substitute — is well documented in the classical manuals. The medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity and cyperus's long use as a common, earthy incense tuber.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 香附子 is the tuber of *Cyperus rotundus* (purple nutsedge) — a common, abundant plant, in contrast to the scarce wild spikenard it sometimes substitutes for.
- It should smell woody-spicy and earthy; the tuber is small and dense.
- In incense it is a blender, used for earthy body rather than as a solo fragrance.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**What is 香附子?** 香附子 is the aromatic tuber of *Cyperus rotundus*, the purple nutsedge — an ordinary-looking sedge that is one of the world's most widespread weeds. Its small tubers carry a woody, spicy, earthy scent and made it a standard Chinese incense *blending* ingredient, as well as a traditional-medicine herb. The name means "fragrant *fuzi* tuber," referring to its scent and the tuber's resemblance to aconite root.

**Is 香附子 the same as spikenard?** No, but it has served as a traditional substitute. 香附子 is *Cyperus rotundus* (a sedge); spikenard (甘松) is *Nardostachys jatamansi* (a Himalayan herb). Their earthy root notes are similar enough that cyperus has been used where the scarcer spikenard is unavailable or costly — a substitution recorded in classical and trade practice, not a botanical identity.

**What does cyperus smell like?** Cyperus tuber has a woody, spicy, earthy aroma with a slightly smoky, rooty undertone — a grounded, dry note rather than a sweet or floral one. It is a supporting incense material, contributing earthy body and warmth to a blend, and its character is close enough to spikenard's earthy-sweet note that it has worked as a stand-in.

**What are the other names for 香附子?** Classical sources record several names, including 香附 (the shortened form), 莎草根 ("sedge root," from the plant *Cyperus rotundus*), and 雀头香 ("sparrow-head incense," from the tuber's shape). The variety of names reflects the plant's ubiquity and its long use across regions, rather than any confusion about the plant itself.

**How is cyperus used in incense?** Cyperus is a classical 合香 (blended-incense) ingredient. The Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records it in the Han-dynasty 建宁宫中香 palace-incense formula alongside 藿香, 零陵香, 檀香, 白芷, and 甘松. It contributes an earthy, woody body and warmth — a supporting note that grounds a formula rather than leading with a bright fragrance.
