> **Direct answer:** Cyperus (香附子, xiāngfùzǐ, "fragrant tuber") is the aromatic tuber/rhizome of *Cyperus rotundus*, the purple nutsedge, one of the world's most widespread weeds. It is a classical Chinese incense blending ingredient, and serves as a traditional substitute where the rarer spikenard (甘松) is unavailable.

## Botanical identity

> **Direct answer:** *Cyperus rotundus* is a perennial sedge of the family Cyperaceae, with slender stolons bearing ellipsoidal tubers and culms 15–90 cm tall. Flora of China records it from near sea level to 2100 m across a vast global range. It is the "purple nutsedge," a notorious agricultural weed whose small aromatic tubers are the incense material.

*Cyperus rotundus* (purple nutsedge) is a perennial sedge of the Cyperaceae family. Flora of China describes slender stolons bearing ellipsoidal tubers, solitary culms 15–90 cm tall with a swollen tuberous base, and narrow bluish-green leaf blades 2–5 mm wide, flowering and fruiting May–November. It grows from near sea level to 2100 m, in grasslands, stream margins, trail edges, and paddy-field margins. The aromatic part is the small tuber — an ordinary-looking structure on an ordinary-looking weed, but the whole source of its incense and materia-medica value. See [*Cyperus rotundus* in Flora of China](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200026713) and [on GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/species/2714818).

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** 香附子 means "fragrant *fuzi* tuber," naming both its scent and the tuber's resemblance to the aconite root (*fuzi*). Classical sources record several names — 香附 (the shortened form), 莎草根 ("sedge root"), and 雀头香 ("sparrow-head incense," from the tuber's shape). The variety of names reflects the plant's ubiquity, not confusion about the plant.

## Where does cyperus come from?

Flora of China records it across most of China, and globally from Africa and Asia through Australia, Europe, and the Americas — near sea level to 2100 m. Its abundance is central to its role: it is a cheap, everywhere-available substitute, not a rarity.

## Why is it used in incense?

> **Direct answer:** Cyperus is used in incense as a classical 合香 (blended-incense) ingredient, contributing an earthy, woody body and warmth that grounds a formula. It is a blender, not a lead note, and its earthy-sweet character made it a traditional stand-in where the scarcer spikenard was unavailable.

Cyperus's role in incense is as a classical *blending* ingredient. It appears in standard formulas — the Ming *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records it in the Han-dynasty 建宁宫中香 palace-incense formula alongside 藿香, 零陵香, 檀香, 白芷, and 甘松 — where it contributes an earthy, woody body and warmth. It is a supporting note that grounds a formula rather than leading with a bright fragrance, and its earthy-sweet character close to spikenard's made it a practical substitute where spikenard was scarce or costly.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Cyperus is used as the dried tuber, harvested, cleaned, and dried whole, then used as whole tubers or ground to powder for blending. No extraction step is needed for the incense tuber; it is a dried-root material, and processing is simple.

The part used is the small aromatic tuber. Processing is simple:

- **Whole dried tubers** — cleaned and dried, for storage and slow aromatic release.
- **Powder** — ground tuber for blending into sticks and cones; particle size and moisture affect how the powder packs and burns.

## What determines cyperus quality?

> **Direct answer:** Quality turns on the tuber's condition and aroma: a good cyperus tuber is small, dense, and woody-spicy-earthy when warmed, without mustiness. Because the plant is abundant and ungraded, quality means freshness and cleanliness rather than origin or density-grade — unlike the spikenard it substitutes for.

The practical quality points, in rough order:

1. **Tuber condition and aroma** — small, dense tubers with a woody-spicy, earthy scent when warmed; musty or hollow tubers are poor.
2. **Cleanliness** — freedom from soil, root hairs, and foreign material.
3. **Freshness** — a dry, intact tuber beats a damp or decayed one.

Cyperus carries no standardized grading ladder — it is an abundant weed tuber, not a graded resin or wood — so quality means freshness and cleanliness rather than origin or a density grade.

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 香附子 is a classical blending ingredient. It appears in standard formulas — the *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records it in the Han-dynasty 建宁宫中香 palace-incense formula alongside 藿香, 零陵香, 檀香, 白芷, 甘松, and others.
- **A traditional substitute for spikenard:** 香附子 has been used where 甘松 (spikenard) is scarce or costly — a substitution recorded in classical and trade practice.
- **Materia medica:** 香附 is a major traditional-medicine herb, classically described for "regulating qi" (理气) and related uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Our Industry View

> **Direct answer:** Cyperus's commercial lesson is the economic inversion behind substitution: an abundant weed tuber stands in for a scarce high-Himalayan root. That is why cyperus matters commercially — it is cheap and everywhere — and why a buyer should know explicitly whether they are paying for cyperus or for the spikenard it imitates.

From a manufacturing perspective, cyperus is instructive because its value is **inversely related to its abundance**. It is one of the world's most widespread weeds, cheap and everywhere, yet it holds a named place in classical incense formulas because its earthy-sweet root note can stand in for the scarce, costly spikenard. Two points follow:

- **Substitution is the material's real function.** Cyperus's historical role is as a spikenard stand-in, and that substitution logic is economic, not botanical. A buyer who wants spikenard's character but accepts cyperus is making a cost decision — which is legitimate, but should be a *conscious* one. The two are different plants with related, not identical, earthy notes.
- **Abundance means consistency, not grading.** Unlike a graded resin or a scarce root, cyperus has no rarity premium and no standardized ladder. The buyer's variables are freshness and cleanliness — a steady, well-dried tuber — which makes it a forgiving, low-risk blender.

In our view, cyperus is a cheap, reliable earthy blender whose chief commercial value is as a *declared* substitute for spikenard — buy it for body and warmth, specify whether you want it or the real spikenard, and treat its abundance as a cost advantage rather than a prestige signal.
