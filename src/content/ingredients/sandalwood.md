# Sandalwood (檀香 / Tan Xiang)

**Direct answer:** Sandalwood — *tanxiang* (檀香) in Chinese — is the fragrant heartwood of *Santalum* trees, most classically Indian sandalwood (*Santalum album*). It is prized in Chinese incense for a warm, creamy, sweet, woody aroma, and has a long history in ritual, incense sticks, perfume, and carving.

## Aroma profile

Sandalwood's scent is warm, creamy, and sweet with a soft woody base. The best material has a distinctive "buttery" smoothness that distinguishes it from sharper or synthetic "sandalwood" fragrances. It is widely used as a base or blending material because its aroma is gentle and long-lasting rather than sharp.

## Species: what "sandalwood" means

"Sandalwood" is not one tree. In trade it can refer to several *Santalum* species:

- **Santalum album** — Indian / white sandalwood; the classic incense wood and the reference standard.
- **Santalum spicatum** — Australian sandalwood; a common commercial substitute.
- **Santalum austrocaledonicum** — New Caledonian sandalwood.
- **Santalum paniculatum** — Hawaiian sandalwood.

> **Buyer note:** "sandalwood" on a label does not guarantee *S. album*. Species substitution is common; the aroma and price differ meaningfully. This is a key authenticity point, not a triviality.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

Sandalwood has a long record of **traditional and ritual use** — in incense, Buddhist and Taoist practice, perfume, carving, and traditional materia medica. It is widely described as "calming" and "grounding" in aromatherapy contexts.

**Important:** much of the online content about "sandalwood incense benefits" repeats health claims — relieving insomnia, relieving anxiety, lowering cortisol, lowering blood pressure, immune support — with little or no sourced evidence. We treat these as **traditional belief or unverified claims**, not established medical fact.

- **What we can say:** sandalwood has a warm, grounding aroma used for centuries in meditation and ritual settings.
- **What we do not claim:** that burning sandalwood treats insomnia, anxiety, or any medical condition.

## In Chinese incense

Sandalwood (檀香) is a staple material in Chinese incense alongside agarwood (沉香). It is used in incense sticks (线香), in blended incense (合香), and in temple/ritual settings. Its gentle, sweet aroma makes it a common pairing or base material.

## Quality & authenticity (buyer intelligence)

- **Real sandalwood powder is expensive.** Genuine sandalwood powder can cost on the order of ~$2/gram in craft supply; very cheap "sandalwood" sticks are almost always synthetic fragrance oil, not wood.
- **Oil ≠ wood.** Sticks made with sandalwood oil smell distinctly different from those made with the actual wood; the wood gives the "buttery" note.
- **Species matters.** *S. album* is the reference standard; substitutes (Australian, New Caledonian) are legitimate but different in aroma and price.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space; avoid inhaling concentrated smoke; keep away from children and pets. See our safety guide.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between sandalwood and agarwood?** Both are prized woods, but they differ: sandalwood is the fragrant heartwood of *Santalum* trees (creamy, sweet, woody), while agarwood is resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* wood formed after injury (woody, resinous, sweet, complex). Sandalwood's scent is present in the living heartwood; agarwood's forms only through a wounding process.

**Is "sandalwood incense" real sandalwood?** Often not — many commercial sticks use synthetic fragrance. Check species and whether it lists real wood powder vs fragrance oil.

**Does sandalwood help you sleep?** There is no established clinical evidence that burning sandalwood treats insomnia. It has a traditional use as a calming ritual scent; we do not make medical claims.
