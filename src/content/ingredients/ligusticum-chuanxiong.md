> **Direct answer:** Chuanxiong (川芎, chuānxiōng) is the aromatic rhizome of *Ligusticum chuanxiong*, an herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) cultivated in Sichuan. It has a strong, herbal, spicy, slightly bitter aromatic-root character and is one of the best-known aromatic roots of Chinese materia medica. It is a different species from, and must be distinguished from, 藁本 (*Ligusticum sinense*).

## Aroma profile

川芎 has a strong, penetrating, herbal-spicy root aroma with a slightly bitter, warm edge — the pungent-sweet smell characteristic of the lovage/carrot-family roots. It is more medicinal and sharp than the softer sweet-herbal notes of angelica (当归), and it anchors a blend with an earthy, aromatic middle rather than a floral or resinous top.

## The 川芎 / 藁本 distinction (read this)

川芎 and 藁本 are **closely related but distinct roots in the same genus**:

- **川芎 (chuanxiong)** — the rhizome of *Ligusticum chuanxiong*, the Sichuan lovage. This entry.
- **藁本 (gaoben)** — the root of *Ligusticum sinense*, the Chinese lovage. Its own entry.

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之三, records them as **separate adjacent entries** — 芎藭 (with 川芎 as its Sichuan name: "出蜀中者，為川芎") and 藁本 (the next item). The two are easily confused because modern taxonomy has shifted both toward *Conioselinum anthriscoides*, but Chinese materia medica keeps them distinct. **This page describes 川芎 = *Ligusticum chuanxiong*, not 藁本.**

## Botanical source and origin

*Ligusticum chuanxiong* is an aromatic perennial of the carrot family (Apiaceae), cultivated in Sichuan as the source of 川芎. Modern botanical databases treat the name as a synonym of *Conioselinum anthriscoides*; Flora of China records the lovage group under *Ligusticum*, where 藁本 (*L. sinense*) sits alongside other species. The Sichuan name 川芎 ("Sichuan lovage") reflects its place of cultivation.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 川芎 is a major aromatic root in traditional Chinese materia medica. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Naming:** the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) explains the name 川芎 by origin: "出蜀中者，為川芎" — the one from Sichuan (蜀) is called 川芎.
- **Incense:** it is primarily a materia-medica root; its aromatic role in incense is secondary.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Apiaceae placement, and its distinction from 藁本.

## FAQ

**What is 川芎 (chuanxiong)?** The aromatic rhizome of *Ligusticum chuanxiong* (syn. *Conioselinum anthriscoides*), an herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) cultivated in Sichuan. It has a strong, herbal, spicy, slightly bitter root aroma and is a major aromatic root in Chinese materia medica.

**Is 川芎 the same as 藁本 (gaoben)?** No — closely related but distinct roots in the same lovage group. 川芎 is *Ligusticum chuanxiong* (Sichuan lovage); 藁本 is *Ligusticum sinense* (Chinese lovage). The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) lists them as separate adjacent entries, and Chinese materia medica keeps them distinct even though modern taxonomy shifts both toward *Conioselinum anthriscoides*.

**Why is it called 川芎 (Sichuan lovage)?** The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) explains: "出蜀中者，為川芎" — the one produced in Sichuan (蜀) is called 川芎. The name records its place of cultivation, distinguishing the Sichuan root from lovage grown elsewhere.

**What does 川芎 smell like?** Strong, penetrating, herbal-spicy, and slightly bitter, with a warm edge — the pungent-sweet character of the lovage/carrot-family roots. It is more medicinal and sharp than the softer, sweeter angelica (当归) note, and it anchors a blend with an earthy, aromatic middle.

**Is chuanxiong an incense ingredient?** Primarily a materia-medica root. Its strong aromatic character can lend an earthy, herbal middle to a blend, but its classical role is in materia medica rather than as a lead 香材. It is documented separately from the related 藁本, which has its own incense formula appearances.
