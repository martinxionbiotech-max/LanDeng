> **Direct answer:** Schisandra (五味子, wǔwèizǐ) is the dried berry of *Schisandra chinensis*, a woody climbing vine of the family Schisandraceae native to China and the Russian Far East. Its name means "five-flavor berry," from the berry's combined sour, sweet, salty, bitter, and pungent tastes. It is a classical Chinese materia-medica fruit with a faint, fruity, sour-berry scent, not a lead incense 香材.

## Aroma profile

五味子 has a soft, fruity, sour-berry scent with a faint woody, tea-like undertone — the tart-sweet aroma of a dried red berry rather than a flower. Its fragrance is quiet and is not a driving note in incense; the berry's fame rests on its "five tastes" in materia medica, not on a strong scent.

## The 五味 (five-taste) naming (read this)

The name 五味子 is literal and matters for identification:

- The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之七, records the explanation: the berry's skin and flesh are sweet and sour, its seed is pungent and bitter, and all of it carries a salty note — "皮、肉甘、酸，核中辛、苦，都有鹹味" — so the five tastes are all present in one fruit.
- Li Shizhen records a **north–south division**: southern berries ripen red (南產者，色紅), northern berries ripen black (北產者，色黑), and the northern ones were preferred for tonic materia-medica use.
- The classical aliases include 荎蕏, 玄及, and 會及.

## Botanical source and origin

*Schisandra chinensis* is a deciduous woody vine in the family Schisandraceae, native to China, Korea, and the Russian Far East, where it climbs trees and shrubs. Flora of China records the species (五味子). The part used is the dried ripe berry, harvested in late summer and dried whole. The genus *Schisandra* holds several Chinese species, but *S. chinensis* is the classical 五味子.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 五味子 is a classical materia-medica fruit, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之七. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Naming:** the "five-flavor" name records the berry's combined tastes, and the classical north–south grading (red southern vs black northern) was a sourcing distinction.
- **Not a lead incense material:** the berry's fragrance is faint; its classical role is materia medica, not as a driving incense 香材.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Schisandraceae placement, and the five-flavor naming.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 五味子 is the dried berry of *Schisandra chinensis*; expect a soft, fruity, sour-berry scent and the combined five tastes.
- Classical grading distinguished northern (black, preferred for tonics) from southern (red) berries; specify the berry, not the vine or leaf, when sourcing.
- In incense it is a quiet fruity accent at most, not a lead note; its classical standing is in materia medica.
