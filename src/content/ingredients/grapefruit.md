**Direct answer:** Grapefruit — *pútáoyòu* (葡萄柚) in Chinese — is the fruit and rind of *Citrus × paradisi*, a modern cultivated citrus hybrid in the rue family (Rutaceae). Its peel carries a fresh, bittersweet, green-slightly-woody citrus scent from its essential oil, and it is a **modern essential-oil fragrance material, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient**. It must be kept distinct from the pomelo (柚, *Citrus maxima*), its larger ancestral parent, which has its own entry as 柚皮 (pomelo peel).

## Aroma profile

葡萄柚 (grapefruit) is fresh, bright, and bittersweet, with a green, slightly woody edge that distinguishes it from the cleaner sweetness of lemon or orange. It is a top note with more body than lemon — the bittersweet, slightly sulfurous green character comes from the compound **nootkatone** and the mercaptan compounds in the rind oil.

## A provenance note (read this)

葡萄柚 (grapefruit) is a **modern essential-oil material**, not a classical Chinese 香材:

- *Citrus × paradisi* is a recent cultivated hybrid — botanically a pomelo (柚, *Citrus maxima*) crossed with the sweet orange (*Citrus sinensis*) — first described from the Caribbean in the 18th century. Flora of China does not record it as a native species.
- The Chinese name 葡萄柚 is modern, a literal rendering of "grapefruit"; the grapefruit as a fragrance note is a **modern essential-oil and Western-perfumery tradition**, absent from the classical 香材 canon.
- It is **not** the same as 柚皮 (pomelo peel, *Citrus maxima*) — its larger, older ancestor, which has its own entry.

This page records it honestly as a modern citrus essential oil — **no classical Chinese origin is claimed, and none is fabricated.**

## The grapefruit / pomelo distinction (read this)

葡萄柚 and 柚 are closely related but distinct:

- **葡萄柚 (grapefruit)** — *Citrus × paradisi*, a small modern hybrid (pomelo × sweet orange), used for essential oil. This entry.
- **柚 / 柚皮 (pomelo / pomelo peel)** — *Citrus maxima*, the largest citrus, a native Southeast Asian species long grown in southern China; its peel is a traditional citrus note.

The grapefruit is a modern descendant of the pomelo, but they are different materials, different sizes, and different Chinese identities. Specify the species when sourcing.

## Botanical source and origin

*Citrus × paradisi* is a small evergreen tree of the rue family (Rutaceae), a cultivated hybrid of the pomelo (*Citrus maxima*) and the sweet orange. It arose in the West Indies in the 18th century and is now grown worldwide. GBIF records the species; it is a modern crop with no native Chinese wild population. The fragrant part is the rind oil.

## In modern use

- **Essential oil:** grapefruit oil, cold-pressed from the peel, is a fresh bittersweet top note in perfumery, home fragrance, and aromatherapy.
- **Home fragrance:** grapefruit scents appear widely in modern candles, diffusers, and room sprays.
- **Not classical Chinese incense:** grapefruit has no place in the classical 香材 canon; its use is modern essential-oil perfumery.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

There is **no classical Chinese incense record** for 葡萄柚 (grapefruit); its position is entirely modern. We make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Rutaceae placement, and its modern essential-oil role.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 葡萄柚 (grapefruit) here is *Citrus × paradisi*; expect a fresh, bittersweet, green-slightly-woody rind scent from cold-pressed peel oil.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. Citrus oils can be skin-irritating at high concentration; use as directed. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**What is 葡萄柚 (grapefruit)?** The fruit and rind of *Citrus × paradisi*, a modern cultivated citrus hybrid in the rue family (Rutaceae), a cross of the pomelo (柚) and the sweet orange. Its peel carries a fresh, bittersweet, green-slightly-woody citrus scent from its essential oil. It is a modern essential-oil fragrance material, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient.

**Is grapefruit a classical Chinese incense ingredient?** No. Grapefruit is a recent hybrid first described from the Caribbean in the 18th century, and it has no place in the classical 香材 canon. Its Chinese name 葡萄柚 is modern, and its fragrance use is a modern essential-oil tradition. The classical Chinese citrus fragrance fruits are citron, finger citron, and pomelo peel.

**How is grapefruit different from pomelo (柚)?** They are closely related but distinct. 葡萄柚 is *Citrus × paradisi*, a small modern hybrid (pomelo × sweet orange) grown mainly for juice and essential oil. 柚 is *Citrus maxima*, the largest citrus, native to Southeast Asia and long grown in southern China; its peel (柚皮) is a traditional citrus note. Grapefruit is a modern descendant of the pomelo.

**What does grapefruit smell like?** Fresh, bright, and bittersweet, with a green, slightly woody edge that distinguishes it from the cleaner sweetness of lemon or orange. Its character comes from the rind oil, including nootkatone and trace sulfur compounds, giving it more body than lemon.

**How is grapefruit used in fragrance?** As a fresh bittersweet top note from its cold-pressed essential oil, used in perfumery, home fragrance, and aromatherapy. It is a modern essential-oil material, not a burned classical incense ingredient, and it is usually blended rather than burned alone.
