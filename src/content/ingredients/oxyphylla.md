> **Direct answer:** Oxyphylla (益智仁, yìzhì rén) is the dried fruit of *Alpinia oxyphylla*, a ginger-family (Zingiberaceae) perennial native to southern China, Hainan, and northern Vietnam. It is a warm, spicy, camphoraceous seed-like fruit used in traditional medicine and recorded as a minor aromatic in classical incense formulas — the Ming *Xiangcheng* (香乘) lists 益智仁 among the ingredients of the inner-court sachet 内苑蕊心衣香.

## Aroma profile

益智仁 is warm and spicy with a camphoraceous, slightly sweet edge — in the ginger-family register alongside galangal and the cardamoms, but with a gentler, more rounded pungency. It reads as a *warm spice accent*, adding a soft, pungent warmth to a blend rather than leading it. Its primary place is traditional medicine, where it is valued more for its described properties than for its scent.

## Botanical source and origin

*Alpinia oxyphylla* is a ginger-family (Zingiberaceae) perennial native to southern China, Hainan, and northern Vietnam. The part used is the dried fruit, which contains the aromatic seed. The genus *Alpinia* includes other aromatic species — galangal (*Alpinia officinarum*) and katsumadai (*Alpinia katsumadae*) — and the wider ginger family includes the cardamom group (*Amomum*) and turmeric group (*Curcuma*).

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 益智仁 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine, classically described as a warming aromatic. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Classical sachet:** the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) lists 益智仁 among the ingredients of the inner-court sachet 内苑蕊心衣香 (藿香、益智仁、白芷…) — a wearable sachet, not a burned material.
- **Minor spice:** 益智仁 is a lesser-used warm spice in Chinese cooking and spiced blends.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The traditional-medicine and minor aromatic use is documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Zingiberaceae placement, and the warm-spice character.

Practical points for buyers:

- Expect a warm, spicy, camphoraceous, slightly sweet character, gentler than galangal's sharp pungency.
