**Technical answer:** 郁金 — *yùjīn* in Chinese — is the dried rhizome of *Curcuma aromatica*, a ginger-family (Zingiberaceae) perennial native to South and Southeast Asia and cultivated in southern China. It is a warm, camphoraceous, earthy rhizome sometimes called "wild turmeric" in English, and its name (郁金) literally means "aromatic gold." It is one of three closely related *Curcuma* rhizomes — 姜黄 (turmeric), 郁金 (aromatic turmeric), and 莪术 (zedoary) — that share overlapping Chinese names and are routinely confused, and this page disambiguates them explicitly.

## Aroma profile

郁金 is earthy and camphoraceous, warm with a slightly bitter edge — it sits close to turmeric but is less brightly earthy and more medicinal-camphoraceous in character. Like the other *Curcuma* rhizomes, its scent is grounded and warm, a *base accent* in the ginger-family register. Its rhizome, like turmeric's, carries aromatic compounds and, in the wild-type form, a milder color than the vivid yellow-orange of culinary turmeric.

## The 郁金 / 姜黄 / 莪术 confusion (read this)

Three closely related *Curcuma* rhizomes share overlapping Chinese names and are routinely confused, even in some classical texts. This encyclopedia distinguishes them by species:

- **姜黄** (*Curcuma longa*) — "turmeric," the yellow-orange culinary rhizome and coloring material. Its own entry.
- **郁金** (*Curcuma aromatica*) — a distinct rhizome used in traditional medicine, sometimes called "wild turmeric"; its name (郁金) literally means "aromatic gold." This entry.
- **莪术** (*Curcuma zedoaria* / *Curcuma phaeocaulis*) — another distinct medicinal rhizome in the zedoary group. Its own entry.

The confusion arises because all three are *Curcuma* rhizomes with warm, earthy, camphoraceous characters, and historical sources have not always kept them apart. **This page describes 郁金 = *Curcuma aromatica* = aromatic (wild) turmeric.** When a source says 姜黄 or 莪术, it is describing a different material.

## Botanical source and origin

*Curcuma aromatica* is a rhizomatous perennial of the ginger family (Zingiberaceae), native to South and Southeast Asia and cultivated in southern China. The part used is the dried rhizome. The genus *Curcuma* also includes 姜黄 (*Curcuma longa*) and the zedoary group (*Curcuma zedoaria* / *C. phaeocaulis*), and the ginger family more broadly includes 高良姜 (galangal) and 山柰 (sand ginger).

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 郁金 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine, classically described as a warming aromatic. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Classical aromatic:** the name 郁金 appears in classical fragrance texts — the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) records 郁金油 (aromatic-turmeric oil) and 郁金香, and notes that "in India people anoint the body with various aromatics, namely sandalwood and 郁金" (印度之人身塗諸香所謂旃檀鬱金也), an imported aromatic-oil use.
- **Not a culinary turmeric:** unlike 姜黄, 郁金 is not the standard culinary turmeric, though the two are closely related.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic and traditional-medicine use is documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the *Curcuma* genus placement, and the aromatic-rhizome use in the turmeric group.
