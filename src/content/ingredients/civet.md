> **Direct answer:** Civet (灵猫香, língmāoxiāng) is the glandular secretion of the civet, a small carnivore of the family Viverridae, most historically from the African civet and the small Indian civet. In classical Chinese incense and perfumery it was used as a fixative with an animalic, musky, sweet-warm scent. Like musk, it is animal-derived, not botanical, and modern perfumery has replaced it with synthetic civetone.

## Aroma profile

> **Direct answer:** Natural civet is animalic and musky at its base — sometimes described as fecal or leathery when raw — with a sweetness that develops and rounds as it ages and is diluted. It is a fixative first: it anchors a blend from below rather than leading with a bright note, the same structural role musk plays.

Natural civet is animalic and musky at its base — sometimes described as fecal or leathery when raw — with a sweetness that develops and rounds as it ages and is diluted. Its function is that of a **fixative**: it does not lead a blend but anchors it from below, adding warmth, depth, and lasting power. This is the same role musk plays, which is why the two are often discussed together as the classical animal fixatives. The raw-versus-matured contrast is central: fresh civet secretion is sharply animalic, even fecal, while the *matured and diluted* material reads as warmth and roundness — a parallel to musk's own raw-versus-aged paradox.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** 灵猫香 means "civet-cat fragrance" (灵猫 = civet/civet-cat, 香 = fragrance), naming the animal source directly. As with musk, the word's modern fate is that "civet" in fragrance now names a scent descriptor carried by synthetic civetone, not a claim of animal origin.

The name 灵猫香 (língmāoxiāng) is precise in the same way 麝香 (musk) is: it names the animal source (灵猫, the civet) plus fragrance (香). This directness is exactly what modern usage has dissolved. In contemporary fragrance, "civet" names a *scent family* — animalic, musky, sweet-warm — now carried almost entirely by synthetic civetone (the principal odor compound of natural civet) and by musky substitutes. The word outlived the animal, as "musk" did: a modern "civet" perfume is, in the overwhelming majority of cases, a synthetic accord. For a buyer, the terminology point is the same as for musk — **"civet" on a label is a scent descriptor, not a provenance claim.**

## The animal-musk family (read this)

> **Direct answer:** Civet is one of three classical animal fixatives — musk (麝香), civet (灵猫香), and castoreum (海狸香). All three are animal secretions prized for warm, animalic fixative power; all are CITES-restricted or otherwise protected; and all are now overwhelmingly replaced by synthetics. Civet is the sharpest and most fecal of the three; musk is the sweetest and most skin-like; castoreum is the smokiest and most leathery.

Civet sits in a small family of classical animal fixatives, and the family itself is worth understanding — the "animal三兄弟" of traditional perfumery and incense:

- **麝香 (musk)** — *Moschus* deer pod secretion; warm, sweet, skin-like, the most precious and most restricted. See [musk](/ingredients/musk/).
- **灵猫香 (civet)** — *Viverra*/civet perineal gland secretion; sharper, more fecal-animalic, rounded by extreme dilution. This entry.
- **海狸香 (castoreum)** — *Castor* beaver castor sac secretion; leathery, smoky, birch-tar-like. See [castoreum](/ingredients/castoreum/).

The three share an animalic fixative register — warm, tenacious, "rounded" — but are different animals, different secretions, and different characters. Musk is the sweetest and most skin-like; civet is the sharpest and most fecal; castoreum is the smokiest and most leathery. In the classical canon, musk was the Chinese court's animal accent of choice, while civet and castoreum are more prominent in other perfumery traditions — a reminder that "animal fixative" is a family, not a single material. Understanding civet's place in this trio is the fastest way to place its character precisely.

## Where does civet come from?

> **Direct answer:** Civet comes from civets of the family Viverridae — the African civet (*Civettictis civetta*) across sub-Saharan Africa and the small Indian civet (*Viverricula indica*) across South and Southeast Asia. Historically the secretion was collected from both wild and captive animals, the latter raising the welfare concerns noted here.

The geographic sources are two species across different ranges: the African civet (*Civettictis civetta*) of sub-Saharan Africa, and the small Indian civet (*Viverricula indica*) of South and Southeast Asia. Historically, the secretion was collected both from wild animals and from animals kept in captivity — the captivity practice being the welfare concern that is now historical. The two-species, two-region sourcing is part of why civet's CITES status is split across the two species (India and Botswana listings respectively).

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Historically, civet was processed by collecting the perineal gland secretion and aging/diluting it — the raw, sharply animalic secretion being matured into the rounded, warm fixative. This is a historical process now obsolete; modern "civet" is synthetic civetone, produced without any animal.

The historical processing is a maturation-and-dilution step: the raw secretion, sharply animalic (even fecal), is aged and diluted until it reads as warmth and roundness rather than as a raw animal note. This is the same paradox as musk — the material's value emerges only after processing transforms the raw secretion. The process is now obsolete, because the animal source is restricted and the welfare history is unacceptable; modern perfumery produces civetone synthetically, reproducing the matured scent without the animal.

From a manufacturing and sourcing perspective, civet extends the fixative logic the musk page sets out, with its own sharp twist. Three points follow:

This is a character point, not a value judgment: civet's register is the "high-pitched" animalic note in the family, and a formulator reaches for it for that edge, not for musk's softness.

**How does civet relate to musk?** Both are classical animal fixatives: musk is the secretion of the male musk deer (genus *Moschus*), and civet is the glandular secretion of civets (*Civettictis civetta* / *Viverricula indica*). **How does civet differ from castoreum?** Both are animal fixatives, but different animals and characters.
