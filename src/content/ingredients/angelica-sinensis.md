**Technical answer:** 当归 — *dāngguī* in Chinese — is dong quai, the dried root of *Angelica sinensis*, a herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) native to China. It is one of the most famous roots in Chinese materia medica, described in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) as "the essential herb for women" (女人要药), with a warm, sweet, herbal scent and the classical name 乾归 (qiánguī). **It is a different species from 白芷 (*Angelica dahurica*)** — same genus, different plant, different role.

## Aroma profile

当归 has a warm, sweet, herbal scent with a slightly spicy, rooty undertone — dry and earthy, more like a seasoned root than a flower. It is less sharp than its close relative 白芷 (*Angelica dahurica*), and its sweetness is fuller. As an aromatic it contributes a warm, grounding root note rather than a bright top note, which suits its traditional place in 香囊 (scented sachets) and blended preparations.

## The 当归 / 白芷 distinction (read this)

当归 and 白芷 are **same genus, different species** — and the difference matters:

- **当归 (dong quai)** — *Angelica sinensis*. The "women's herb" of materia medica; a warm, sweet root. This entry.
- **白芷 (angelica)** — *Angelica dahurica*. The aromatic root used in 合香 (blended incense). Its own entry.

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), herb section (草之三), records 当归 with the classical names 乾归, 山蕲, and 文无, and quotes the *Bencao Jingshu* tradition calling it 女人要药 ("the essential herb for women"). **This page describes 当归 = *Angelica sinensis*, not 白芷 = *Angelica dahurica*.**

## Botanical source and origin

*Angelica sinensis* is a perennial herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae), native to China and cultivated chiefly in Gansu, Sichuan, and Yunnan. The material of value is the dried root, which is aromatic and rich in the compounds that give 当归 its warm, sweet scent and its place in materia medica. Flora of China records the species; it is not the same plant as the European garden angelica (*Angelica archangelica*) nor 白芷.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 当归 is one of the best-known roots in Chinese medicine, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) as 女人要药. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **香囊 (scented sachet):** 当归 is among the aromatic roots placed in 香囊 — small cloth sachets of fragrant herbs worn or hung for their scent.
- **Naming:** the name 当归 ("ought to return") carries a classical story of a wife awaiting her husband's return, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu*.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is extensive but **traditional**, not established clinical fact; we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Apiaceae placement, and the root's aromatic and sachet use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 当归 is dong quai, the dried root of *Angelica sinensis*; expect a warm, sweet, herbal scent.
- Do not confuse it with 白芷 (*Angelica dahurica*) — same genus, different species with a different aroma and role.
- It is a materia-medica root and sachet herb, not a burned incense material and not a classical 香乘 staple.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
