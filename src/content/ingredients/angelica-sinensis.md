> **Direct answer:** Dong quai (当归, dāngguī) is the dried root of *Angelica sinensis*, a herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) native to China. One of the most famous roots in Chinese materia medica, the *Bencao Gangmu* calls it "the essential herb for women" (女人要药). It is a different species from 白芷 (*Angelica dahurica*) — same genus, different plant, different role.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** The name 当归 literally means "ought to return," and it carries a classical story recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* of a wife awaiting her husband's return. The *Bencao Gangmu* also records the classical aliases 乾归, 山蕲, and 文无 — multiple names for one root, a pattern that signals how central the herb was to the materia-medica tradition.

The name 当归 (dāngguī) is a phrase, not a descriptive compound: 当 "ought to" + 归 "return." The *Bencao Gangmu* records the classical story behind it — a wife awaiting her husband's return — which is why the root's name reads as a proverb rather than a botanical description. The same text records three classical aliases — 乾归, 山蕲, and 文无 — a cluster of names that signals the herb's long and central place in the materia-medica canon.
