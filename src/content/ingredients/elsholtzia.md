**Technical answer:** 香薷 — *xiāngrú* in Chinese — is a leafy aromatic herb of the mint family (Lamiaceae), chiefly *Elsholtzia ciliata* (with related *Elsholtzia* species also used). It is a classical Chinese aromatic-medicinal herb, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) under its aromatic names 香柔 and 香茸 ("fragrant-soft," "fragrant-downy"), and it appears among the aromatics listed in the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘). Its character is a fresh, minty-spicy green note.

## Aroma profile

香薷 is minty and herbal, with a spicy, faintly sweet, camphor-adjacent lift — a fresh green note close to mint and perilla but with its own softer, warmer cast. It is not as sharply cooling as mint, nor as anise-leaning as perilla. In incense it reads as a *clean green aromatic* that freshens a blend, a bright herbal top or mid note in the mint-family register.

## The 香薷 of the aromatic record (read this)

The name itself is an aroma: the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), in its fragrant-herb section (草之三), records 香薷 with the alternative names 香柔, 香茸, and 蜜蜂草, and explains:

> "時珍曰：薷，本作葇。……其氣香，其葉柔，故以名之。" — the character 薷 is originally 葇; its smell is fragrant and its leaves soft, hence the name.

The herb was moved by Li Shizhen from the vegetable section into the fragrant-herb (芳草) section, a correction that itself signals its standing as an aromatic rather than a mere potherb. The Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) also lists 香薷 among aromatics, confirming its place in the classical Chinese fragrance vocabulary.

## Botanical source and origin

*Elsholtzia ciliata* is an annual herb of the mint family, native to East Asia and long used across China, Korea, Japan, and adjacent regions; related *Elsholtzia* species (such as *E. splendens*) are also gathered. The part used is the aerial herb — leaves and flowering tops — which carry the volatile oils (rich in elsholtzia ketone and related compounds in some species) that give the fresh, minty-spicy scent. Flora of China records the species.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Aromatic herb:** 香薷 is a classical aromatic herb, grouped by the *Bencao Gangmu* among the 芳草 (fragrant herbs).
- **Materia medica:** 香薷 appears in traditional Chinese medicine. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **香乘 aromatic:** the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) lists 香薷 among aromatics, placing it in the classical fragrance vocabulary.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic classification and culinary use are documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Lamiaceae placement, and the classical aromatic record.

## FAQ

**What is 香薷 (elsholtzia)?** A leafy aromatic herb of the mint family (Lamiaceae), chiefly *Elsholtzia ciliata*, native to East Asia. It is a classical Chinese aromatic-medicinal herb, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) as 香薷 (also 香柔, 香茸), and listed among aromatics in the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘).

**What does the name 香薷 mean?** It means "fragrant-soft." The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records that the character 薷 is originally 葇, and that "its smell is fragrant and its leaves soft, hence the name" (其氣香，其葉柔，故以名之). Li Shizhen moved the herb into the fragrant-herb (芳草) section, signaling its standing as an aromatic.

**What does elsholtzia smell like?** Minty and herbal, with a spicy, faintly sweet, camphor-adjacent lift. It is a fresh green note close to mint and perilla but softer and warmer, and in incense it reads as a clean green aromatic that freshens a blend.

**Is 香薷 used in classical Chinese incense?** It appears among the aromatics listed in the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘), and the *Bencao Gangmu* groups it with the 芳草 (fragrant herbs). Its documented role is as an aromatic herb, not as a self-sufficient burned incense material.

**Is elsholtzia the same as mint or perilla?** No — they are related Lamiaceae herbs with distinct profiles. Elsholtzia (香薷) is minty-spicy and slightly sweet; mint (薄荷, *Mentha*) is sharply cooling; perilla (紫苏, *Perilla frutescens*) is more anise-like with a shiso character. Different genera, overlapping green-aromatic families.

**What part of the plant is used?** The aerial herb — leaves and flowering tops — which carry the volatile oils responsible for the fresh, minty-spicy scent. The whole aromatic herb is the material of use, whether fresh or dried.
