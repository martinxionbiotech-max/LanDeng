> **Direct answer:** Mastic (玛蹄脂, mǎtízhī) is the aromatic resin of *Pistacia lentiscus*, the mastic tree, a small evergreen shrub of the cashew family (Anacardiaceae) native to the Mediterranean, most famously cultivated on the Greek island of Chios. It is a fresh, piney, balsamic resin used since antiquity in incense, perfumery, and as a chewing gum. Its Chinese name 玛蹄脂 is also spelled 乳香脂 or 洋乳香, inviting confusion with frankincense (乳香).

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Mastic is a tapped resin: incisions are made in the bark of *Pistacia lentiscus*, and the exuding resin hardens into pale, translucent tears, which are then cleaned and sorted. The Chios method involves repeated scoring and collection, and the best material is pale and clean.

Mastic is obtained by *tapping*: incisions are made in the bark of the shrub, the resin exudes, and it hardens into pale, translucent tears, which are then cleaned and sorted by color and clarity. The traditional Chios method involves repeated scoring of the bark and collection of the hardened tears over a season, and the finest material is pale, clean, and translucent. Quality mastic is therefore a sorting and cleaning question as much as a tapping one — pale, clean tears are the good grade, and dark or stale resin is lower.

## What determines mastic quality?

> **Direct answer:** Quality is determined by color, clarity, and freshness: pale, clean, translucent tears with a fresh, piney-lemon scent are the good grade, while dark or stale resin is lower. Provenance (Chios, with its PDO status) is the strongest origin signal. The mastic/frankincense distinction is the identity check.

The quality factors, in rough order:

1. **Color and clarity** — pale, clean, translucent tears are the good grade; dark or stale resin is lower.
2. **Aroma** — fresh, piney, with a hint of lemon and cedar; a clean balsamic sweetness beats a flat or musty one.
3. **Provenance** — Chios mastic carries PDO status, the strongest origin signal available for this resin.
4. **Identity** — *Pistacia lentiscus*, not frankincense (*Boswellia*); different families, and the Chinese names overlap.

There is no single standardized grading ladder, but "pale, clean, fresh, and correctly identified" is the durable signal.
