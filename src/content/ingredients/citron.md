> **Direct answer:** Citron (香橼, xiāngyuán) is *Citrus medica*, one of the oldest cultivated citrus, in the rue family (Rutaceae). It is a thick-rinded, intensely fragrant fruit grown for its aromatic rind rather than its juice, used in Chinese culture as a 清供 (scholar's-table offering) and room-fragrance fruit. It is the parent species of the finger citron (佛手), and the two must be kept distinct.

## Aroma profile

香橼 is citrus and floral, with a sweet, lemony, slightly bitter rind scent that is more perfumed and complex than an orange, and more reserved than the finger citron's exuberant floral lift. The fragrance lives in the thick, oil-rich rind, not the scant juice. In a room it reads as a *clear, elegant citrus*, experienced by proximity and evaporation rather than by burning.

## The 香橼 / 佛手 relationship (read this)

香橼 and 佛手 are the same species in different forms:

- **香橼 (citron)** — *Citrus medica*, the parent species, a fragrant, thick-rinded, lemon-like fruit. This entry.
- **佛手 (finger citron)** — *Citrus medica* var. *sarcodactylis*, a variety whose fruit splits into finger-like segments. Its own entry.

Both are grown for fragrant rind rather than juice, and both serve as 清供 and room-fragrance fruits. **This page describes 香橼 = *Citrus medica*.** When a source means the fingered form, it is describing 佛手.

## Botanical source and origin

*Citrus medica* is one of the most ancient cultivated citrus, native to the foothills of the Himalaya and long grown across China, Southeast Asia, and the Mediterranean. Its fruit is large, thick-rinded, and intensely aromatic, with relatively little pulp. Flora of China records the species (including the fingered form *sarcodactylis*); the citron is also the ancestor — alongside pomelo and mandarin — of much of the cultivated citrus family.

## In Chinese tradition

- **清供 (scholar's-table offering):** 香橼 is a classic 清供 fruit, displayed for its fragrance and auspicious form.
- **Room fragrance:** its oil-rich rind scents a room by evaporation — a fragrance of presence, not combustion.
- **Not a 香乘 incense ingredient:** 香橼 does not appear in the classical incense compendium 香乘; its role is as a displayed fragrance fruit, not a burned incense material.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The 清供 and room-fragrance use is well documented. We make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Rutaceae placement, and the fragrant offering-fruit use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 香橼 is the citron (*Citrus medica*); expect a sweet, floral, slightly bitter rind scent from the thick, oil-rich skin, not the scant juice.
- It is a fragrance fruit for display and room scent, not a burned incense material, and is not a 香乘 ingredient.
- Distinguish it from 佛手 (*Citrus medica* var. *sarcodactylis*) — same species, plain fruit vs fingered form.

**Is 香橼 the same as 佛手 (finger citron)?** They are the same species in different forms. 香橼 is the plain citron (*Citrus medica*), and 佛手 is its fingered variety (*Citrus medica* var. *sarcodactylis*). **Is the citron eaten?** Its fruit flesh is scant and not the point — the citron is grown for its thick, fragrant rind, which may be candied or used to perfume spaces, and the whole fruit is displayed for scent and symbolism.
