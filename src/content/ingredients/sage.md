> **Direct answer:** Sage (鼠尾草, shǔwěicǎo) is the culinary sage, *Salvia officinalis*, a mint-family (Lamiaceae) subshrub native to the Mediterranean. Its leaves carry a savory, herbal, camphoraceous, slightly peppery scent, familiar from cooking and, more recently, modern incense and cleansing rituals. It is a modern crossover, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** 鼠尾草 ("mouse-tail herb") is a broad Chinese name, not a species. It covers the whole *Salvia* genus — including native Chinese species such as *Salvia japonica* and the medicinal 丹参 (*Salvia miltiorrhiza*) — while "sage" in the culinary and fragrance sense is specifically the Mediterranean *Salvia officinalis*.

The name 鼠尾草 is a *genus-level* Chinese term, not a species label. It translates the shape of the flower spike ("mouse-tail") and applies across the entire *Salvia* genus, which includes numerous native Chinese species — among them the medicinally important 丹参 (*Salvia miltiorrhiza*). This is a genuine naming collision, not a minor overlap: a Chinese reader encountering 鼠尾草 in a materia-medica context is likely meeting a native *Salvia*, while "sage" in Western culinary and fragrance use means *Salvia officinalis* specifically. For a buyer, the terminology rule is simple — **specify the species**, because 鼠尾草 alone does not identify which *Salvia* is meant.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Sage is used fresh or dried, and for incense and cleansing it is typically dried and burned as a leaf or a tied bundle. Drying concentrates the savory, camphoraceous character; no distillation or refinement is required for the leaf itself.

Sage leaves are used fresh in cooking or dried for storage and incense use. For smoke-cleansing and incense, the leaf is dried and burned directly — often as a tied bundle — releasing the savory, camphoraceous smoke. Drying concentrates the character, and the material requires no further processing. Sage essential oil (steam-distilled from the leaves) is a separate product for aromatherapy, distinct from the burned leaf.
