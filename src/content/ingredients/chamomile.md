> **Direct answer:** Chamomile (洋甘菊, yánggānjú) is the German chamomile, *Matricaria chamomilla* (syn. *Matricaria recutita*), a daisy-family (Asteraceae) annual native to Europe and western Asia. Its flowers carry a soft, sweet, apple-like, hay-like scent, familiar from herbal tea and modern aromatherapy. It is a modern crossover, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient.

## The "洋" (foreign) prefix (read this)

> **Direct answer:** The 洋 (yáng, "foreign") prefix in 洋甘菊 is a systematic Chinese botanical convention for marking non-native plants: 洋甘菊 means "foreign sweet-chrysanthemum," explicitly distinguishing the European chamomile from the native 菊花 (chrysanthemum). The prefix is the provenance marker.

The name 洋甘菊 (yánggānjú) is a *prefixed* foreign name, and the prefix is systematic:

- **洋 (yáng)** — "foreign / oceanic," the prefix Chinese botany uses to mark plants of foreign origin.
- **甘菊 (gānjú)** — "sweet chrysanthemum," linking chamomile to the native chrysanthemum family.

So 洋甘菊 means literally **"foreign sweet-chrysanthemum"** — a name that both borrows the native chrysanthemum's flower (菊花, a classical Chinese flower) and flags the chamomile as non-native via the 洋 prefix. This is the third foreign-naming pattern in this encyclopedia: descriptive (薰衣草, "clothes-scenting herb"), phonetic (依兰, sound-borrowed), and prefixed (洋甘菊, "foreign X"). The prefix is a clear, honest provenance marker — the plant is foreign, and the name says so.

## Botanical source and origin

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Chamomile's fragrant material is both the dried flower head (for tea and some incense) and the distilled essential oil (for aromatherapy). The flowers are dried whole; the oil is steam-distilled. The dried flower is the simpler, milder product.

The processing is a **flower-drying / oil-distillation** split:

1. **Drying** — the flower heads are dried whole, for tea and sachets.
2. **Distillation** — chamomile essential oil is steam-distilled from the flowers for aromatherapy.
3. **Use** — the dried flower for tea and home fragrance, the oil for aromatherapy and fragrance.

The two products — dried flower and essential oil — have different uses; the oil is the concentrated material, the dried flower the milder, simpler form.
