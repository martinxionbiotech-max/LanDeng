**Technical answer:** Champaca — *báilánhuā* (白兰花) in Chinese — is the intensely fragrant white flower of *Michelia alba* (now often placed as *Magnolia × alba*), a magnolia-family tree cultivated across southern China and Southeast Asia. It is a heady, sweet, slightly fruity white-floral, widely used to scent tea, sachets, and floral incense. The flower is also one of the defining scents of southern-Chinese fragrance culture, worn as a fresh blossom in daily life.

## Aroma profile

Champaca is sweet, heady, and white-floral, with a slightly fruity, almost tropical lift that distinguishes it from the creamier gardenia or the sharper jasmine. It is a rich, enveloping floral note, strongest in the fresh blossom. The fresh flower is worn and used to scent tea; in incense the dried or oil form is used as a delicate floral accent.

## Botanical source and origin

*Michelia alba* is a magnolia-family tree native to Southeast Asia and long cultivated in southern China, where it is grown for its fragrant white blossoms. Taxonomically it has moved between *Michelia* and *Magnolia*; the currently accepted name is *Magnolia × alba*, with *Michelia alba* widely used as a synonym. The flower is the part used.

> **A naming caution:** 白兰花 (*Michelia alba*, white champaca) is not an orchid despite the 兰 in its name. It belongs to the magnolia family. It is also distinct from the pale-flowered 玉兰 (yulan, *Magnolia denudata*), a different magnolia.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Tea scenting:** 白兰花 is used to scent tea, one of the classical aromatic techniques of southern China, alongside jasmine and osmanthus.
- **Worn blossom:** the fresh flower is famously worn in daily life in southern China for its scent — a living, personal fragrance rather than a burned material.
- **Floral incense and sachets:** the flower's heady scent is referenced in floral incense and sachet blends.
- **Materia medica:** 白兰花 has traditional aromatic uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The fragrance and tea-scenting roles are well documented. Any medicinal use is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity and the flower's long use as a fresh, heady white-floral.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Champaca's fragrance is strongest fresh; the dried flower is subtle, used as a delicate floral accent.
- Distinguish 白兰花 (*Michelia alba*, white champaca) from 玉兰 (*Magnolia denudata*, yulan) — different magnolias.
- In incense, champaca is usually a floral accent or a themed note, not a dominant burned material.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
