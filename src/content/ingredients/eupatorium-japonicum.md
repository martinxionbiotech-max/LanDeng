> **Direct answer:** Zelan (泽兰, zélán) is the aromatic herb *Eupatorium japonicum*, a daisy-family (Asteraceae) perennial native to East Asia. It is a same-genus relative of, and must be distinguished from, 佩兰 (*Eupatorium fortunei*). The name 泽兰 is contested in Chinese materia medica, and that naming tangle is set out plainly rather than papered over.

## A naming caution (read this)

泽兰 is a **contested name**, and the honesty matters:

- **Classical 泽兰:** the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之三, records 泽兰 as a plant with "方莖紫節" (square stem, purple nodes) growing by water — which modern botanists identify as the mint-family herb *Lycopus lucidus* (地笋), **not** a *Eupatorium*.
- **This entry — 泽兰 as *Eupatorium japonicum*:** the daisy-family (Asteraceae) herb that Flora of China gives the Chinese name 白头婆, and which is also called 泽兰/山泽兰 in some regional usage. It is the **same genus as 佩兰 (*Eupatorium fortunei*)** — a distinct species, not the mint-family 泽兰.

The *Bencao Gangmu* itself flags the 泽兰/兰草 (佩兰) confusion — "其與蘭草為一物二種" — showing how old the naming tangle is. **This page describes 泽兰 = *Eupatorium japonicum*, the daisy-family herb, not *Lycopus lucidus*.**

## Aroma profile

泽兰 has a fresh, herbaceous, lightly aromatic scent with a gentle sweetness — the clean "green" character of the *Eupatorium* herbs, close to but a touch less sweet than 佩兰. It reads as a fresh herbal note, useful as a light accent rather than a dominant incense smell.

## The 泽兰 / 佩兰 distinction (read this)

泽兰 and 佩兰 are **closely related but distinct species in the same genus** (*Eupatorium*):

- **泽兰 (this entry)** — *Eupatorium japonicum*, the daisy-family herb; Flora of China names it 白头婆.
- **佩兰** — *Eupatorium fortunei*, the classical "worn-orchid" fragrant herb, its own entry.

Both are Asteraceae herbs with fresh, herbaceous scents; they differ as species within *Eupatorium*. The two must be kept distinct in sourcing.

## Botanical source and origin

*Eupatorium japonicum* is a herbaceous perennial of the daisy family (Asteraceae), native to China, Japan, and Korea, where it grows in open and semi-shaded places. Flora of China records the species under the Chinese name 白头婆. The part used is the aerial herb. The name 泽兰 is applied to it in some Chinese usage, but the classical 本草纲目 泽兰 refers to a different (mint-family) plant — a documented naming conflict.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Fragrant herb:** as an aromatic *Eupatorium*, it belongs to the same fresh, herbaceous family of scented herbs as 佩兰.
- **Naming:** the 泽兰/兰草 (佩兰) confusion is ancient, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* itself.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Asteraceae placement, the 佩兰 distinction, and the 泽兰 naming conflict.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 泽兰 (*Eupatorium japonicum*) should be clean and green, with a fresh, herbaceous scent when rubbed.
- Distinguish it from 佩兰 (*Eupatorium fortunei*) — a different *Eupatorium* species, a touch sweeter.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
