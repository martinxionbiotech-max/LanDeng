> **Direct answer:** Asarum (细辛, xìxīn) is the dried root and rhizome of *Asarum sieboldii*, a low woodland herb of the birthwort family (Aristolochiaceae) native to China, Korea, and Japan. Its name means "thin and pungent," from the slender root's intense, spicy, camphoraceous scent. It is a classical Chinese materia-medica root, not a documented sachet or incense ingredient.

## Aroma profile

细辛 has an intense, pungent, spicy, rooty scent with a camphoraceous, almost pepper-like bite — the hot, aromatic smell of a wild-ginger root rather than a flower or leaf. It is a sharp, medicinal aromatic, far hotter and more biting than the sweet, resinous 香材. Its name records exactly this: 细 ("slender") + 辛 ("pungent"), the thin root with the very hot taste and smell.

## The 细辛 / 杜衡 distinction (read this)

细辛 has a classical look-alike worth knowing:

- **细辛 (asarum)** — *Asarum sieboldii*, the slender, intensely pungent root. This entry.
- **杜衡 (wild ginger)** — *Asarum forbesii* (and related *Asarum* species), a similar-looking root often passed off for 细辛.

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之二, quotes Shen Kuo's *Mengxi Bitan*: 细辛 grows on Mount Hua, "極細而直，柔韌，深紫色，味極辛" (extremely thin, straight, pliant, deep purple, very pungent), and warns that 杜衡 (with curved, brittle, yellow-white roots) was long sold in its place — "杜衡亂細辛，自古已然矣." **This page describes 细辛 = *Asarum sieboldii*.**

## Botanical source and origin

*Asarum sieboldii* is a low, shade-loving woodland herb in the birthwort family (Aristolochiaceae), native to China, Korea, and Japan. Flora of China records the species (细辛), alongside the closely related *Asarum heterotropoides*, which is also gathered as 细辛 in northern China. The part used is the slender, aromatic root and rhizome, dug and dried.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 细辛 is a classical materia-medica root, recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之二. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Naming:** the *Bencao Gangmu* records the aliases 小辛 and 少辛 and explains "華州真細辛，根細而味極辛" — the true 细辛 has a thin root and an extremely pungent taste.
- **A documented caution:** classical texts note 细辛 is potent and was used in small amounts — a materia-medica dosing caution, not an incense note.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Aristolochiaceae placement, and the 杜衡 look-alike distinction.

## A note on 香囊 (sachet) use

We did **not** find 细辛 documented as a classical 香囊 or incense ingredient in the incense manuals and materia-medica records consulted here. Its classical role is materia medica, and its pungent root scent is medicinal rather than a fragrance for sachets or burning. This is stated honestly: **no sachet or incense origin is claimed.**

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 细辛 is the slender root of *Asarum sieboldii*; expect an intense, pungent, spicy, camphoraceous scent.
- Distinguish it from 杜衡 (*Asarum forbesii*), a similar wild-ginger root with curved, brittle, yellow-white roots — a classical substitution warning.
- It is a sharp aromatic root, not a sweet incense 香材; its classical role is materia medica.

## FAQ

**What is 细辛 (asarum)?** The dried root and rhizome of *Asarum sieboldii*, a low woodland herb of the birthwort family (Aristolochiaceae) native to China, Korea, and Japan. Its name means "thin [and] pungent," from the slender root's intense, spicy, camphoraceous scent. It is a classical Chinese materia-medica root.

**What does 细辛 mean?** 细 means "slender" and 辛 means "pungent." The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 草之二, explains "華州真細辛，根細而味極辛" — the true 细辛 has a thin root and an extremely pungent taste. The classical aliases 小辛 and 少辛 carry the same meaning.

**Is 细辛 the same as 杜衡?** No. 细辛 is *Asarum sieboldii*, slender and very pungent; 杜衡 is *Asarum forbesii*, a similar wild-ginger root with curved, brittle, yellow-white roots. The *Bencao Gangmu* warns that 杜衡 was long sold in 细辛's place — "杜衡亂細辛，自古已然矣."

**Is 细辛 an incense or sachet (香囊) ingredient?** We did not find it documented as a classical 香囊 or incense ingredient. Its classical role is materia medica (草之二), and its pungent root scent is medicinal rather than a fragrance for sachets or burning. No sachet or incense origin is claimed.

**What does asarum smell like?** Intense, pungent, spicy, and rooty, with a camphoraceous, almost pepper-like bite — the hot, aromatic smell of a wild-ginger root. It is far sharper and more medicinal than the sweet, resinous incense 香材.
