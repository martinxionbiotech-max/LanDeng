> **Direct answer:** Arborvitae leaves (侧柏叶, cèbǎi yè) are the leafy branchlet of the oriental arborvitae, *Platycladus orientalis* (侧柏), a cypress-family tree native to China and Korea. It is the leafy part of the very same tree that yields cypress seed (柏子) — same species, different part. The *Bencao Gangmu* records 柏 in its tree section, explaining the name 侧柏 from the flat, sideways-growing leaves.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** The name 侧柏 means "sideways cypress" — 侧 (side/sideways) + 柏 (the cypress/arborvitae register). The *Bencao Gangmu* explains it from the leaf's flat, side-oriented growth: only the flat, sideways-growing leaves are taken for use, hence 侧柏. The 柏 character, as with 崖柏 and 柏子, names a broad conifer class.

The name 侧柏 (cèbǎi) is a growth-form name: 侧 "side/sideways" + 柏, the cypress/arborvitae character. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) explains it precisely from the leaf: "入药惟取叶扁而侧生者，故曰侧柏" — only the flat, sideways-growing leaves are taken for use, hence 侧柏. As with 崖柏 and 柏子, the 柏 character is a *category* marker — "cypress/arborvitae conifer" — not a species name, which is why these three 柏-named materials are easily blurred despite naming different species and parts.

## The leaf-versus-seed boundary in practice

> **Direct answer:** The practical meaning of "same tree, different parts" is a sourcing rule: the leaf and the seed are bought and used differently. The leaf is a fresh, green, materia-medica part; the seed is a roasted, nutty incense material. A buyer who conflates them buys the wrong part of the tree.

The leaf-versus-seed split is not a technicality; it is a sourcing rule:

- **Different characters.** The leaf is fresh, woody, green, and slightly resinous; the seed, once prepared (scalded, soaked in wine and honey, and dried), is roasted, nutty, and woody. They do not substitute for each other.
- **Different uses.** The seed is the material of the classical scholar's incense 柏子香 — the deliberately inexpensive, everyman counterpoint to costly agarwood-and-musk blends. The leaf is chiefly a materia-medica part, not a classical incense lead.
- **Different entries, one identity.** This site keeps them as two entries precisely because a knowledge graph records *parts* as distinct materials even when the plant is one. The disambiguation is the content.

The lesson generalizes: when a single plant yields multiple aromatic parts, "the plant" is not specific enough for sourcing — the part is the material.
