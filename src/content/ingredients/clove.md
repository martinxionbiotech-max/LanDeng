# Clove (丁香 / Ding Xiang)

**Direct answer:** Clove — *dīngxiāng* (丁香) in Chinese — is the dried flower bud of *Syzygium aromaticum*, prized for a warm, spicy, penetrating aroma. It is used as a spice, in incense, and in traditional materia medica.

## Aroma profile

Clove has a sharp, warm, spicy, slightly sweet aroma — one of the most penetrating spice notes. A little goes a long way; it is usually a supporting note in blends rather than a base.

## A naming trap (disambiguation)

In Chinese, **丁香 (dīngxiāng) means two different plants**:

- **Clove** — *Syzygium aromaticum*, the spice/incense material.
- **Lilac** — *Syringa* species, the ornamental flowering shrub (sometimes called 欧丁香, "European clove").

The spice clove and the ornamental lilac are botanically unrelated. Context distinguishes them; in an incense/spice context, 丁香 means clove.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

Clove's principal compound, **eugenol**, is the subject of laboratory research on anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antimicrobial activity. In traditional materia medica, clove is described as a warming herb.

**Important:** we treat medicinal claims about clove as **traditional and/or preliminary research, not established clinical fact.** We describe clove's aromatic and culinary use; we do not make medical claims.

## Safety

Clove oil/eugenol is potent and can be irritating; it should not be applied undiluted. Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter — burn in a well-ventilated space.

## FAQ

**Is 丁香 clove or lilac?** Both — the Chinese word 丁香 names the spice clove (*Syzygium aromaticum*) and the ornamental lilac (*Syringa*). In a spice/incense context it means clove.
