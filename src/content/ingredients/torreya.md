> **Direct answer:** Torreya (香榧, xiāngfěi) is the nut of the Chinese torreya, *Torreya grandis*, an evergreen conifer of the yew family (Taxaceae) native to eastern China. The large, aromatic seed is a prized Chinese nut, and the *Bencao Gangmu* records it under 榧实 with the classical name 玉山果 ("jade-mountain fruit"), echoed in a poem by Su Dongpo.

## Aroma profile

香榧 is nutty and woody, with a resinous, slightly roasted, faintly sweet character — the scent of a rich, oily conifer nut. It is subtle and food-adjacent rather than floral or spicy. Its primary role is culinary (the seed is eaten), and in aromatics the wood and seed contribute a quiet, warm, conifer-nut undertone rather than a lead fragrance.

## The 玉山果 (yu shan guo) name (read this)

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), in its fruit section (果之三), records the torreya under 榧实 with the classical name 玉山果 ("jade-mountain fruit"), and quotes the poet Su Dongpo (苏轼):

> "彼美玉山果，粲为金盘实。" — "That lovely jade-mountain fruit, gleaming as a dish of gold."

Li Shizhen explains the name: "榧亦作棐，其木名文木，斐然章采，故谓之榧。信州玉山县者為佳" — the tree is called 文木 (patterned wood) for its handsome grain, hence 榧, and the finest come from Yushan county in Xinzhou. The point for this encyclopedia: 香榧 is a valued Chinese nut with a genuine classical record — a culinary and materia-medica seed, not a burned incense material.

## Botanical source and origin

*Torreya grandis* is an evergreen conifer of the yew family (Taxaceae), native to eastern China (Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, and adjacent provinces) and long cultivated for its large, edible, aromatic seeds. The seed — commonly called 香榧 — is the material of value, with a hard shell enclosing an oily, aromatic kernel. Flora of China records the species.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Culinary nut:** 香榧 is a prized Chinese nut, eaten roasted for its rich, aromatic kernel.
- **玉山果 (yùshānguǒ):** the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records the classical name 玉山果, quoting Su Dongpo's praise.
- **Materia medica:** 榧实 appears in traditional Chinese medicine. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The culinary use is unambiguous and well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Taxaceae placement, and the culinary nut record.

## FAQ

**What is 香榧 (torreya)?** The nut of the Chinese torreya, *Torreya grandis*, an evergreen conifer of the yew family (Taxaceae) native to eastern China. Its large, aromatic seed is a prized Chinese nut, and the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records it under 榧实 with the classical name 玉山果.

**Why is torreya called 玉山果 (jade-mountain fruit)?** The name reflects the tree's fine wood and a famous growing region. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records 玉山果 as a classical name and quotes Su Dongpo: "彼美玉山果，粲为金盘实" (that lovely jade-mountain fruit, gleaming as a dish of gold), noting the finest come from Yushan county.

**What does torreya smell like?** Nutty and woody, with a resinous, slightly roasted, faintly sweet character — the scent of a rich, oily conifer nut. It is subtle and food-adjacent rather than floral or spicy, and its primary role is culinary rather than aromatic.

**Is 香榧 a burned incense material?** Not in the classical tradition. Torreya is a culinary nut with a materia-medica record (榧实); it is not a burned incense ingredient. Its aromatic value is quiet — a warm conifer-nut undertone — rather than a lead fragrance.

**What is the difference between torreya and cypress seed?** Both are conifer seeds, but torreya (香榧, *Torreya grandis*) is a yew-family (Taxaceae) culinary nut, while cypress seed (柏子, *Platycladus orientalis*) is a cypress-family (Cupressaceae) seed used in incense. Different families and different uses — food versus incense.

**Where does the Chinese torreya grow?** In eastern China — Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, and adjacent provinces — where it is long cultivated for its large, edible, aromatic seeds. The finest are traditionally associated with Yushan county.
