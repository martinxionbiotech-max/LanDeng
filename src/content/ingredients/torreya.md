> **Direct answer:** Torreya (香榧, xiāngfěi) is the nut of the Chinese torreya, *Torreya grandis*, an evergreen conifer of the yew family (Taxaceae) native to eastern China. The large, aromatic seed is a prized Chinese nut, and the *Bencao Gangmu* records it under 榧实 with the classical name 玉山果 ("jade-mountain fruit"), echoed in a poem by Su Dongpo.

## Botanical source and origin

> **Direct answer:** *Torreya grandis* is an evergreen tree to 25 m tall of the yew family (Taxaceae), with an ellipsoid-to-ovoid seed 2–4.5 × 1.2–2.5 cm. It is native to the mountains of eastern China — southern Anhui, northern Fujian, Zhejiang, and adjacent provinces — at 200–1,400 m, and has long been cultivated for its large, edible, aromatic seeds.

*Torreya grandis* is an evergreen tree of the yew family (Taxaceae). Flora of China records it as a **tree to 25 m tall**, with linear-lanceolate leaves and an **ellipsoid to ovoid seed 2–4.5 × 1.2–2.5 cm**, the seed maturing in the second year (**pollination April, seed maturity September–November of the second year**). It grows in **mountains, open valleys, and by streams at 200–1,400 m**, native to **southern Anhui, northern Fujian, northeastern Guizhou, western Hunan, southern Jiangsu, northern Jiangxi, and Zhejiang**. The seed — commonly called 香榧 — is the material of value, with a hard shell enclosing an oily, aromatic kernel. See [Torreya grandis in Flora of China](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200005500).

## The 玉山果 (yu shan guo) name (read this)

> **Direct answer:** The *Bencao Gangmu* records the torreya under 榧实 with the classical name 玉山果 ("jade-mountain fruit"), and quotes Su Dongpo's praise. Li Shizhen explains the name through the tree's handsome grain — 文木 ("patterned wood") — and names Yushan county as the finest source.

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), in its fruit section (果之三), records the torreya under 榧实 with the classical name 玉山果 ("jade-mountain fruit"), and quotes the poet Su Dongpo (苏轼):

> "彼美玉山果，粲为金盘实。" — "That lovely jade-mountain fruit, gleaming as a dish of gold."

Li Shizhen explains the name: "榧亦作棐，其木名文木，斐然章采，故谓之榧。信州玉山县者為佳" — the tree is called 文木 (patterned wood) for its handsome grain, hence 榧, and the finest come from Yushan county in Xinzhou. The point for this encyclopedia: 香榧 is a valued Chinese nut with a genuine classical record — a culinary and materia-medica seed, not a burned incense material.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Torreya is processed by harvesting the mature seed, removing the outer covering, and roasting the kernel for eating. There is no incense-processing tradition for the seed; its processing is culinary, and the aromatic note is a food scent rather than a prepared incense material.

The processing of 香榧 is culinary, not incense:

1. **Harvest** — the mature seed is collected in autumn of the second year.
2. **Dehusk / shell** — the outer covering and the hard shell are removed to reach the kernel.
3. **Roast** — the kernel is roasted, which develops its rich, nutty, faintly sweet aroma.

There is no classical incense-processing record for torreya; it is prepared for eating, and its aroma is a byproduct of that culinary preparation.
