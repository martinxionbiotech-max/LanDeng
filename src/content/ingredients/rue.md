> **Direct answer:** Rue (芸香, yúnxiāng) is the dried leafy herb of *Ruta graveolens*, a small evergreen shrub of the rue family (Rutaceae) native to the Mediterranean and western Asia, long cultivated in China. It is a strongly bitter, herbaceous aromatic with a green, faintly woody, penetrating scent, used in Chinese incense and as a scenting and book-preserving herb. Its Chinese name is also applied to a group of aromatic "book herbs."

## Aroma profile

Rue is strongly bitter and herbaceous, with a green, faintly woody, penetrating scent that is unmistakably sharp — often described as acrid or medicinal to modern noses. It is a *powerful accent* rather than a pleasant base: a little goes a long way, and it is valued more for its intense, protective, "green" character than for sweetness. This intensity is why it is blended sparingly, not burned alone.

## Botanical source and origin

*Ruta graveolens* is a small evergreen shrub of the rue family (Rutaceae), native to the Mediterranean and western Asia, and long cultivated as an aromatic and ornamental across Europe and China. The part used is the dried leafy herb. In Chinese, 芸香 has a broad application: it names rue, and it is also used for a group of aromatic herbs placed among books to repel insects — the "book herb" tradition that gives a library its classical name (芸阁 / 芸台).

> **A naming caution:** 芸香 primarily names rue (*Ruta graveolens*), but in Chinese it also carries a broad sense as an aromatic "book herb" placed among books to deter insects. The two senses are related — both are strong, protective green aromatics — but a label that says only 芸香 should be read with the herb context in mind.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Scenting and book herb:** 芸香 is the classical "book herb," placed among books and scrolls for its strong aromatic and insect-repelling character; a library or study is poetically called 芸阁 or 芸台.
- **Incense accent:** rue appears as a powerful, bitter-green accent in some 合香 blends, used sparingly for its penetrating character.
- **Materia medica:** 芸香 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 木部) with various described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The scenting and book-herb uses are well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the book-herb tradition, and the bitter-green aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Dried rue should be green, intensely aromatic, and bitter-herbaceous; its strength fades with age.
- Note rue's intensity — it is a powerful accent, blended sparingly, not a pleasant base.
- Rue (*Ruta graveolens*) can cause skin irritation on contact in some people; handle with care and keep away from children and pets.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. Rue herb can irritate skin on contact in some people. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
