**Direct answer:** Bergamot — *fóshǒugān* (佛手柑) in Chinese — is the bergamot orange, *Citrus × bergamia*, a cultivated citrus (Rutaceae) grown chiefly in southern Italy for its fragrant rind. Its peel yields bergamot essential oil, with a bright, citrus-floral, fresh, faintly bitter scent, familiar from Earl Grey tea and modern perfumery. It is a **modern essential-oil material, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient** — and it must be distinguished from the finger citron (佛手, *Citrus medica* var. *sarcodactylis*) and the citron (香橼, *Citrus medica*).

## Aroma profile

佛手柑 (bergamot) has a bright, sparkling citrus scent with a distinct floral-green, slightly bitter, almost tea-like edge — more complex and less sweet than lemon, with a delicate neroli-like lift. It is a classic top note in modern perfumery and the signature flavor of Earl Grey tea, prized for its fresh, uplifting clarity.

## A provenance note (read this)

佛手柑 (bergamot) is a **modern crossover**, not a classical Chinese 香材:

- The bergamot orange, *Citrus × bergamia*, is a cultivated Mediterranean citrus, not a native Chinese species; Flora of China does not record it.
- In Chinese, the name 佛手柑 is shared with — and easily confused with — the finger citron (佛手, *Citrus medica* var. *sarcodactylis*), which **is** a classical Chinese offering fruit. These are different plants: bergamot is the essential-oil citrus; finger citron is the Buddha's-hand fruit.
- Bergamot essential oil is a modern aromatherapy and perfumery material, absent from the classical 香材 canon.

This page records it honestly as a modern essential-oil material — **no classical Chinese origin is claimed, and none is fabricated.**

## Botanical source and origin

*Citrus × bergamia*, the bergamot orange, is a cultivated citrus (Rutaceae) grown chiefly in Calabria, southern Italy, for its fragrant rind. It is a modern hybrid — botanists generally treat it as a sour-orange × lemon cross. Modern taxonomy (GBIF) places *Citrus bergamia* under *Citrus × limon*. The rind is cold-pressed to yield bergamot essential oil.

## In modern use

- **Essential oil:** bergamot oil is a classic citrus top note in perfumery and aromatherapy.
- **Flavor:** the oil scents Earl Grey tea and some confectionery.
- **Not classical Chinese incense:** bergamot has no place in the classical 香材 canon; its Chinese use is modern essential oil and flavor.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

There is **no classical Chinese incense record** for 佛手柑 (bergamot); its position is entirely modern. We make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Rutaceae placement, and its modern essential-oil role.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. Bergamot essential oil can be phototoxic on skin; this page is about fragrance, not topical use. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
