> **Direct answer:** Silk tree flower (合欢花, héhuān huā) is the flower of *Albizia julibrissin* (合欢, "collective joy"), a legume tree (Fabaceae) native to Asia. Its pink, thread-like blossoms carry a delicate sweet scent, and the tree is a famous cultural plant: the *Bencao Gangmu* records 合欢, quoting the saying that it dispels anger and calms the heart — a cultural association rather than a documented incense use.

## Botanical identity

> **Direct answer:** *Albizia julibrissin* is a deciduous legume tree (Fabaceae). Flora of China describes a tree to 16 m tall with an open crown, feathery bipinnate leaves whose leaflets fold at night, and pink flowers with thread-like pink stamens about 2.5 cm long, flowering May–July. Its leaves fold together at night, giving the classical names 合昏 and 夜合.

Flora of China describes *Albizia julibrissin* precisely: **deciduous trees to 16 m tall, crown open**; branchlets angular; leaves bipinnate with 4–12 pairs of pinnae and 10–30 pairs of small, obliquely linear leaflets 6–12 × 1–4 mm; **flowers pink**, the filaments pink and about **2.5 cm long**, in terminal panicles; legume strap-shaped, flat, 9–15 × 1.5–2.5 cm; flowering **May–July**, fruiting August–October. Its leaves fold together at night — the "sleep movement" that gives the classical names 合昏 and 夜合 ("night-closing"). It is widely cultivated across China and in central, eastern, and southwestern Asia. See [Albizia julibrissin in Flora of China](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200011875) and [Albizia julibrissin on GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/species/2972983).

## Where does silk tree come from?

> **Direct answer:** *Albizia julibrissin* is native to a broad swath of Asia and widely planted for its feathery foliage and pink summer blossoms, from China through central, eastern, and southwestern Asia. Flora of China records it as widely cultivated across many Chinese provinces.

*Albizia julibrissin* is native to a broad swath of Asia and widely planted for its feathery foliage and its pink, thread-like summer blossoms, from China through central, eastern, and southwestern Asia. Flora of China records it as widely cultivated across many Chinese provinces — Anhui, Fujian, Gansu, Guizhou, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Jiangsu, Jiangxi, Shandong, Shanxi, Taiwan, Yunnan, and Zhejiang, among others.
