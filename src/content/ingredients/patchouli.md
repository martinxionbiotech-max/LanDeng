# Patchouli (广藿香 / Guang Huo Xiang)

**Direct answer:** Patchouli — *guǎnghuòxiāng* (广藿香) in Chinese — is the dried leaf of *Pogostemon cablin*, a bushy mint-family herb, with a heavy, earthy, woody, balsamic aroma. It is an important ingredient in East Asian incense and a classic perfumery base note.

## Aroma profile

Patchouli has a heavy, earthy, woody-balsamic scent with a musty-sweet, spicy edge. It is a strong base note that lingers. Like vetiver, it tends to be a supporting/base note rather than the star.

## In East Asian incense

Patchouli is an **important ingredient in East Asian incense**, not just a Western "hippie" scent (its 1960s–70s popularity in the West is a more recent chapter). In China, 广藿香 also appears in traditional materia medica.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

Patchouli is used as an insect repellent and in traditional medicine; early research on its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties exists but is **preliminary, not established clinical fact.** We describe its aromatic and incense use; we do not make medical claims.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space; avoid inhaling concentrated smoke.

## FAQ

**Is patchouli a wood?** No — it is the dried leaf of a mint-family herb (*Pogostemon cablin*).

**Is patchouli only a "hippie" scent?** Its 1960s–70s Western popularity is well-known, but patchouli has a much longer history in East Asian incense and perfumery.

## People Also Ask (Long-Tail)

**What does patchouli smell like?** Heavy, earthy, woody-balsamic with a musty-sweet, spicy edge — a strong base note that lingers.

**Where does patchouli come from?** Southeast Asia — patchouli (*Pogostemon cablin*) is native to the region and has long been an ingredient in East Asian incense and perfumery, well before its 1960s–70s Western popularity.

**What does patchouli pair with?** It anchors heavier blends — often paired with woods (sandalwood, cedar) and resins, or brightened with citrus. Like vetiver, it works as a base rather than a solo star.

**Is patchouli a wood?** No — it is the dried leaf of a mint-family herb (*Pogostemon cablin*), steam-distilled for its oil.
