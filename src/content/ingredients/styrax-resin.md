**Technical answer:** Storax — *sūhéxiāng* (苏合香) in Chinese — is an aromatic balsam produced from the inner bark of *Liquidambar orientalis*, the Oriental sweetgum of Asia Minor. In classical Chinese incense it was a prized *imported* resin, distinct from the native Chinese sweetgum (枫香, *Liquidambar formosana*), with a sweet, balsamic, cinnamon-like warmth.

## Aroma profile

Storax has a sweet, balsamic, resinous scent with a warm, faintly cinnamon-vanilla character. It is a *softening* resin — it rounds and sweetens a blend rather than adding a sharp or smoky edge, which is why classical sources valued the "aromatic, oily" grade over a woody, scentless one.

## Botanical source and the naming distinction

*Liquidambar orientalis* is the Oriental sweetgum, native to southwestern Asia Minor, whose wounded bark yields storax balsam. This is **not** the same tree as the Chinese sweetgum:

- **苏合香 (storax)** — *Liquidambar orientalis*, the imported balsam of classical texts.
- **枫香 (Chinese sweetgum)** — *Liquidambar formosana*, the native Chinese tree, treated on this site as a separate material (see [sweetgum](/ingredients/sweetgum/)).

The two are related trees with related resins, but the classical 苏合香 was specifically the *imported* storax. The Song-dynasty *Xiangpu* (香譜) traces the name to 苏合国 ("Sogdiana") and gives the Sanskrit name 咄鲁瑟剑 (*duruṣka*, "storax").

## In Chinese tradition

- **Incense:** 苏合香 was a valued imported resin, described as dense ("heavy as stone") and aromatic, with the best grade burning to white ash (烧之灰白者好).
- **The oil note:** classical texts specify that only the *oily, resinous* storax is genuinely aromatic — 药中只用有膏油者极芳烈 — and record that producers extracted the fragrant balsam first, leaving a woody residue of little scent. This is a useful buyer-intelligence point even today.
- **Materia medica:** storax appears in classical Chinese medicine (most famously the 苏合香丸 formula). We treat any such use as **traditional**, not established clinical fact.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The classical descriptions of storax — its origin, appearance, and the "oily grade is the aromatic grade" distinction — are historical and observational. The medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity and storax's long role as an imported aromatic resin.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Storax is a balsam/resin, distinct from benzoin (安息香, a *Styrax* genus resin that is actually a different family — see the note below) and from the native 枫香 (*Liquidambar formosana*).
- The aromatic value is in the *oily/resinous* fraction; a dry, woody, low-resin "storax" has little scent — the same point classical sources made.
- "Storax" in English can be confused with "styrax" (the *Styrax* genus, which yields *benzoin*, not storax). The naming overlap is a classic source of confusion.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**Is storax the same as benzoin?** No, and the naming overlap is a classic trap. Storax (苏合香) comes from *Liquidambar orientalis*, the Oriental sweetgum. Benzoin (安息香) comes from trees in the *Styrax* genus — despite the confusing fact that the English word "styrax" resembles "storax." They are different resins from different plants with different scents: storax is sweet-balsamic and cinnamon-like, benzoin is sweet-vanilla. See our [benzoin](/ingredients/benzoin/) page.

**What is the difference between 苏合香 and 枫香?** Both are sweetgum resins, but from different trees. 苏合香 (storax) is *Liquidambar orientalis*, the imported balsam of classical Chinese texts; 枫香 (Chinese sweetgum) is *Liquidambar formosana*, the native tree. The classical 苏合香 was specifically the imported storax, which is why the two are treated as separate materials on this site.

**What does storax smell like?** Storax is sweet, balsamic, and resinous, with a warm, faintly cinnamon-vanilla character. It is a softening resin that rounds and sweetens a blend. Classical sources emphasize that only the oily, resinous grade is genuinely aromatic — the woody, low-resin material has little scent — a distinction that still matters for buyers.

**Where does storax come from?** Storax balsam comes from the wounded inner bark of *Liquidambar orientalis*, the Oriental sweetgum native to southwestern Asia Minor. In classical Chinese texts it was an *imported* resin, traced to 苏合国 (Sogdiana) with the Sanskrit name 咄鲁瑟剑 (*duruṣka*). Its import status is part of its identity — it was a prized foreign aromatic, distinct from native Chinese resins.

**Why do classical texts emphasize the "oily" grade of storax?** Because the aromatic compounds are concentrated in the resinous, oily fraction. Classical sources record that producers extracted the fragrant balsam first and sold the spent woody residue — 大秦国人采得苏合香先煎其汁以为香膏乃卖其滓 — leaving a material with little scent. The same principle applies today: a dry, low-resin "storax" is not the aromatic grade.
