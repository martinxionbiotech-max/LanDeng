> **Direct answer:** Storax (苏合香, sūhéxiāng) is an aromatic balsam produced from the inner bark of *Liquidambar orientalis*, the Oriental sweetgum of Asia Minor. In classical Chinese incense it was a prized imported resin, distinct from the native Chinese sweetgum (枫香, *Liquidambar formosana*), with a sweet, balsamic, cinnamon-like warmth.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** 苏合香 is a foreign name for an imported resin. The Song-dynasty *Xiangpu* (香譜) traces it to 苏合国 ("Sogdiana") and gives the Sanskrit name 咄鲁瑟剑 (*duruṣka*, "storax") — recording both its Central Asian trade origin and its foreign name. It is distinct from 枫香 (the native sweetgum) and from 安息香 (benzoin).

## The "oily grade" buyer-intelligence note

> **Direct answer:** Classical texts specify that only the oily, resinous storax is genuinely aromatic — 药中只用有膏油者极芳烈 — and record that producers extracted the fragrant balsam first, leaving a woody residue of little scent (大秦国人采得苏合香先煎其汁以为香膏乃卖其滓). This is a 1,000-year-old supply-chain warning that still applies: a dry, low-resin "storax" is not the aromatic grade.

This is one of the most useful classical observations about storax, and it reads like a modern supply-chain warning:

- **Only the oily fraction is aromatic.** Classical texts specify that only the *oily, resinous* storax is genuinely aromatic — 药中只用有膏油者极芳烈.
- **The residue was sold separately.** Producers extracted the fragrant balsam first and sold the spent woody residue — 大秦国人采得苏合香先煎其汁以为香膏乃卖其滓 — leaving a material of little scent.

The same principle applies today: the aromatic value is in the resinous, oily fraction, and a dry, woody, low-resin "storax" is not the aromatic grade. This is buyer-intelligence that has not aged in a millennium.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Storax is obtained by wounding the inner bark of the sweetgum and collecting the balsam that exudes — a resin-tapping process, not a simple harvest. The aromatic value is in the oily, resinous fraction, so processing separates the fragrant balsam from the woody residue.

Storax is a *balsam*, obtained by wounding the inner bark of the tree and collecting the resin that exudes. Processing is therefore a resin-tapping sequence:

- **Tapping** — the bark is wounded, and the balsam exudes and is collected.
- **Separation** — the fragrant, oily balsam is separated from the spent woody residue; as classical sources note, this separation is what defines the aromatic grade.
