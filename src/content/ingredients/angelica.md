# Angelica (白芷 / Bai Zhi)

**Direct answer:** Angelica — *báizhǐ* (白芷) in Chinese — is the dried root of *Angelica dahurica* (family Apiaceae), an aromatic herb with a distinctive herbal, slightly sweet-bitter scent. It is a classic ingredient in Chinese 合香 (blended incense) and in traditional materia medica.

## Aroma profile

Angelica root has a warm, herbal, aromatic scent with a slightly sweet-bitter edge and a subtle spicy undertone. It is a "root" aroma — earthy and dry rather than floral — and it reads more like a seasoning than a flower.

## A naming note (do not confuse the two "angelicas")

"Angelica" in Chinese herbal and incense contexts usually means **白芷 (*Angelica dahurica*)**. But the same English name is also used for:

- **当归 (dāngguī)** — *Angelica sinensis*, the well-known "female ginseng" of Chinese materia medica, a different species with a different role.
- **European angelica** — *Angelica archangelica*, used in European liqueurs and perfumery.

They share a genus but are different species, with different aromas and uses. In incense, 白芷 (*A. dahurica*) is the relevant one.

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 白芷 is a classic supporting ingredient in Chinese incense formulas, contributing a warm, herbal body.
- **Materia medica:** 白芷 appears in traditional Chinese medicine, where it is described for various uses.
- **Cultural role:** it is one of many aromatic roots (alongside 菖蒲, 藿香, and others) that give classical 合香 its layered, herbal character.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

Traditional Chinese medicine describes 白芷 with a range of therapeutic uses. We treat these as **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. This page describes its aromatic and cultural role only.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter; burn in a well-ventilated space and avoid inhaling concentrated smoke. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**Is angelica the same as dong quai?** No — 白芷 (*Angelica dahurica*) and 当归 / dong quai (*Angelica sinensis*) are different species. 白芷 is the aromatic incense herb; 当归 is a different materia medica herb. The shared English word "angelica" hides the difference.

**What part of angelica is used?** The dried root — *Angelica dahurica* root is ground or sliced for incense and traditional use.

## People Also Ask (Long-Tail)

**What does angelica smell like?** Warm, herbal, and aromatic, with a slightly sweet-bitter edge — an earthy root note rather than a floral or woody one.

**Is angelica used alone in incense?** Rarely — it is usually a supporting ingredient in 合香 blends, adding herbal body beneath woods and resins rather than leading the scent.

**What is angelica root used for in incense?** It contributes a warm, herbal base note to Chinese blended incense and is one of the classical aromatic roots of the 合香 tradition.
