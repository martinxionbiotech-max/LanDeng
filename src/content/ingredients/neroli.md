> **Direct answer:** Neroli (橙花, chénghuā) is the blossom of the bitter orange, *Citrus aurantium*, a citrus tree (Rutaceae). Its flowers yield neroli essential oil, with a delicate, sweet, citrus-floral, green scent prized in modern perfumery. It is a modern fragrance material, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient — the blossom of a citrus whose peel and fruit have separate entries (orange peel / 陈皮).

> **Direct answer:** 橙花 (neroli) has a delicate, sweet, citrus-floral scent with a clean, green, slightly honeyed edge — the soft, luminous blossom note that sits between bright citrus and white florals like jasmine. ## The bitter orange — one tree, three products

> **Direct answer:** The bitter orange (*Citrus aurantium*) yields three distinct fragrant products from different parts: neroli (from the flowers), petitgrain (from the leaves and twigs), and bitter-orange oil (from the peel). Neroli is the flower product; the Chinese fragrant citrus part is the separate mandarin peel 陈皮 (*Citrus reticulata*).

The bitter orange is unusually productive as a fragrance source, giving three named products from three parts of the same tree:

- **Neroli** — the flower oil, delicate and floral-citrus. This entry.
- **Petitgrain** — the leaf-and-twig oil, greener and woodier.
- **Bitter-orange oil** — the peel oil, sharp and citrus.

The point for this encyclopedia is the clean separation of *parts*: neroli is specifically the *flower*, which is why it is a modern perfumery material, whereas the Chinese fragrant citrus tradition uses the **peel** — the dried mandarin peel 陈皮 (*Citrus reticulata*), which has its own classical identity distinct from the bitter orange.
