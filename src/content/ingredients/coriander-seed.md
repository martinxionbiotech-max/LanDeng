**Technical answer:** Coriander seed — *yánsuī zǐ* (芫荽籽) in Chinese — is the dried fruit of *Coriandrum sativum*, an annual herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) native to the Mediterranean and western Asia. It is a warm, citrusy spice with a woody, faintly floral undertone, used in Chinese incense as a fresh spice accent. It is the seed of the same plant as the leaf herb cilantro (芫荽, also 香菜), but the seed and the leaf are very different aromatics.

## Aroma profile

Coriander seed is warm and citrusy, with a woody, slightly spicy, faintly floral character that is completely different from the fresh, soapy-tasting leaf. The seed's aroma comes largely from the essential oil linalool, which gives it a clean, sweet-woody, citrusy lift. It is a *fresh spice accent* rather than a base note — it brightens heavier blends in the way that citrus and fennel do.

## Botanical source and origin

*Coriandrum sativum* is an annual herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae), native to the Mediterranean and western Asia, now grown worldwide. The same plant yields two distinct aromatics: the fresh leaf — cilantro in English, 芫荽 or 香菜 in Chinese — and the dried fruit, coriander seed. The seed is the part used in incense and most spice blends; its warm, citrusy character is entirely unlike the leaf.

> **A naming caution:** coriander seed (芫荽籽) and the leaf herb cilantro (芫荽 / 香菜) come from the *same plant*, *Coriandrum sativum*, but are different aromatics. The seed is warm, citrusy, and woody; the leaf is fresh, green, and polarizing. In incense it is the seed that is used.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Culinary spice:** coriander seed is a warm, citrusy spice used in cooking and spice blends.
- **Materia medica:** 芫荽 and 胡荽 (older name) are recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 菜部) as aromatics. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Incense context:** coriander seed appears as a fresh spice accent in some 合香 blends, alongside fennel and citrus peels, rather than as a classical defining material.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The culinary role is well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the seed-vs-leaf distinction, and the fresh, citrusy aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Whole coriander seed should be round, dry, and fragrant with a warm, citrusy, slightly floral aroma; ground seed loses scent faster.
- Distinguish the seed (芫荽籽, warm and citrusy) from the leaf herb (芫荽 / 香菜, fresh and green) — different aromatics from the same plant.
- In incense, coriander seed is a fresh spice accent, usually blended, not burned alone.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**What is coriander seed?** The dried fruit of *Coriandrum sativum*, an annual herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae) native to the Mediterranean and western Asia. It is a warm, citrusy spice with a woody, faintly floral undertone, used in cooking, spice blends, and as a fresh accent in some incense. It is the seed of the same plant as the leaf herb cilantro.

**Are coriander seed and cilantro the same thing?** They come from the same plant, *Coriandrum sativum*, but are different aromatics. The seed (芫荽籽) is warm, citrusy, woody, and faintly floral; the leaf (cilantro, 芫荽 / 香菜) is fresh, green, and strongly flavored. In incense and most spice blends, it is the seed that is used. They are not interchangeable in aroma.

**What does coriander seed smell like?** Warm and citrusy, with a woody, slightly spicy, faintly floral character that comes largely from the essential oil linalool. It is a fresh spice accent — it brightens heavier blends in the way that citrus and fennel do — rather than a base note. The seed's aroma is entirely unlike the fresh, green scent of the coriander leaf.

**What is 芫荽 in Chinese tradition?** 芫荽 (also 胡荽 in older texts) is the coriander plant, recorded as a culinary herb and a materia-medica aromatic in the 本草綱目 菜部. The seed (芫荽籽) is the part used as a spice, and it appears as a fresh accent in some 合香 (blended incense) formulas alongside fennel and citrus peels.

**How should coriander seed be stored for quality?** Whole, rather than ground. Whole coriander seed is round, dry, and fragrant with a warm, citrusy, slightly floral aroma, and it retains its scent far longer than pre-ground powder. The strength of the aroma when the seed is freshly crushed is the main quality indicator, and in incense it is a fresh spice accent used in blends.
