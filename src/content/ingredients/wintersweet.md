> **Direct answer:** Wintersweet (蜡梅, làmèi) is the flower of *Chimonanthus praecox*, a winter-flowering shrub of the wintersweet family (Calycanthaceae) native to central China. It is a sweet, honeyed, waxy floral that blooms in deep winter, prized in Chinese gardens and used as a winter floral note in incense. Despite its name, it is not a plum — it belongs to a different family from the true plum blossom (梅花, *Prunus mume*).

## Botanical identity

> **Direct answer:** *Chimonanthus praecox* is a deciduous or sometimes persistent-leaved shrub or small tree, 3–13 m tall, of the wintersweet family (Calycanthaceae). Flora of China records its sweetly fragrant flowers, 1.5–4 cm across, with 15–21 yellow tepals — the inner ones usually marked purplish-red — blooming October to March.

*Chimonanthus praecox* is a deciduous (sometimes partly persistent-leaved) shrub or small tree, 3–13 m tall, of the wintersweet family (Calycanthaceae). Flora of China describes sweetly fragrant flowers 1.5–4 cm in diameter, appearing on the previous year's branches generally before the leaves, with 15–21 yellow tepals whose inner ones usually carry purplish-red pigment; it blooms from October to March. The family Calycanthaceae is small and distinct from the rose family (Rosaceae) that contains the true plum. The Chinese name 蜡梅 means "wax plum" — a reference to the flower's waxy, translucent petals, not to any botanical relation to the plum. ## Where does wintersweet come from?

> **Direct answer:** Wintersweet is native to central China and has been cultivated for centuries as a winter-blooming ornamental. Flora of China notes it is now grown throughout most of China and in other temperate regions of the world, particularly for its sweetly fragrant flowers — and that its long cultivation makes some of its reported native distribution uncertain.

*Chimonanthus praecox* is a Chinese native, cultivated for centuries as a winter-blooming ornamental. Flora of China records that it is "cultivated throughout most of China as well as in other temperate areas of the world as a decorative plant, particularly for its sweetly fragrant flowers," and notes that because of its extensive and long cultivation, some of the reported distribution is uncertain as to whether it is actually native.
