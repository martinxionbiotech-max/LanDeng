**Technical answer:** 迷迭香 — *mídiéxiāng* in Chinese — is rosemary, the aromatic herb *Salvia rosmarinus* (long known as *Rosmarinus officinalis*), an evergreen shrub of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to the Mediterranean. It is a herbal, camphoraceous, fresh aromatic used in cooking and fragrance, and used in Chinese incense as a **herbal accent**. Its classical Chinese presence is real but specific: 香乘 records it as an exotic Western import introduced to the court in the third century (魏文帝), not as a core native 香道 ingredient — and this page positions that honestly.

## Aroma profile

Rosemary is herbal and camphoraceous, with a fresh, piney, slightly woody sharpness and a clean, resinous lift. It is brighter and more "green-woody" than mint, with none of mint's cool sweetness, and its camphor edge makes it a natural partner to bay leaf and eucalyptus notes. In a blend it reads as a *fresh herbal top note*, adding clarity and a dry, aromatic lift.

## The 迷迭香 classical record (read this)

Rosemary has a genuine — but narrow — classical Chinese footprint:

- **香乘 entry:** 香乘 records 迷迭香, quoting 广志 (出西域) and 魏略 (出大秦国), and noting it was transplanted to the imperial court in the 魏文帝 (Cao Pi) period — described as an imported aromatic "可佩服令人衣香" (worn to scent clothing), not as a native incense-canon material.
- **Western import, not native:** the record frames rosemary as a curiosity from the Western Regions / 大秦 (the Roman sphere), introduced to China around the third century.
- **Modern crossover:** today rosemary's incense and fragrance use is a Western-herbal crossover, not a continuation of a core 香道 tradition.

## Botanical source and origin

Rosemary is an evergreen shrub of the mint family (Lamiaceae), native to the Mediterranean. It was long classified as *Rosmarinus officinalis*; genetic work moved it into the sage genus as *Salvia rosmarinus*, and GBIF accepts the newer name. It is not a Chinese native and has no Flora of China treatment; its identity is anchored in GBIF and Western sources.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Third-century import:** 香乘 records 迷迭香 as an aromatic transplanted from the Western Regions to the 魏文帝 court, praised for scenting clothing.
- **Not a 香道 core material:** its classical appearance is as an exotic imported plant, not a staple incense ingredient, and it does not carry the recipe-canon weight of 甘松, 乳香, or 木香.
- **Culinary and fragrance herb:** rosemary is a staple Western culinary and fragrance herb, now used in modern blends and sachets.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic and culinary use is documented in Western sources, and the 香乘 import record is a documented historical note. We make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Lamiaceae placement, and the herbal camphoraceous aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 迷迭香 is rosemary (*Salvia rosmarinus*); expect a herbal, camphoraceous, piney character, brighter and woodier than mint.
- Note the taxonomy shift — *Rosmarinus officinalis* is the older name, *Salvia rosmarinus* the current one; either may appear on labels.
- In incense it is a fresh herbal top note, usually blended rather than burned alone, and is a modern crossover rather than a 香道 heritage material.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
