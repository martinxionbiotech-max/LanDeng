**Technical answer:** 干姜 — *gānjiāng* (dried ginger) in Chinese — is the dried rhizome of *Zingiber officinale*, the common ginger of the ginger family (Zingiberaceae), native to tropical Asia and cultivated worldwide. It is a warming, pungent spice with a sharp, slightly citrusy warmth that comes from gingerol and the volatile essential oil. In Chinese incense it is a **warming spice accent**, used sparingly to add a dry, penetrating heat to 合香 blends.

## Aroma profile

Dried ginger is spicy and pungent, with a warm, dry heat over a faintly citrusy, resinous base. Its heat comes from **gingerol**, which partly converts to the sharper **shogaol** on drying — which is why dried ginger is hotter and more penetrating than fresh. In a blend, dried ginger reads as a *warming spice*: it adds a dry, aromatic heat that supports and brightens other spices without the sweetness of cinnamon or clove.

## Botanical source and origin

*Zingiber officinale* is a rhizomatous perennial of the ginger family (Zingiberaceae), native to tropical Asia and now cultivated across the world's warm regions. The part used is the rhizome, used fresh (生姜, shēngjiāng) or dried (干姜, gānjiāng). The ginger family also includes several other incense-relevant aromatics — most notably 高良姜 (galangal, *Alpinia officinarum*) and 山柰 (sand ginger, *Kaempferia galanga*) — which share a warm, camphoraceous family character but are distinct species.

> **A naming caution:** 干姜 is the *dried* rhizome of *Zingiber officinale*, while 生姜 (shēngjiāng) is the same rhizome *fresh*. They are one species processed two ways — and both are distinct from 高良姜 (galangal) and 山柰 (sand ginger), other gingers with their own entries.

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 干姜 is a warming spice accent, used in small quantities to add a dry, penetrating heat to spice-forward blends.
- **Culinary staple:** ginger is a foundational spice of Chinese cooking, used fresh and dried across a vast range of dishes.
- **Materia medica:** 干姜 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 菜部) with described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic and culinary use is well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the *Zingiber* genus placement, and the warm, pungent aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Dried ginger should be clean, firm, and sharply aromatic when cut or warmed; stale ginger loses its pungency.
- Distinguish 干姜 (dried ginger, *Zingiber officinale*) from 高良姜 (galangal) and 山柰 (sand ginger) — related but distinct gingers.
- In incense, dried ginger is a *sparing* accent, used to support and sharpen other spices rather than lead.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. Dried ginger's pungency can irritate the nose if burned heavily; use sparingly. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

It is a warming, pungent spice with a sharp, slightly citrusy warmth from gingerol and the volatile essential oil. In Chinese incense it is used sparingly as a warming spice accent in 合香 blends.

**What is the difference between 干姜 (dried) and 生姜 (fresh) ginger?** They are the same rhizome, *Zingiber officinale*, processed two ways. 生姜 (shēngjiāng) is the fresh rhizome; 干姜 (gānjiāng) is the dried rhizome. Drying partly converts the heat compound gingerol into the sharper shogaol, so dried ginger is hotter and more penetrating than fresh. They are one species with two different characters, not two plants.

**How does 干姜 relate to galangal and sand ginger?** They are related but distinct gingers. 干姜 is *Zingiber officinale*; 高良姜 (galangal) is *Alpinia officinarum*; and 山柰 (sand ginger) is *Kaempferia galanga*. They share a warm, camphoraceous family character from the ginger family (Zingiberaceae), but each is a separate material with its own entry in this encyclopedia.

**What does dried ginger smell like?** Spicy and pungent, with a warm, dry heat over a faintly citrusy, resinous base. Its heat comes from gingerol, which partly converts to the sharper shogaol on drying — making dried ginger hotter than fresh. In a blend it reads as a warming spice that adds dry, aromatic heat without the sweetness of cinnamon or clove.

**Why is dried ginger hotter than fresh?** Because drying partly converts gingerol — the primary heat compound of fresh ginger — into shogaol, a sharper, more penetrating compound. The result is that dried ginger (干姜) carries a more intense, dry heat than fresh ginger (生姜), even though both come from the same rhizome. This is why they are treated as distinct grades in traditional usage.
