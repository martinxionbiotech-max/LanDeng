> **Direct answer:** Galbanum (白松香, báisōngxiāng) is the gum-resin of *Ferula gummosa* (and related *Ferula* species), a giant fennel of the carrot family (Apiaceae) native to Iran and Central Asia. It is a green, balsamic, sharp resin used since antiquity in Western and Middle-Eastern incense and perfumery; its closest relative here is asafoetida (阿魏), another *Ferula* resin. It is not attested in the Chinese compendium 香乘.

## Aroma profile

Galbanum is green and sharp at the top, with a bitter, earthy, balsamic depth beneath — the archetypal "green" note of classical perfumery, smelling of crushed stems, pine, and wet earth with a resinous backbone. It is more vegetal and astringent than frankincense, and less sulfurous than its relative asafoetida. In a blend it reads as a *green, bracing resin*, adding a fresh, sappy lift that few resins provide.

## The 白松香 / 阿魏 family note

Two *Ferula* gum-resins from the same region are easily related but distinct:

- **白松香 (galbanum)** — *Ferula gummosa* and relatives; green, balsamic, sharp. This entry.
- **阿魏 (asafoetida)** — *Ferula assa-foetida* / *F. foetida*; far more sulfurous and pungent. Its own entry.

Both are giant fennel resins from Iran and Central Asia, but their scent characters differ sharply, and the Chinese names are not interchangeable.

## Botanical source and origin

*Ferula gummosa* is a large perennial herb of the carrot family (Apiaceae), native to the mountains of Iran and Central Asia. The gum-resin is tapped from the root and lower stem, hardening into the "tears" traded since antiquity. Unlike most entries here, it is **not** a Chinese native and has no Flora of China treatment — its identity is anchored in GBIF and Western classical sources (where galbanum is documented from Dioscorides and Pliny onward).

## In Chinese tradition

- **Not a 香乘 ingredient:** 白松香 does not appear in the classical incense compendium 香乘. We could not verify a classical Chinese incense-canon entry for galbanum, and we record that honestly rather than asserting one.
- **Translated Western knowledge:** the name 白松香 (and 格蓬) enters Chinese via translated Western materia-medica and perfumery vocabulary, reflecting galbanum's Mediterranean/Middle-Eastern origin.
- **Western classical incense:** galbanum is a documented ingredient of Western and Middle-Eastern incense and perfumery from antiquity.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The incense and perfumery use is documented in Western classical sources. Medicinal uses, where they appear, are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Apiaceae placement, and the green balsamic aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 白松香 is galbanum (*Ferula gummosa*), a green, sharp resin — distinct from 阿魏 (asafoetida), its sulfurous relative.
- Expect a green, sappy, balsamic character; genuine galbanum is bitter-green rather than sweet or citrus.
- Because it is not a Chinese-canon material, treat any "classical Chinese" provenance claim with care — its documented heritage is Western and Middle-Eastern.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
