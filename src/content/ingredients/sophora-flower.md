> **Direct answer:** Sophora flower (槐花, huáihuā) is the blossom of the pagoda tree, *Styphnolobium japonicum* (formerly *Sophora japonica*), a large legume tree (Fabaceae) native to China. Its flowers carry a light, sweet, honey-like floral scent and are used in traditional materia medica and, to a lesser extent, as a fragrant flower. The *Bencao Gangmu* records the tree under 槐, folding in 槐花 (the flower).

## Aroma profile

槐花 has a light, sweet, delicate floral scent with a honey-like, slightly green edge — a soft, pollen-like sweetness rather than the heady richness of jasmine or rose. It reads as a gentle garden flower, pleasant and mild, and it lends a quiet floral accent rather than a dominant note.

## Botanical source and origin

*Styphnolobium japonicum*, the pagoda tree, is a large legume tree (Fabaceae) native to China, long planted as a shade and ornamental tree. Its name was long given as *Sophora japonica*, now treated as a synonym; Flora of China records the species under *Styphnolobium*. The tree flowers in yellow-white clusters in summer, and both the flower (槐花) and the fruit (槐实) appear in the Chinese record.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Materia medica:** 槐花 appears in traditional Chinese materia medica. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **Naming:** the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), 木之二, records the tree under 槐, folding in 槐花 (the flower) among its parts.
- **Symbolism:** the 槐 tree (pagoda tree) carried classical associations with officialdom — "面三槐，三公位焉" (the three pagoda trees marked the seats of the three highest ministers).

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The materia-medica record is **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Fabaceae placement, and the flower's light fragrant role.

## FAQ

**What is 槐花 (sophora flower)?** The blossom of the pagoda tree, *Styphnolobium japonicum* (formerly *Sophora japonica*), a large legume tree (Fabaceae) native to China. Its flowers have a light, sweet, honey-like floral scent and are used in traditional materia medica and as a fragrant flower.

**What tree does 槐花 come from?** The pagoda tree (槐, *Styphnolobium japonicum*), long recorded as *Sophora japonica*. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records the tree under 槐 in its tree section (木之二), folding in the flower (槐花) among its parts.

**What does 槐花 smell like?** Light, sweet, and delicate, with a honey-like, slightly green edge — a soft, pollen-like sweetness rather than the heady richness of jasmine or rose. It reads as a gentle garden flower and lends a quiet floral accent.

**Is sophora flower an incense ingredient?** Not a lead one. Its traditional role is chiefly in materia medica, with a light fragrant-flower use. It can lend a soft, sweet floral accent, but it does not carry the intensity of the classical floral 香材 like jasmine or osmanthus.

**Is 槐花 the same as the 槐 fruit?** No — different parts of the same tree. 槐花 is the flower; 槐实 (or 槐角) is the fruit. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records both under the tree 槐, but they are distinct parts with distinct uses.
