> **Direct answer:** Copaiba (古巴香脂, gǔbā xiāngzhī) is the oleoresin tapped from trees of the genus *Copaifera*, a legume-family (Fabaceae) group native to tropical South America. It yields a woody, balsamic, resinous, slightly sweet balsam used in modern perfumery and aromatherapy. It is a modern crossover, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient.

## Aroma profile

古巴香脂 (copaiba) has a soft, woody, balsamic, resinous scent with a gentle, slightly sweet, almost creamy warmth — a mild, honeyed-wood balsam rather than a sharp pine or smoke. It is smoother and lighter than the heavier, vanilla-sweet Peru balsam.

## Botanical source and origin

*Copaifera* is a genus of large legume-family trees (Fabaceae) native to tropical South America and parts of Africa. The balsam is a natural oleoresin tapped from the trunk, collected much like a tree sap, and used directly or steam-distilled for copaiba oil. GBIF records the genus *Copaifera* (several species, all South American natives). It is not a Chinese species and has no classical Chinese record.

## In modern use

- **Aromatherapy and perfumery:** copaiba balsam and oil are used as a soft, woody, balsamic base note and a modern aromatherapy resin.
- **Fragrance fixative:** the balsam can act as a mild fixative in modern blends.
- **Not classical Chinese incense:** copaiba has no place in the classical 香材 canon; its Chinese name and use are modern.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

There is **no classical Chinese incense record** for 古巴香脂 (copaiba); its position is entirely modern. We make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Fabaceae placement, and its modern aromatherapy role.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 古巴香脂 (copaiba) is the tapped oleoresin of *Copaifera* spp.; expect a soft, woody, balsamic, resinous, slightly sweet scent.
- Distinguish it from Peru balsam (*Myroxylon balsamum*) — another South American balsam, but denser and vanilla-sweet.
- It is a modern balsam note, not a classical Chinese 香脂 (such as 安息香 or 乳香).

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

**How is copaiba used in fragrance?** As a soft woody-balsamic base note and a mild fixative in modern aromatherapy and perfumery, usually from the balsam or copaiba oil. It is a modern addition, not a classical 香材.
