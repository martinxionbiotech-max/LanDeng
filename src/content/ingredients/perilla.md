**Technical answer:** 紫苏 — *zǐsū* (perilla) in Chinese — is the aerial herb of *Perilla frutescens*, an annual of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to East Asia and long cultivated across China, Japan, and Korea. It is a fragrant, minty-basil herb with a sweet, anise-like character from its essential oil, used in Chinese incense as a **green herbal note** and as a culinary herb. The name 紫苏 refers to the purple-leaved form; the green-leaved form is called 白苏.

## Aroma profile

Perilla is herbal and minty, with a sweet, anise-like warmth and a faint basil edge — a fresh, green-sweet scent typical of the aromatic mint family. It is lighter and sweeter than true mint, and warmer than a plain green herb. In a blend it reads as a *green herbal note*, adding freshness and a soft, anise-sweet lift without the sharpness of mint.

## Botanical source and origin

*Perilla frutescens* is an annual herb of the mint family (Lamiaceae), native to East Asia and cultivated across China, Japan (where it is called shiso), and Korea. The part used is the aerial herb — leaves and flowering tops — fresh or dried. The species has two main color forms: the purple-leaved 紫苏 (zǐsū) and the green-leaved 白苏 (báisū), which differ in pigment but are the same species. The mint family also includes true mint (薄荷, *Mentha haplocalyx*) and 藿香 (agastache, *Agastache rugosa*), which share a green-herbal family character.

> **A naming caution:** 紫苏 (purple perilla) and 白苏 (green perilla) are **the same species**, *Perilla frutescens*, in two color forms — not different plants. Both are distinct from true mint (薄荷) and 藿香 (agastache), other mint-family herbs with their own entries.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Culinary herb:** perilla is a staple culinary herb across East Asia, used fresh and dried in cooking, pickling, and tea.
- **Incense:** 紫苏 is used as a light, green herbal note in incense, adding freshness and a soft anise-sweet lift.
- **Materia medica:** 紫苏 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 草部) with described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The culinary and aromatic use is well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the *Perilla* genus placement, and the green herbal aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Fresh perilla should be fragrant and clean; the dried herb should retain a sweet, anise-minty scent.
- Note that 紫苏 and 白苏 are the same species in two color forms — a labeling point, not two materials.
- In incense, perilla is a light green top note, usually blended rather than burned alone.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.
