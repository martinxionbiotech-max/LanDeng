> **Direct answer:** Magnolia flower bud (辛夷, xīnyí) is the dried flower bud of the magnolia tree, most classically *Magnolia biondii* (treated in Flora of China under *Yulania biondii*), a small tree native to central China. It is a floral-spicy aromatic with a green, slightly camphoraceous edge, used in Chinese incense as a floral top note. The name is also applied to closely related magnolias such as *Magnolia denudata*.

## Aroma profile

辛夷 is floral and spicy at once — a soft magnolia bloom crossed with a green, slightly camphoraceous sharpness from its essential oil. It is lighter and spicier than the creamy, heady florals like jasmine or gardenia, sitting closer to a "green floral" register. In a blend it works as a *floral top note*, adding lift and a fresh, slightly aromatic edge rather than deep sweetness.

## Botanical source and origin

The classical source is *Magnolia biondii*, a small deciduous tree of the magnolia family (Magnoliaceae) native to the mountains of central China (Shaanxi, Gansu, Henan, Sichuan). Flora of China places it in the segregate genus *Yulania* as *Yulania biondii*; GBIF accepts *Magnolia biondii*. The part used is the dried, unopened flower bud, gathered in late winter or early spring before the flower opens. In practice, 辛夷 may refer to the buds of several closely related spring-flowering magnolias, including the white magnolia *Magnolia denudata* (玉兰).

> **A naming caution:** 辛夷 (magnolia flower bud, *Magnolia biondii* and related) is distinct from 白兰花 (champaca/white champaca, *Michelia alba*), another aromatic magnolia-family flower with its own entry, and from 梅花 (plum blossom, *Prunus mume*), which is in an entirely different family. The shared "floral" register should not blur their separate identities.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Incense and scenting:** 辛夷 is used as a light, spicy floral note in incense and as a scenting material for garments and spaces.
- **Flowering culture:** the magnolia is a celebrated ornamental of classical Chinese gardens, and its buds carry the flower's early-spring association.
- **Materia medica:** 辛夷 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 木部), where it is traditionally associated with the nose and nasal passages. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The aromatic use is documented in classical records. Medicinal uses — including the traditional nasal association — are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the *Magnolia*/*Yulania* placement, and the floral-spicy aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 辛夷 should be clean, intact flower buds with a fresh, spicy-floral scent; broken or faded buds are lower grade.
- Note that 辛夷 may be sourced from *Magnolia biondii* or related magnolias such as *M. denudata* — a labeling point, not a quality defect.
- In incense, 辛夷 is a light floral top note, usually blended rather than burned alone.
