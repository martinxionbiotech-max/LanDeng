> **Direct answer:** Magnolia flower bud (辛夷, xīnyí) is the dried flower bud of the magnolia tree, most classically *Magnolia biondii* (treated in Flora of China under *Yulania biondii*), a small tree native to central China. It is a floral-spicy aromatic with a green, slightly camphoraceous edge, used in Chinese incense as a floral top note. The name is also applied to closely related magnolias such as *Magnolia denudata*.

## 辛夷 vs 玉兰: same family, different parts (read this)

> **Direct answer:** 辛夷 and 玉兰 belong to the same magnolia family, and their buds can even overlap in source, but they name different things in practice: 辛夷 is the dried unopened bud used as an aromatic, while 玉兰 is the open ornamental flower. The two are related materials, not one plant with two unrelated names.

This is the key same-family distinction. 辛夷 (magnolia bud, *Magnolia biondii* and related) and 玉兰 (yulan, *Magnolia denudata*) sit in the same magnolia family, and because the bud of *M. denudata* can also supply 辛夷, the two entries overlap at the source:

- **辛夷** — the dried *unopened bud*, the aromatic material.
- **玉兰** — the *open flower* of *Magnolia denudata*, the ornamental celebrated in gardens.

The practical point: 辛夷 is a bud material defined by its part and its spicy-floral character, while 玉兰 is a flower defined by its bloom. They are related, and a single tree can supply both names, but a buyer should know whether they are asking for the dried bud (辛夷) or the open flower (玉兰). 辛夷 is also distinct from 白兰花 (champaca, *Michelia alba*) and from 梅花 (plum blossom, *Prunus mume*), which is in an entirely different family.
