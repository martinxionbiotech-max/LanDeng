**Technical answer:** White cardamom — *báidòukòu* (白豆蔻) in Chinese — is the dried fruit of *Amomum kravanh*, a ginger-family (Zingiberaceae) plant of Southeast Asia. It is a warm, camphoraceous spice used in Chinese cooking, traditional medicine, and — as the classical texts specify — as a *blending* ingredient in incense (和香), not a burning material in its own right.

## Aroma profile

> **Direct answer:** White cardamom has a warm, camphoraceous, slightly eucalyptus-like aroma — more "medicinal-spicy" than sweet. It is one of a cluster of ginger-family spice scents, and in Chinese incense it adds a warm, pungent lift rather than a sweet one.

White cardamom has a warm, camphoraceous, slightly eucalyptus-like aroma — more "medicinal-spicy" than sweet. It is one of a cluster of ginger-family spice aromas, and in Chinese incense it is used to add a warm, pungent lift. The classical manuals specify that its role is *blending*: the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) notes that cardamom "is not a burning incense material — its seed and root are pungent and can only be used for blending" (豆蔻非焚爇香具…止可用以和香).

## Botanical identity

> **Direct answer:** 白豆蔻 is *Amomum kravanh*, a ginger-family (Zingiberaceae) plant of Southeast Asia; the dried fruit and its seed are the parts used. A closely related Java white cardamom, *Amomum compactum* (爪哇白豆蔻), is recorded by Flora of China as 1–1.5 m tall, native to Java and cultivated in Hainan and southern Yunnan.

White cardamom belongs to the ginger family, Zingiberaceae. Two names carry the botanical story:

- ***Amomum kravanh*** — the white/round cardamom, the Southeast Asian ginger-family spice that is 白豆蔻 in Chinese.
- ***Amomum compactum*** — the related Java white cardamom (爪哇白豆蔻), which Flora of China records as **1–1.5 m tall**, native to Java (Indonesia), and **cultivated in Hainan and southern Yunnan**, with medicinal use. See [Amomum compactum in Flora of China](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200028314).

The part used is the **dried fruit**, whose seeds carry the aroma. This is a spice fruit, structurally distinct from the woods, resins, and roots it is often blended with.

## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** The name 豆蔻 (cardamom) is underspecified — it covers several different plants. 白豆蔻 (white cardamom) is *Amomum kravanh*; 草豆蔻 (katsumadai) is *Alpinia katsumadae*; 肉豆蔻 is nutmeg (*Myristica fragrans*); green cardamom is *Elettaria cardamomum*. The 豆蔻 name alone does not identify the plant.

The word "豆蔻" (cardamom) covers several different spice plants, which is a standing source of confusion:

- **白豆蔻 (this page)** — *Amomum kravanh*, white/round cardamom, the Southeast Asian ginger-family spice.
- **草豆蔻 (katsumadai)** — *Alpinia katsumadae* (and relatives), a different ginger-family seed used in materia medica. See [katsumadai](/ingredients/katsumadai/).
- **肉豆蔻 (nutmeg)** — *Myristica fragrans*, a completely different plant and family, not a cardamom at all despite the shared 豆蔻 name.
- **Green cardamom** — *Elettaria cardamomum*, the familiar "cardamom" of Indian and Middle Eastern cooking, called 小豆蔻 in Chinese.

A label that says only "cardamom" or "豆蔻" is underspecified — these are different plants with different scents and uses.

## Where does cardamom come from?

> **Direct answer:** *Amomum kravanh* is a Southeast Asian ginger-family plant; the related *Amomum compactum* is native to Java and cultivated in Hainan and southern Yunnan. The spice is a tropical Southeast Asian crop, not a temperate or temperate-Chinese material.

White cardamom is a Southeast Asian spice. *Amomum kravanh* is native to mainland Southeast Asia, and the related Java white cardamom (*Amomum compactum*, 爪哇白豆蔻) is native to **Java (Indonesia)**, cultivated in **Hainan and southern Yunnan** per Flora of China. This tropical Southeast Asian origin distinguishes it from the temperate and montane roots (such as angelica and ligusticum) that also appear in Chinese incense.

## Why is it used in incense?

> **Direct answer:** White cardamom (白豆蔻) is used in incense as a blending spice — it adds a warm, pungent lift to a formula, but the classical manuals specify it is not burned alone. Its role is 和香 (blending), not a solo burn.

Cardamom's incense role is explicitly a **blending** one. The Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘) is specific: cardamom "is not a burning incense material — its seed and root are pungent and can only be used for blending" (豆蔻非焚爇香具…止可用以和香). So it appears in *huxiang* (合香) formulas as a warm, pungent supporting note — its warmth supports a formula rather than carrying one alone.

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Cardamom is used as the dried whole fruit, whose seeds carry the aroma; the seeds are separated and ground for blending. No extraction step is involved in the fruit or seed form; only an essential oil would require distillation.

The fruit is harvested and **dried**; the aroma is carried in the **seeds**, which are separated from the husk and ground for blending. No extraction step is involved in the dried-fruit or seed form. For powder buyers, particle size and moisture are the practical variables — but the exact target depends on the formula, binder, and production line (see Our Industry View below). The grinding and forming mechanics sit within the wider [how incense is made](/blog/how-incense-is-made/) process.

## What determines cardamom quality?

> **Direct answer:** Quality turns on the fruit's fullness and the seed's aromatic intensity — a well-filled, intact fruit with fragrant, camphoraceous seeds beats a shrunken or stale one. Because the seeds carry the scent, seed fullness and freshness are the decisive variables.

The quality factors, in rough order:

1. **Seed fullness and aromatic intensity** — the seeds carry the aroma; well-filled, fragrant, camphoraceous seeds are the benchmark.
2. **Fruit intactness** — whole, sound fruits beat broken or shrunken ones.
3. **Freshness** — the volatile camphoraceous character fades with age.
4. **For powder** — consistent particle size and moisture, judged against the specific formula.

There is **no standardized grading system**; the buyer evaluates the physical condition and aroma of the lot.

## In Chinese tradition

- **合香 (blended incense):** 白豆蔻 is a classical *blending* spice in incense formulas, contributing a warm, pungent lift — but specifically as a 和香 ingredient, not a solo burn.
- **Culinary:** 白豆蔻 is a warming spice in Chinese cooking and spiced-broth traditions.
- **Materia medica:** 白豆蔻 is a traditional-medicine herb, classically described for "transforming dampness and moving qi" (化湿行气) and related uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Cardamom vs related ginger-family spices

> **Direct answer:** 白豆蔻 (*Amomum kravanh*) is one of several ginger-family "cardamom" spices. 草豆蔻 (*Alpinia katsumadae*) is a more medicinal seed; 草果 (*Amomum tsao-ko*) is the smoky "black cardamom"; nutmeg (肉豆蔻) is an unrelated plant. They share the 豆蔻 or ginger-family name but are distinct.

| Spice | Botanical source | Character |

| 草豆蔻 (katsumadai) | *Alpinia katsumadae* | Warm, camphoraceous, more bitter |
| 草果 (tsaoko) | *Amomum tsao-ko* | Smoky, spicy, "black cardamom" |
| 肉豆蔻 (nutmeg) | *Myristica fragrans* | Sweet, warm, an unrelated family |

## Adulteration & misidentification

> **Direct answer:** The main risk is misidentification under the shared 豆蔻 name: 白豆蔻, 草豆蔻, 肉豆蔻 (nutmeg), and green cardamom are different plants. The Chinese name and botanical name on the label resolve which is meant.

Cardamom is not a heavily faked, expensive aromatic; its risk is the **shared 豆蔻 name**. A buyer who wants the white cardamom (*Amomum kravanh*) should not unknowingly receive 草豆蔻 (*Alpinia*), nutmeg (肉豆蔻, *Myristica fragrans*), or green cardamom (*Elettaria*) — all different plants with different scents and uses. The Chinese name (白豆蔻 vs 草豆蔻 vs 肉豆蔻) or the botanical name resolves the matter. ## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## Our Industry View

Its warm pungency supports a formula but can dominate or vanish depending on dosage and batch, so consistency — of aroma, particle size, moisture, and identity — matters more than any grade name.

From a manufacturing perspective, 白豆蔻 is a **blender whose value is warm pungency, not volume**. The classical manuals are explicit that it is a 和香 ingredient, not a burning material — which means its role in a modern formula is a supporting accent whose character must stay reproducible, and whose identity must not slip between the several "cardamoms."

Two points follow:

- **Identity is a specification, not a detail.** 白豆蔻, 草豆蔻, and nutmeg are different plants sharing a name; a formula built on one will not reproduce if the source switches silently to another. The Chinese name or botanical name is part of the specification.
- **Particle size and moisture affect behavior, not just aroma.** In powder, particle size changes how the material packs, draws air, and burns; moisture changes how it grinds and how the finished stick dries. The right target **depends on formulation** — the binder, the other ingredients, and the production line — so a figure that works in one factory does not transfer to another.

In our view, the commercial sweet spot is a **correctly identified, consistently full-seeded 白豆蔻** used as a measured blending accent — not a rare spice, but one that must be reorderable to the same specification.

## FAQ

**Is cardamom the same as nutmeg?** No. The Chinese name 豆蔻 covers both, which is the source of the confusion, but they are different plants and families. 白豆蔻 (white cardamom) is *Amomum kravanh*, a ginger-family spice; 肉豆蔻 ("meat 豆蔻") is *Myristica fragrans*, nutmeg, a completely different plant. The shared 豆蔻 name hides the difference, so a label should specify which is meant.

**Is cardamom used in Chinese incense?** Yes, but as a blender. **What is the difference between 白豆蔻 and 草豆蔻?** They are different ginger-family (Zingiberaceae) seeds. 白豆蔻 is *Amomum kravanh* (white/round cardamom), the Southeast Asian spice; 草豆蔻 is *Alpinia katsumadae* (and relatives), used in materia medica. **What is green cardamom?** Green cardamom is *Elettaria cardamomum*, the familiar "cardamom" of Indian and Middle Eastern cooking, called 小豆蔻 in Chinese. It is yet another distinct plant — not 白豆蔻 (*Amomum kravanh*), 草豆蔻 (*Alpinia*), or nutmeg (肉豆蔻). The English word "cardamom" usually means the green one, while 白豆蔻 is the white/round variety, which is a useful distinction to keep straight.

**Is 白豆蔻 the same as 草果 (tsaoko)?** No. 白豆蔻 is *Amomum kravanh* (white/round cardamom); 草果 is *Amomum tsao-ko*, the smoky "black cardamom" of southwestern Chinese cooking.
