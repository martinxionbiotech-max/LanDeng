**Technical answer:** 薄荷 — *bòhé* in Chinese — is the aerial herb of *Mentha haplocalyx*, a perennial of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to East Asia; modern botanical databases treat this name as a synonym of the broader *Mentha canadensis*. It is a cooling, minty, fresh aromatic from its menthol-rich essential oil, used in Chinese incense as a **cooling top note** and as a culinary herb. It is the "mint" of the Chinese pharmacopoeia, distinct in source from European peppermint.

## Aroma profile

Mint is cooling and fresh, with the unmistakable menthol character that gives it its crisp, penetrating, slightly sweet-green scent. It is a *top note* in the truest sense — bright, volatile, and instantly recognizable. In a blend it adds a cooling lift and freshness, cutting through heavier, warmer notes rather than adding body. Its cooling character comes chiefly from the compound **menthol**.

## Botanical source and origin

The Chinese pharmacopoeial mint is *Mentha haplocalyx*, a perennial herb of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to East Asia. Modern botanical databases treat *Mentha haplocalyx* as a synonym of *Mentha canadensis*, a widely distributed northern-hemisphere mint — so the scientific label may appear either way. The part used is the aerial herb — leaves and flowering tops — fresh or dried. The mint family also includes 紫苏 (perilla, *Perilla frutescens*) and 藿香 (agastache, *Agastache rugosa*), which share a green-herbal family character.

> **A naming caution:** 薄荷 (*Mentha haplocalyx*, syn. *Mentha canadensis*) is the East Asian mint of the Chinese pharmacopoeia, distinct in source from European peppermint (*Mentha × piperita*) and spearmint (*Mentha spicata*). The shared "minty" character reflects the common family and menthol chemistry, not the same plant.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Culinary herb:** 薄荷 is a staple fresh and dried culinary herb, used in teas, soups, and as a garnish across Chinese cooking.
- **Incense:** 薄荷 is used as a cooling, fresh top note in incense, adding lift and a clean minty character.
- **Materia medica:** 薄荷 is recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 草部) with described uses. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The culinary and aromatic use is well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the *Mentha* genus placement, and the cooling minty aromatic use.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- Fresh mint should be fragrant with a crisp menthol scent; the dried herb fades quickly and should be used promptly.
- Note that 薄荷 (*Mentha haplocalyx* / *M. canadensis*) is the East Asian mint, distinct from European peppermint and spearmint.
- In incense, mint is a bright cooling top note, usually blended and used sparingly.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. Mint's menthol can be intensely cooling; use sparingly. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**What is 薄荷 (mint)?** The aerial herb of *Mentha haplocalyx*, a perennial of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to East Asia; modern botanical databases treat the name as a synonym of *Mentha canadensis*. It is a cooling, minty, fresh aromatic from its menthol-rich essential oil, used in Chinese incense as a cooling top note and as a culinary herb. It is the "mint" of the Chinese pharmacopoeia.

**How does 薄荷 differ from peppermint or spearmint?** 薄荷 is *Mentha haplocalyx* (syn. *Mentha canadensis*), the East Asian mint of the Chinese pharmacopoeia. Peppermint is *Mentha × piperita* and spearmint is *Mentha spicata* — different mints of the same family. They share a minty, menthol character, but 薄荷 is the source used in the Chinese tradition. The shared character reflects the common family and chemistry, not the same plant.

**What does mint smell like?** Cooling and fresh, with the unmistakable menthol character that gives it a crisp, penetrating, slightly sweet-green scent. It is a true top note — bright, volatile, and instantly recognizable. In a blend it adds a cooling lift and freshness, cutting through heavier, warmer notes rather than adding body.

**Why is mint described as "cooling"?** Because of menthol, the dominant compound in its essential oil, which triggers the sensation of coolness on skin and mucous membranes. This cooling character is sensory — a perception, not a temperature change — and it is what gives mint its crisp, fresh, penetrating quality in both flavor and fragrance.

**How is 薄荷 used in Chinese tradition?** Primarily as a staple culinary herb — fresh and dried in teas, soups, and as a garnish — and as a cooling, fresh top note in incense. It is also recorded in traditional Chinese medicine (本草綱目, 草部). The medicinal uses are traditional language, not modern clinical evidence, and this encyclopedia makes no medical claims.
