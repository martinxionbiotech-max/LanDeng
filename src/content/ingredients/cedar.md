# Cedar (雪松 / Xue Song)

**Direct answer:** "Cedar" in incense is a confusing common name. It can mean **true cedar** (*Cedrus* species, such as Himalayan cedar *C. deodara*) or — very often in "cedarwood" products — **red cedar** (*Juniperus virginiana*), a juniper, not a true cedar. Both are used for a clean, woody, slightly sweet aroma.

## Aroma profile

Cedar has a clean, woody, slightly sweet-and-resinous aroma — a "dry wood" note distinct from the creamy sweetness of sandalwood or the resinous depth of agarwood. It is a common base or blending wood.

## The naming problem (buyer intelligence)

"Cedar" is one of the most ambiguous names in fragrance:

- **True cedars** (*Cedrus* spp.) — e.g. Himalayan cedar (*C. deodara*), Atlas cedar (*C. atlantica*), Lebanon cedar (*C. libani*).
- **"Cedarwood oil"** in commerce frequently comes from **red cedar** (*Juniperus virginiana*) — which is a juniper, not a cedar.
- Other "cedar" names (Western red cedar *Thuja plicata*, etc.) add further confusion.

> When you see "cedar" on a label, the actual species matters for aroma and price.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

Much "cedarwood incense" content repeats claims about relieving anxiety, insomnia, and depression. These are **traditional or unverified claims**, not established clinical fact. We describe cedar's aromatic use; we do not make medical claims.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space; avoid inhaling concentrated smoke.

## FAQ

**Is "cedarwood" a true cedar?** Often not — commercial "cedarwood" oil is frequently from red cedar (*Juniperus virginiana*), a juniper. Check the species.
