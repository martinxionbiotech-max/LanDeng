**Direct answer:** Lemon balm — *xiāngfēngcǎo* (香蜂草) in Chinese — is the herb *Melissa officinalis*, a perennial of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to southern Europe and the Mediterranean. Its leaves carry a soft, lemony, fresh, green scent, and it is a **modern crossover, not a classical Chinese incense ingredient**.

## Aroma profile

香蜂草 (lemon balm) is soft and lemony, with a fresh, green, herbaceous character and a gentle sweetness — the delicate lemon scent of the crushed leaf, quieter and more herbal than the bright zest of actual lemon. Its lemony character comes from the compounds **citral** and **citronellal**, shared with other lemon-scented herbs.

## Botanical source and origin

*Melissa officinalis*, lemon balm, is a perennial herb of the mint family (Lamiaceae), native to southern Europe and the Mediterranean. Flora of China records the species (under the name 香蜂花) as a cultivated or introduced plant. The fragrant part is the leaf, used fresh or dried. It shares the mint family with 薄荷 (*Mentha haplocalyx*) and 留兰香 (*Mentha spicata*), but is a distinct genus (*Melissa*).

## In modern use

- **Herbal tea and home fragrance:** lemon balm is a classic European herbal, used in teas and, in modern practice, home fragrance.
- **Fragrance:** lemon balm appears in modern perfumery and aromatherapy as a soft, fresh, green-lemony note.
- **Not classical Chinese incense:** lemon balm has no place in the classical 香材 canon; its Chinese use is modern and Western-influenced.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

There is **no classical Chinese incense record** for 香蜂草 (lemon balm); its position is entirely modern. We make **no medical claims**. The documented facts are botanical identity, the Lamiaceae placement, and its modern herbal and fragrance role.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 香蜂草 (lemon balm) here is *Melissa officinalis*; expect a soft, lemony, fresh, green scent.
- Distinguish it from lemon (*Citrus × limon*) — a bright citrus essential oil — and from the mints (薄荷, 留兰香), which are a different genus.
- In fragrance it is usually a soft green-lemony note, a modern addition rather than a classical 香材.
