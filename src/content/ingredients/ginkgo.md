> **Direct answer:** Ginkgo (银杏, yínxìng) is the seed of *Ginkgo biloba*, a "living fossil" tree that is the sole surviving species of its division, native to China. The seed, called 白果 (báiguǒ, "white fruit"), is a mild, nutty, faintly bitter food seed. The *Bencao Gangmu* records it under 白果 and 鸭脚子, noting it entered tribute in the Song dynasty.

## Aroma profile

Ginkgo nut is nutty and mild, with a faint sweetness and a slightly bitter, starchy edge — a quiet, food-like scent closer to a roasted chestnut or bean than to any spice or flower. The scent is subtle and secondary to its culinary role; the seed is eaten (roasted, boiled, or in savory and sweet dishes) far more than it is used for fragrance. In aromatics its value is negligible — a gentle food note, not a lead or supporting incense material.

## Botanical source and origin

*Ginkgo biloba* is a deciduous tree and the only living member of the division Ginkgophyta — a "living fossil" whose lineage predates the flowering plants and once ranged worldwide. Native to China, it is widely planted for its fan-shaped leaves and its edible seeds. The seed (白果) is enclosed in a fleshy, malodorous outer coat that is removed to reach the edible kernel. Flora of China records the species; no living relatives survive.

## In Chinese tradition

- **Food:** the seed 白果 is eaten roasted or boiled, in both sweet and savory dishes.
- **Materia medica:** 白果 appears in traditional Chinese medicine. This is **traditional materia-medica language, not modern clinical evidence.**
- **A naming record:** the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records 银杏 as 白果 and 鸭脚子, explaining the Song-dynasty tribute origin of the name 银杏 ("silver apricot").

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The food use is unambiguous and well documented. Medicinal uses are **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and we make **no medical claims**. The documented, non-medical facts are botanical identity, the Ginkgoaceae placement, and the food-seed record.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Practical points for buyers:

- 银杏 is the ginkgo seed (白果, *Ginkgo biloba*); expect a mild, nutty, faintly bitter, starchy character.
- The raw seed carries a fleshy, malodorous outer coat that must be removed; the edible part is the kernel.
- It is a food seed, not a burned incense material and not a classical 香乘 ingredient; position it accordingly.

## Safety

## FAQ

**What is 银杏 (ginkgo nut)?** The seed of the ginkgo, *Ginkgo biloba*, a "living fossil" tree that is the sole surviving species of its entire division and is native to China. The seed, called 白果 ("white fruit"), is a mild, nutty, faintly bitter food seed eaten roasted or boiled, and recorded in traditional materia medica.

**Why is ginkgo called a living fossil?** Because it is the only living member of the division Ginkgophyta, a lineage that predates the flowering plants and once ranged worldwide. Every other species in its group is extinct, leaving *Ginkgo biloba* as a solitary survivor of an ancient plant lineage, native to China and now widely planted for its fan-shaped leaves and edible seeds.

**Does 银杏 appear in the Bencao Gangmu?** Yes. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records 银杏 under the names 白果 and 鸭脚子, explaining that it entered tribute in the Song dynasty and was renamed 银杏 — "silver apricot" — for its shape and white kernel. The food use is documented, while medicinal uses are traditional, not established clinical fact.

**What does ginkgo nut smell like?** Nutty and mild, with a faint sweetness and a slightly bitter, starchy edge — a quiet, food-like scent closer to a roasted chestnut or bean than to any spice or flower. The scent is subtle and secondary to its culinary role, which is why it is eaten far more than it is used for fragrance.

**Is ginkgo a classical incense ingredient?** No. Ginkgo is a food seed, not a burned incense material and not part of the 香乘 incense canon. Its role is culinary and, traditionally, materia-medica, and its scent is a gentle food note rather than a lead or supporting fragrance in 合香.
