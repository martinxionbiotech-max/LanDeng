**Technical answer:** Plum blossom — *méihuā* (梅花) in Chinese — is the flower of *Prunus mume*, the East Asian "plum" (actually a close relative of the apricot) that blooms in late winter. It is one of the great floral motifs of Chinese incense and poetry, and the flower is used dried in floral incense blends and in the classical "plum incense" (梅花香) recipes.

## Aroma profile

The fresh plum blossom has a delicate, cool, almond-sweet scent that arrives while most plants are still dormant — a "winter" floral note. The dried flower is far subtler, used for a gentle floral lift in blends. The blossom's cultural weight — first flower of the year, a symbol of resilience — has always mattered as much as its scent.

## Botanical source and origin

*Prunus mume* is native to China and has been cultivated for millennia for its flowers and fruit. The same tree yields:

- **梅花 (méihuā)** — the blossom, used dried in incense and tea.
- **乌梅 (wūméi)** — the smoked, dried fruit, a materia-medica and culinary item.

Note that "plum blossom" in English is a translation of convenience: *Prunus mume* is botanically closer to the apricot than to the European plum, and its Chinese name 梅 is its own thing. The flower's identity — *P. mume*, the winter-blooming "mei" — is what matters.

## In Chinese tradition

- **梅花香 (plum incense):** classical incense recipes built around the plum-blossom theme are among the most famous in the tradition, including the storied 寿阳公主梅花香 (Princess Shouyang's plum incense). The Song-dynasty *Xiangpu* (香譜) includes a 梅花香 method.
- **A naming caution:** "梅花" also names a *grade of borneol* — 梅花脑 / 梅花片, the top ice-like borneol crystal named for resembling plum-blossom ice. That is 龙脑 (borneol), a completely different material. The word 梅花 in an incense label can mean either the flower or the borneol grade.
- **Seasonal culture:** plum blossom is the emblem of early spring and the "first of the four gentlemen" (梅兰竹菊), woven through poetry, painting, and seasonal ritual.

## Traditional use vs modern evidence

The plum blossom's role is cultural and aromatic, not medical. The dried flower (白梅花 / 绿萼梅) does appear in traditional materia medica, but we treat any such use as **traditional**, not established clinical fact, and make **no medical claims**. This page describes the flower's floral and cultural role only.

## Quality and sourcing notes

Dried plum blossom is subtle; its quality is judged by cleanliness and a gentle, intact floral character. Practical points:

- "梅花" on an incense label may refer to the flower, or to borneol (梅花脑 / 梅花片) — check which is meant.
- The flower is usually blended (it is too delicate to carry a formula alone), or referenced as a *theme* in a named recipe rather than as a dominant ingredient.

## Safety

Incense combustion produces smoke and particulate matter. Burn in a well-ventilated space, avoid inhaling concentrated smoke, and keep away from children and pets. This is editorial knowledge content, not medical advice.

## FAQ

**Is plum blossom used in Chinese incense?** Yes, in two distinct senses. The flower (*Prunus mume* blossom) is used dried in delicate floral blends, and it gives its name and theme to the famous classical "plum incense" (梅花香) recipes, including Princess Shouyang's plum incense. But "梅花" also names a *grade of borneol* — 梅花脑 / 梅花片, the top ice-like crystal. So an incense label saying 梅花 may mean the flower or the borneol grade, and the two are completely different materials.

**What does plum blossom smell like?** The fresh blossom has a delicate, cool, almond-sweet, green-floral scent that appears in late winter, before most plants bloom. It is subtle rather than heavy — a quiet "first flower of the year" note. The dried flower used in incense is even more delicate and is blended to add a gentle floral lift rather than to dominate a formula.

**What is Princess Shouyang's plum incense (寿阳公主梅花香)?** It is one of the most famous named incense recipes in the Chinese tradition, built around the plum-blossom theme and attributed to Princess Shouyang (寿阳公主). It is a *blended* incense whose name and character reference the plum blossom, and it survives as a classical recipe — see our page on [the Shouyang princess plum recipe](/blog/shouyang-princess-plum-recipe/). It is a cultural and aromatic tradition, not a medical formula.

**Is the "plum" in plum blossom the same as a European plum?** Not exactly. *Prunus mume* is a close relative of the apricot, native to East Asia and grown for its winter blossom and its fruit (乌梅), not the European plum. The English name "plum blossom" is a translation of convenience for 梅 (méi). The botanical identity that matters is *Prunus mume*, the winter-blooming "mei."

**What is the difference between 梅花 and 乌梅?** Both come from the same tree, *Prunus mume*. 梅花 (méihuā) is the blossom, used dried in floral incense and tea; 乌梅 (wūméi) is the smoked, dried fruit, used in materia medica and cooking. They are different parts of the same plant with different uses, and neither should be confused with the borneol grade 梅花脑 / 梅花片.
