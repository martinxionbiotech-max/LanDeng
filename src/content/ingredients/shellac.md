> **Direct answer:** Shellac (紫草茸, zǐcǎoróng) is lac resin, the resinous secretion of the lac insect *Kerria lacca*, a scale insect that coats twigs with a hard crust. It is an insect-derived resin, not a plant material; the *Bencao Gangmu* records lac under 紫鉚, and the Ming *Xiangcheng* (香乘) lists 紫矿 in incense formulas.

## Aroma profile

> **Direct answer:** Lac resin is resinous and waxy, with a subtle, faintly sweet, slightly smoky character when gently heated. It is not a powerful fragrance on its own — its historical value was as a red dye and a fixative-binder in incense formulas, not as a lead scent.

Lac resin is resinous and waxy, with a subtle, faintly sweet, slightly smoky character when gently heated. It is not a powerful fragrance on its own — its historical value was as a red dye and a fixative-binder in incense formulas, not as a lead scent.

The material's aromatic weakness is exactly the point: 紫草茸 was prized in the classical formulas for what it *did* — color, binding, and fixative support — not for what it *smelled like*. This places it in a distinct functional category alongside the binders (白及) and the animal-process materials (蜂蜡 / beeswax), rather than among the fragrant woods and resins.

## An insect resin, not a botanical (read this)

> **Direct answer:** 紫草茸 is the resinous secretion of a scale insect, not a plant material. The classical sources understood this clearly — the *Bencao Gangmu* notes that insects make lac on tree bark "just as bees make honey" (正如蜂造蜜也), and the *Xiangcheng* (香乘) lists 紫矿 in incense formulas such as 宝金香.

紫草茸 must be understood for what it is: **the resinous secretion of a scale insect, not a plant material.** The classical sources understood this clearly:

- The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目), in its insect section (虫之一), records lac under 紫鉚, noting "蚁于海畔树藤皮中为之……正如蜂造蜜也" — insects make it on the bark of trees and vines by the sea, just as bees make honey.
- The *Xiangcheng* (香乘) lists 紫矿 (lac) in incense formulas — for example the 宝金香 formula, which calls for "沉香一两、檀香一两、乳香一钱、紫矿二钱、安息香一钱" (agarwood, sandalwood, frankincense, lac, benzoin) — as a minor ingredient ground and blended with the aromatics.

**This page describes 紫草茸 = lac resin = shellac.** It is insect-derived, and we state that plainly rather than filing it under a living plant. The modern term "shellac" refers to the purified form used as a varnish, and it is the same material.

## Botanical/source identity

> **Direct answer:** Lac resin is produced by the lac insect *Kerria lacca* (family Kerriidae), a scale insect that colonizes the twigs of host trees in South and Southeast Asia and secretes a protective resinous coating. The host trees are incidental — the material is the insect's secretion, harvested, washed, and melted into shellac.

Lac resin is produced by the lac insect *Kerria lacca* (family Kerriidae), a scale insect that colonizes the twigs of certain host trees (in South and Southeast Asia) and secretes a protective resinous coating. The resin is harvested, crushed, washed, and melted to form shellac. The host trees are incidental — the material itself is the insect's secretion.

The identity point parallels beeswax: both are animal-process materials produced by insects on plants, and both are named for their animal source rather than a plant. See [Kerria lacca on GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/species/5812747). This is the framing that explains everything downstream — the dye use, the incense-formula use, and why the material must not be confused with plant resins.

## Why is it used in incense formulas?

> **Direct answer:** In classical Chinese incense, lac was a minor supporting ingredient — a dye and a fixative-binder ground into formulas like 宝金香 — not a lead fragrance. Its role was functional: to color, bind, and support the aromatic core of agarwood, sandalwood, frankincense, and benzoin.

Lac's place in classical incense was **supporting, not leading**. In the 香乘 formula 宝金香, it appears as 紫矿二钱 (two *qian* of lac) alongside the aromatics — a minor dose that served to color and bind rather than to scent. This reveals something about how classical 合香 formulas were engineered: they were built from an aromatic core (agarwood, sandalwood, frankincense, benzoin) plus functional non-aromatics (binders, dyes, fixative-support materials), and 紫草茸 belonged to the functional layer. It is the same logic by which 白及 (bletilla) appears as a binder — see [bletilla](/ingredients/bletilla/) and [how the 合香 blending system works](/blog/hexiang-blending-system/).

## How is it processed?

> **Direct answer:** Lac is processed by harvesting the resin-encrusted twigs, crushing and washing the raw lac to remove impurities, and melting it into shellac. The purified shellac flakes or blocks are the modern form; the classical dye and incense uses employed the raw or semi-purified lac.

The processing chain is: harvest the resin-encrusted twigs → crush and wash the crude lac to separate the resin from twig debris and insect remains → melt and strain into shellac. The purified shellac flakes or blocks are the modern varnish form. The classical Chinese dye and incense uses employed the lac in a less purified state, ground and blended into the formula — a functional ingredient rather than a finished varnish.

## What determines quality?

> **Direct answer:** Lac quality turns on purity — freedom from twig debris, insect parts, and adulterants — and on the color grade, since lac's classical value was as a red dye. Modern shellac is also graded by wax content and clarity for varnish use.

For a buyer, the practical checks are **purity** (freedom from twig fragments, insect matter, and added resins) and **color** (the red dye value that made lac historically prized). Modern shellac is additionally graded by **wax content and clarity** for varnish applications. In incense, lac is a minor supporting material, so the buyer's concern is a clean, correctly identified insect resin rather than a strong fragrance.

## Common forms

## Shellac vs dragon's blood

> **Direct answer:** Both are red resins, but from different origins. Lac (紫草茸) is an insect secretion (*Kerria lacca*); dragon's blood (血竭) is a plant fruit resin (*Daemonorops* / *Calamus* palms). The *Bencao Gangmu* itself records that the two were formerly confused and then separated.

## FAQ

**What is 紫草茸 (lac resin / shellac)?** The resinous secretion of the lac insect *Kerria lacca* (formerly *Laccifer lacca*), a scale insect of the family Kerriidae that coats the twigs of host trees with a hard resin. It is an insect-derived resin — not a plant material — and the purified form is known as shellac.

**Is lac resin a botanical?** No. Lac is produced by a scale insect, not by a plant. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) records this accurately: lac is made by insects on tree bark "正如蜂造蜜也" (just as bees make honey). The host tree is incidental; the material is the insect's secretion.

**How is 紫草茸 recorded in the classical texts?** As 紫鉚 or 紫矿 in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) insect section, and as 紫矿 in the Ming-dynasty *Xiangcheng* (香乘), which lists it in incense formulas — for example 宝金香, where it appears alongside agarwood, sandalwood, frankincense, and benzoin as a minor ground ingredient.

**What was lac resin used for?** Primarily as a red dye and colorant, and secondarily as a minor ingredient in incense formulas. Its incense role in the 香乘 is supporting rather than leading — it is not a strong fragrance on its own, but a resinous, waxy, subtle material.

**Is 紫草茸 the same as dragon's blood (血竭)?** No. Both are red resins, but dragon's blood (血竭) comes from a plant — the fruit resin of *Daemonorops* (syn. *Calamus*) palms — while lac (紫草茸) is the secretion of an insect. The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草纲目) itself notes the two were formerly confused and then separated, as they differ in origin and use.

**What is the difference between lac and shellac?** They are the same material. "Shellac" is the modern English name for the purified, processed form of lac resin — the insect secretion melted and formed into flakes — used as a varnish and finish. 紫草茸/紫鉚/紫矿 are the classical Chinese names for the same insect resin.

**How does lac relate to other animal-process materials like beeswax?** Both are insect-produced materials named for their animal source. Beeswax (蜂蜡) is the wax of honey bees, used as a process material in incense; lac (紫草茸) is the resinous secretion of the lac insect, used as a dye and fixative-binder. They belong together as the animal-process materials, distinct from the botanical resins. See [beeswax](/ingredients/beeswax/).
