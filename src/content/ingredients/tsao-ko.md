> **Direct answer:** Tsaoko (草果, cǎoguǒ) is the dried fruit of *Amomum tsao-ko* (recently reclassified as *Lanxangia tsao-ko* in some databases), a ginger-family herb (Zingiberaceae) native to the mountains of Yunnan and Guangxi. It is a smoky, spicy, camphoraceous aromatic best known as the "black cardamom" of southwestern Chinese cooking, used in incense as a 香药 (aromatic-medicine) spice accent.

## Aroma profile

> **Direct answer:** 草果 is smoky and spicy, with a camphoraceous, resinous warmth and a distinctly earthy-smoky depth that sets it apart from the brighter, sweeter white cardamom. ## Chinese name and terminology

> **Direct answer:** The name 草果 (cǎoguǒ) means "grass fruit" — 草 (grass/herb) + 果 (fruit) — a plain name that sits confusingly near 草豆蔻 ("grass cardamom," katsumadai). The near-homophone is a real trap: 草果 and 草豆蔻 are different ginger-family fruits with different species, yet the shared 草 and the shared "cardamom" register blur them.

The name 草果 (cǎoguǒ) is plain — "grass fruit" — and that plainness is itself a hazard, because it sits beside 草豆蔻 (cǎodòukòu), "grass cardamom" (katsumadai). The two names share the 草 character and a ginger-family register, but name different plants:

- **草果 (tsaoko)** — *Amomum tsao-ko*, the smoky "black cardamom." This entry.
- **草豆蔻 (katsumadai)** — *Alpinia katsumadae*, a seed of the ginger family. Its own entry.

A buyer who conflates 草果 and 草豆蔻 — two "grass"-named ginger-family fruits — has made a genuine sourcing error, because they are different species with different characters.

## The "black cardamom" false friend (read this)

> **Direct answer:** In English, 草果 is often called "black cardamom," but that name is a false friend: 草果 (*Amomum tsao-ko*) is not the Indian black cardamom (*Amomum subulatum*), and it is not white/round cardamom (*Amomum kravanh*) either. The English name collapses three different ginger-family fruits into one "cardamom" label, and the near-homophone 草豆蔻 adds a fourth layer of confusion.

The English name "black cardamom" does more harm than help with 草果, because it merges several distinct ginger-family fruits:

- **Indian black cardamom (*Amomum subulatum*)** — a different species from the Himalayan region, also smoky but botanically distinct.
- **白豆蔻 (*Amomum kravanh*)** — white/round cardamom, the bright, eucalyptus-sweet fruit. Its own entry.
- **草豆蔻 (*Alpinia katsumadae*)** — katsumadai, the "grass cardamom" seed, a fourth, distinct material.

So "black cardamom" is not a species name; it is a loose English category that 草果 happens to fall into. **This page describes 草果 = *Amomum tsao-ko* (*Lanxangia tsao-ko*).** When a source means *Amomum subulatum*, 白豆蔻, or 草豆蔻, it is describing a different material. ## How is it processed?
