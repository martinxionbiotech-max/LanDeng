## Botanical identity

- **Sandalwood** = heartwood of **Santalum** trees (Santalaceae), a hemiparasitic genus.
- Principal species: **Santalum album** (Indian / white sandalwood — the classic incense wood), plus S. spicatum (Australian), S. austrocaledonicum (New Caledonian), S. paniculatum (Hawaiian). Trade substitution is common and a buyer-intelligence point.
- Aroma: creamy, sweet, soft, woody — the "buttery" note distinguishes quality material.
- Chinese: **檀香** (tánxiāng). Long used in 香道, Buddhist/Taoist ritual, incense sticks, and traditional materia medica.

## Evidence review (the key opportunity)

The English SERP for "sandalwood incense" is **dominated by unverified health claims**: cures/relieves insomnia · relieves anxiety · lowers cortisol · mild sedative · lowers blood pressure · immune-boosting · anti-inflammatory. These are repeated across DTC blogs with little-to-no sourcing.

**Finding:** most of these are TRADITIONAL_BELIEF or UNVERIFIED_CLAIM, not ESTABLISHED EVIDENCE. Any LanDeng content must:
- Use "traditional use / aromatic experience / calming atmosphere" language — never "treats insomnia / relieves anxiety".
- If citing research, use preliminary/mechanistic language (santalol compounds), never clinical-cure framing.
- Disclose combustion risk (smoke/particulate matter).

**Original angle:** honest, evidence-governed sandalwood content — traditional use vs modern evidence — stands out against a SERP full of overclaiming. This is a trust differentiator, not a keyword play.

## Buyer-intelligence points (to develop)

- Santalum album vs substitutes (Australian/New Caledonian, or synthetic "sandalwood" fragrance).
- Powder vs oil-based sticks (oil ≠ wood aroma — noted in enthusiast reviews).
- Quality: true sandalwood powder is expensive (~$2/g in craft supply); cheap sticks are usually fragrance oil.

## Conflicts / uncertainty

- Species substitution in trade (which species = "real sandalwood") — needs S1/S2 verification.
- Whether any modern clinical evidence supports "relaxation" claims — needs an evidence sweep before any research page.
