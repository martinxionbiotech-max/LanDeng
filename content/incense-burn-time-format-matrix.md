# Incense Burn Time & Format Matrix: Stick, Coil, Cone, Resin, Powder

**Direct answer:** Incense formats differ mainly by **burn time** and **smoke level**: a stick runs roughly 20–60 minutes, a cone 15–30, a coil 1.5–4 hours, and resin/powder is session-based (minimal smoke on an electric heater, more on charcoal). Choose the format to match the session length and how much smoke you want.

**Key facts:** These times are **approximate** — they vary by thickness, material, and maker. The matrix is a planning aid, not a spec sheet.

---

## The Matrix

| Format | Typical burn time | Smoke level | Best for | Holder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thin / coreless stick | ~20–40 min | Low–moderate | Short sessions, small rooms | Stick holder / tray |
| Standard stick | ~30–60 min | Moderate | Everyday sessions | Stick holder + ash catcher |
| Cone | ~15–30 min | Moderate | Quick sessions, backflow effects | Flat heatproof dish |
| Coil (盘香) | ~1.5–4 hours | Moderate | Long, continuous scent | Coil stand / plate |
| Resin — electric heater | Session-based (hours) | Minimal | Low-smoke, controlled aroma | Electric heater + dish |
| Resin — charcoal | ~15–30 min per charcoal | Higher | Traditional, strong release | Charcoal in sand/ash |
| Powder (香粉) | Depends on method | Varies | Seal patterns (篆香), custom blends | Charcoal or pressed |

## The Author's Take

**Position:** Match the burn to the session first, then choose the scent — the format is the plan, the scent is the flavor.

**Reasoning:**
- People agonize over scent and ignore burn time, then wonder why a 25-minute stick dies halfway through a 45-minute sit.
- The best incense is the one whose burn ends when your session ends — a stick that outlives your meditation by twenty minutes is a mismatch.

*This is the author's editorial view and experience — not a verified fact.*
---

## How to Read the Matrix

1. **Session length first.** A short sit needs a stick or cone; hours of scent needs a coil; controlled, low-smoke appreciation needs resin on an electric heater.
2. **Smoke second.** If smoke matters (small room, sensitivity), choose electric-heated resin or a thin stick. See [ventilation & indoor air](/incense-ventilation-indoor-air/).
3. **Format ≠ scent.** The same sandalwood can be a stick, coil, or resin — the format changes the burn, not the aroma.

---

## The Two Variables That Matter

- **Burn time** — how long the scent lasts in one session.
- **Smoke output** — how much particulate you are adding to the room.

Everything else (holder, cleanup, convenience) follows from these two. Most "which format" confusion is really "which burn time and smoke level do I want."

## FAQ

### Q: How long does an incense stick burn?
A: Roughly 20–60 minutes, depending on thickness and material. Thin sticks are shorter; thick or coreless sticks can be longer.

### Q: What burns the longest?
A: A coil (盘香) — often 1.5 to 4 hours. Resin on an electric heater is session-based and can run for hours with low smoke.

### Q: Which format produces the least smoke?
A: Resin on an electric heater — it warms rather than burns, so it produces the least smoke.

### Q: Is a cone or a stick better?
A: For a quick, self-contained session, a cone (15–30 min); for everyday use, a stick (20–60 min). It depends on session length.
