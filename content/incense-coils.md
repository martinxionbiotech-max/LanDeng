# Incense Coils: The Hours-Long Burn, Explained

**Direct answer:** An incense coil (盘香, *pánxiāng*) is incense pressed into a spiral that **burns for hours** — often several — making it the format for long, steady room scenting and for temple and overnight-style use. The spiral shape is the whole trick: it packs a long burn into a compact footprint.

**Key facts:** A coil is essentially a long stick coiled into a flat spiral. Burn time scales with diameter and thickness, commonly ranging from a couple of hours to many. It is hung or set on a heatproof surface.

---

## Key Takeaways

- A coil is a **spiral** that packs a long burn into a small footprint.
- It burns for **hours** — the longest of the common formats.
- It is the classic format for **temples, long ceremonies, and all-day room scent**.
- It is hung (on a stand) or set on a **heatproof** surface, and needs ventilation.

---

## Why the Spiral

The coil's shape is a packaging solution: instead of a meter-long stick, the same length of incense is wound into a flat spiral. Light the outer tip and it smolders inward, giving a **continuous, even burn** for hours. Chinese 盘香 and the widely used mosquito-coil form are the familiar examples.

Burn time varies with the coil's **diameter and thickness** — a small coil might last a couple of hours; a large one can burn most of a day. Check the product's stated time rather than assuming.

---

## How to Burn a Coil

1. **Suspend it** on a coil stand (a wire or peg through the center), or set it on a heatproof plate with a layer of ash or sand beneath.
2. Light the **outer tip** so it smolders inward.
3. Keep it clear of drafts and flammable material, and ventilate the room.

Because a coil burns for hours, it rewards a little more attention to placement and air flow. See [incense holder & burner guide](/incense-holder-burner-guide/) and [incense safety](/safety/).

---

## Coils vs Sticks

| | Coil | Stick |
|---|---|---|
| Burn time | Hours | ~20–60 min |
| Footprint | Compact spiral | A straight rod |
| Best for | All-day scent, temples | A timed session |

If you want a **short, timed** scent, a stick is easier; if you want **hours of steady scent** without relighting, a coil is the format.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Assuming all coils burn the same time.** Diameter and thickness change it a lot — check the label.
2. **Burning a coil without a stand or heatproof base.** It smolders for hours; the surface matters.
3. **Forgetting ventilation** on a multi-hour burn.

## FAQ

### Q: How long does an incense coil burn?
Typically hours — a small coil a couple of hours, a large one most of a day. Check the product's stated time. See [which incense format](/which-incense-format/).

### Q: How do I burn an incense coil?
Hang it on a coil stand or set it on a heatproof plate over ash/sand, light the outer tip, and let it smolder inward. See [incense holder & burner guide](/incense-holder-burner-guide/).

### Q: Are coils the same as mosquito coils?
The form is the same spiral, but incense coils are made of aromatic material for scent; mosquito coils are a different, insecticide product. Do not confuse the two.

### Q: Is a coil better than a stick for scenting a room?
For long, steady scent (hours), yes — a coil burns continuously; a stick is ~20–60 min. See [incense sticks](/incense-sticks/).
