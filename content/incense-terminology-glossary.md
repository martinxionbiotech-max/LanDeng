# Incense Terminology Glossary: Stick, Coil, Cone, Resin, and Powder

**Direct answer:** Incense comes in several **forms** — stick (线香), coil (盘香), cone, resin (香脂/香片), and powder (香粉) — plus prepared styles like backflow cones and coreless sticks. The form determines how it burns, how long it lasts, and what holder it needs.

**Key facts:** This glossary covers **form** terminology. For **ingredient** terms (sandalwood, frankincense, makko), see the [ingredients glossary](/incense-ingredients-glossary/).

---

## Forms

| Term | Chinese | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Stick | 线香 | The everyday stick — bamboo-core (common) or coreless (Japanese-style) |
| Coil | 盘香 | A spiral that burns for a long time (hours), laid flat on a stand |
| Cone | 塔香 | A self-supporting cone that burns down completely |
| Resin | 香脂 / 香片 | Dried gum-resin or wood chips, burned on charcoal or an electric heater |
| Powder | 香粉 | Loose ground material, burned on charcoal or pressed into seal patterns |
| Backflow cone | 倒流香 | A hollow cone that channels smoke downward like a waterfall |

| Term | Chinese | Meaning |

| 香道 | xiāngdào | "The Way of Incense" — the practice of preparing and appreciating incense |

| 篆香 | zhuàn xiāng | "Seal incense" — powder pressed into a pattern that burns along a timed path |
| 四般闲事 | sì bān xián shì | The "Four Leisure Arts": incense, tea, hanging scrolls, flower arranging |

## How Form Affects Use

- **Stick and cone** — self-contained, convenient, session-length (roughly 20–60 min).
- **Coil** — long, continuous burn (hours); suits a room you want scented for a while.
- **Resin and powder** — the classical materials; need charcoal or an electric heater, and reward a slower, more deliberate approach (especially 隔火焚香).

Choose the form to match the session, not just the scent. See [which incense format](/which-incense-format/) for the full decision guide.

## FAQ

### Q: What are the main forms of incense?
A: Stick (线香), coil (盘香), cone (塔香), resin (香脂/香片), and powder (香粉) — plus prepared styles like backflow cones.

### Q: What is 隔火焚香?
A: "Indirect-fire incense" — warming material over buried charcoal (often with a plate), releasing aroma with little smoke. It is the ancestor of Japanese kōdō.

### Q: What is the difference between a stick and a coil?
A: A stick burns for roughly 20–60 minutes; a coil burns for hours. The choice is about session length.

### Q: What is a backflow cone?
A: A hollow cone that channels smoke downward, creating a "waterfall" effect — a visual, decorative style of cone.

## People Also Ask / 长尾问答

### Q: What is 香道 (xiāngdào)?
"The Way of Incense" — the broader Chinese and Japanese practice of preparing and appreciating incense. In Japan it was formalized into kōdō; the refined technique behind both is Song-dynasty 隔火焚香. See [Japanese kōdō](/japanese-incense-kodo/).

### Q: What is the difference between 合香 and a single-material incense?
合香 (hé xiāng) is a blended incense composed on a formula principle (君臣佐使), whereas a single-material incense is one wood or resin. Traditional Chinese incense is usually 合香. See [the blending system](/hexiang-blending-system/).

### Q: What is a coreless stick?
A stick pressed from the fragrant material itself with no bamboo core — thinner, shorter-burning, and generally lower-smoke than a bamboo-core stick. Common in Japanese and Tibetan traditions. See [incense sticks](/incense-sticks/).
