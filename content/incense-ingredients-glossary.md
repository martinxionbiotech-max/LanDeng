# Incense Ingredients Glossary: Wood, Resin, Flower, and Spice

**Direct answer:** Incense ingredients fall into a few families — **woods** (sandalwood, agarwood), **resins** (frankincense, myrrh, benzoin), **flowers** (jasmine, rose, osmanthus, lotus), **spices** (clove, cassia), and **binders** (makko). Knowing the family tells you most of what you need to know about how a scent will behave.

**Key facts:** This glossary is a quick reference, not an exhaustive list. Each entry names the material, its source, and its general character. The full guides (linked) carry the depth.

---

## Woods

| Ingredient | Source | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Sandalwood** (檀香) | *Santalum* heartwood | Creamy, sweet, soft-woody |
| **Agarwood** (沉香) | Resin-impregnated *Aquilaria* wood | Deep, resinous, sweet |

## Resins

| Ingredient | Source | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Frankincense** (乳香) | *Boswellia* gum-resin | Fresh, citrus, balsamic |
| **Myrrh** (没药) | *Commiphora* gum-resin | Bitter, balsamic, earthy |
| **Benzoin** (安息香) | *Styrax* resin | Sweet, vanilla-like, warm |

## Flowers

| Ingredient | Source | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Jasmine** (茉莉) | *Jasminum* flowers | Rich, sweet, heady floral |
| **Rose** (玫瑰) | *Rosa* petals | Classic, sweet floral |
| **Osmanthus** (桂花) | *Osmanthus fragrans* | Sweet, fruity-apricot floral |
| **Lotus** (莲花) | *Nelumbo* flower | Fresh, subtle, aquatic-floral |

## Spices & Others

| Ingredient | Source | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Clove** (丁香) | *Syzygium aromaticum* buds | Warm, spicy, sweet |
| **Cassia** (桂皮) | *Cinnamomum cassia* bark | Warm, sweet-spicy |
| **Aged mandarin peel** (陈皮) | *Citrus reticulata* peel | Warm, sweet, earthy citrus |
| **Mugwort** (艾草) | *Artemisia* herb | Herbal, green, slightly bitter |

## Binders

| Ingredient | Source | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Makko** (抹香) | *Machilus thunbergii* bark | Near-odorless combustible binder |

---

## How to Read This Glossary

- **Woods and resins** are the classical backbone of Chinese incense — they burn (or heat) slowly and carry deep, persistent scent.
- **Flowers** are usually fragrance accords in modern incense rather than the raw flower (see the [lotus](/lotus-incense/) and [lavender](/lavender-incense/) guides).
- **Spices** add warmth and are usually blend components, not solo scents.
- **Binders** are functional — they hold the form and burn, without adding their own scent.

## The Author's Take

In my view, the family framework — woods, resins, flowers, spices, binders — is the fastest way to become literate about ingredients, because the family predicts the behavior. My experience is that the two distinctions worth memorizing are wood versus resin (slow-deep versus fresh-balsamic) and flower-as-material versus flower-as-accord. Once you have those, the rest of the glossary falls into place.

## FAQ

### Q: What are the main families of incense ingredients?
A: Woods (sandalwood, agarwood), resins (frankincense, myrrh, benzoin), flowers (jasmine, rose, osmanthus), spices (clove, cassia), and binders (makko).

### Q: Are flowers real in incense?
A: Often not — many floral incense scents are fragrance accords, because the raw flower does not burn well. See the [lotus guide](/lotus-incense/).

### Q: What is the difference between wood and resin incense?
A: Woods (sandalwood, agarwood) are heartwood; resins (frankincense, myrrh) are dried tree gum. Woods give deep, persistent scent; resins give fresh or balsamic brightness.

### Q: What is a binder in incense?
A: A functional ingredient — usually a combustible wood powder like makko — that holds the form together and burns, without adding its own scent.

## People Also Ask / 长尾问答

### Q: What is the difference between benzoin and frankincense?
Both are resins but different plants: benzoin (*Styrax*) is sweet and vanilla-balsamic; frankincense (*Boswellia*) is fresh, citrus, and earthy. Benzoin is more of a sweet base/fixative, frankincense a bright ritual resin.

### Q: What is makko and why does it matter?
Makko (抹香) is the powdered bark of *Machilus thunbergii*, the traditional combustible binder. It holds incense together and burns cleanly with little odor — a sign of a material-based stick. See [makko & natural binders](/makko-natural-binders/).

### Q: Are clove and cassia interchangeable in incense?
They are both warm spices but distinct: clove is the dried flower bud (*Syzygium aromaticum*), cassia is bark (*Cinnamomum cassia*). Clove is sharper and more pungent; cassia is warmer and sweeter. They are used differently in blends.
